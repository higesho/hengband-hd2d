=== What is "Gensoband"? ===

Keine: "Gensoband is one of the many variants of the roguelike game.

You know the word "roguelike", I trust?
In 1980, at the dawn of computer games, a dungeon-crawling RPG called "Rogue"
was written. The dungeon-crawling RPGs descended from it are called
roguelikes as a group. What exactly makes a roguelike is a matter of endless
argument, but broadly it means a game where you explore a randomly built
dungeon laid out on a grid, taking turns, killing monsters and gathering
treasure, and where the game simply ends when you fall.

A "variant" means a derived work.
The roguelike branched again and again, and a great many variants were made.
Gensoband stands at the end of this line:
"Rogue" -> "Moria" -> "Angband" -> "Zangband" -> "Hengband" -> "Gensoband".

Moria and Angband borrowed a great deal from Tolkien.
Younger players may know it better as "The Lord of the Rings" than as any
book, I suppose.

Zangband then borrowed a great deal more, on an even larger scale, from the
Cthulhu Mythos and from the Amber novels.

Hengband in turn stuffed in mountains of material from Japanese animation and
much else besides. The result is a thoroughly chaotic game -- and yet the
system that makes it worth playing has been handed down intact.

Gensoband is that same Hengband, altered here and there, with the elements of
the Touhou Project added on top.

The early roguelikes were plain games indeed.
The graphics were letters and nothing more, there was no sound at all, and the
world of the game was built by the player's imagination alone.
But then, a role-playing game was always meant to be an adventure inside the
imagination. The progress of computer games has been remarkable, but there is
something to be said for touching the old things once in a while."

=== Hengband and its variants ===

Keine: "Hengband's program code is published free of charge.
Anyone can rewrite it and compile it and so make a Hengband of their own.
Such a thing is called a "private version" or a "modified version".

Gensoband itself was first published under the title "Hengband, private
version, with assorted Touhou material added". That being rather a mouthful,
people took to calling it "the Touhou version". Over the years that followed,
the differences from Hengband proper piled up and began to cause confusion,
so the title was changed -- belatedly.

Perhaps some of you now feel like making a private version or a variant of
your own. If so, I strongly recommend building on Hengband's program code
rather than on Gensoband's.

Hengband's code is at present being refactored on a large scale by a good
many developers, and it must be far easier and far safer to work with than
Gensoband's code -- which is nearly ten-year-old code that an amateur has
been pulling about at will. See Hengband's own site and help files for the
details."


***** <First>
=== The goal of the game ===

Keine: "Now then. The story moves to Gensokyo.
A suspicious cave has appeared west of the Human Village, and violent
creatures are pouring out of it. Your adventure is to go down into that cave
and resolve the incident.

At the outset you are frail enough that a stray dog could bite you and leave
you in tears. But by exploring the dungeon, gathering experience and putting
together your equipment, you will in time be able to stand against any power
however great."


***** <Gameover>
=== Game over ===

Keine: "I said a moment ago that in a roguelike the game ends when you fall.
That was neither exaggeration nor figure of speech.
The instant your hit points go below zero, every coin you have earned, every
point of experience, and every treasure piled up in your home is gone.
The game starts again from the beginning.

Two things remain, however.
The monster lore you have recorded across your adventures -- and the
experience that accumulates in you, the player.
At first you will fall over at the least little thing. But each time you fall
you will learn a better way to behave, and before long there will be scarcely
anything that can kill you except by sheer bad luck.

...Well, Mokou alone does not end when she falls, but she is the exception.
Set the difficulty to EASY and others may come back as well."
