=== Guide for newcomers ===

Keine: "What!? You have never played Hengband and this is your first Gensoband?
Very well, calm yourself. Your teacher will take you through it step by step.

This file is /lib/help/instruction.txt.
It may be easier to play if you open it again in a text editor and set it
beside the game window.

The game window can be enlarged by dragging with the mouse.
If you want to see more of the map, make it a little bigger.
It is also worth opening one or two sub-windows from the menu at the top of
the screen, and setting them to show the monster list and the message log.

For now, start as a Warrior.
An early Warrior can fight with the movement keys almost alone, so learn the
various basic operations with one first.

For a race, the Oni, the Hakurou-Tengu, the Tennin, the Death, the Daiyoukai
and the Haniwa are easy to understand and strong.
If you want a unique class, Yuuka, Kasen, Yorihime, Mayumi, Saki and Momoyo
are approachable even for a newcomer.

Turning on the "fix bonus points" option at character creation gives you 20
points. Spend them on strength, dexterity and constitution so that you fight
well hand to hand.
If you prefer to hunt for good bonus rolls as in the Wizardry games, leave the
points unfixed and reroll as often as you like.

When the game begins, you see something like a town drawn in colourful letters
and symbols, do you not? That is the game screen. Computer games older than
the Famicom looked like this.
This is the Human Village of Gensokyo. The white "@" near the entrance is you.

If a screen of nothing but letters seems dull, you can use 8x8 dot tile
graphics instead. See the links on the distribution site for details.

Everything in this game is done, in principle, by typing commands on the
keyboard. There are about a hundred commands, but let me set down the bare
minimum you need to reach about floor 20.
Look for the others in the window that the Enter key opens, and learn them
slowly, starting with the ones you use most.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Enter key       Opens a command window from which you choose what to do.

  Direction keys (number pad)  Moves the @. If an enemy stands where you move,
                  you attack it.
  Shift + direction  Moves the @ in one go until something blocks it.
                  It will not move unless Num Lock is on.
  Ctrl + direction  Attacks in the direction pressed without moving.

  5 Do nothing where you stand.
    Use it to wait for a nearby monster to come to you, or to rest a moment.
  s Search around you. It finds secret doors, floor traps and traps on chests
    on the floor. With a low searching skill you often have to search several
    times.
  < Go up a staircase. On the surface it switches to the wilderness map.
  > Go down a staircase. On the wilderness map it switches to the ordinary
    map. Also used when you want to step on a trap deliberately.

  g (get)       Pick up the item at your feet.
  d (drop)      Drop an item on the ground.
  e (equip)     Show the list of your equipment.
  i (inventory) Show the list of what you carry.
  w (wield)     Wear or wield an item.
  t (take off)  Take off a piece of equipment.

  E (eat)   Eat food. When you run out, the shop marked "1" in the Human
            Village sells it.
  q (quaff) Drink a potion. Do not drink unidentified potions carelessly.
  r (read)  Read a scroll. You cannot read while confused or blind, or with
            your light gone out.
  F (fuel)  Refill your lantern with oil.
            Running out of oil in the dungeon leaves you unable to see, which
            is extremely dangerous.
  a, z, u (aim, zap, use staff)  Use a wand, a rod and a staff respectively.
            They can fail. Using unidentified ones carelessly is not advised.

  o (open)   Open a door or a chest. If it is locked you try to pick it
             automatically.
  c (close)  Close a door.
  D (disarm) Disarm a chest trap or a floor trap. With a low disarming skill
             you can fail and set it off.

  J (job skill)  Use a class ability. Some classes have none.
  U (use special skill)  Use a racial or mutation power.
            Tewi and Momizi, for instance, can listen and learn where the
            monsters around them are, which is very useful.

  l (look)  Examine your surroundings.
            After the command, press the "*" key or a direction key and the
            cursor moves to the monsters and so on around you.
            With the cursor on a monster, press the "r" key and you see what
            you know about it.
  C (character)  Examine the player's own details.
            After the command, each press of h switches the information shown.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

That is the bare minimum of commands.

  Once the game has begun, set your options first.
Enter the option screen with the "=" command and type "H" then "5".
That warns you when your HP falls by half.
Then press ESC to go back one step, type "D" then "1" or "2", and the paths of
the spells the enemies fire are drawn on the screen.

  Next, put together your equipment with the money you have.
At the shop marked "1" on the Human Village map, buy a Brass Lantern and
flasks of oil;
at the shop marked "2", body armour, a hat, boots and a cloak (you will not
need a shield);
at the shop marked "5", a few Scrolls of Teleportation, and if they have them
a few Scrolls of Confuse Monster, and a Scroll of Word of Recall if you can
afford one;
at the shop marked "6", a Ring of Warning if they have one.
Spend what is left at the shop marked "4" on as many Potions of Cure Serious
Wounds as you can get.
If what you want is sold out, come back after a while and the stock will have
been renewed. Staying at an inn to pass the time works too.

  Once you have finished shopping, go to a dungeon.
Press the "<" key to show the wilderness map, and a little below and to the
right of the @ is Myouren Temple.
While you walk the wilderness map, monsters may ambush you and send you back
to an ordinary map, but a character built as above should not lose easily.
When your HP falls, drink a potion to heal or flee with a Scroll of
Teleportation.
Reach the edge of a map and you pass to the next one; once you change maps,
every enemy that was there is gone.

  When you reach Myouren Temple, press the ">" key to return to the ordinary
map. Can you see a red > mark near the top right of the Myouren Temple map?
That is the entrance to the Great Mausoleum, a beginner's dungeon where strong
enemies are rare. Go in and learn the basics of fighting and moving for a
while.
Nearly every symbol in the dungeon is terrain or an item, and nearly every
letter is a monster.
Use the 'l' command and move the cursor with the direction keys and you can
find out what a thing is.

If you find a horde of white worm masses (white w) it is your chance to gather
experience. Stand in the doorway of the room and keep swinging with
Ctrl + direction, and the multiplying worms will fall of their own accord.
That should take you to about level 6.

  You will find a great many items whose effect is unclear -- "a Red Potion",
"a Scroll titled 'xxxxx yyy'" and so on.
And weapons and armour you put on may turn out to be cursed.
"Identifying" these tells you their basic effects.
Identification is done at the Hieda Manor in the Human Village (the entrance
marked + in the upper middle of the Human Village map) by choosing "identify
an item". It costs $25.
You may end up with an item under a slightly unusual name, such as "Leather
Boots of the Fairy". That is a fine item. Use it carefully.
Fine items taken to the Scarlet Devil Mansion or Kourindou for closer study
sometimes turn out to have further special effects.

  Now then, once you are about level 6, take on a quest.
Choose "q) Quest" at the Hieda Manor in the Human Village and you are asked to
deal with some ruffians.
Go to the upper left of the Human Village and a new staircase has appeared;
go in and you fight the band of thieves.
As you are now, you will not lose.
To make quite sure, read a Scroll of Confuse Monster.
After that, your next attack can confuse the enemy once.
That kills most early enemies easily.

Kill every thief and "You have completed the quest!" appears.
Report back at the Hieda Manor and the quest is complete, and from then on you
can use that building as a base of operations and leave your items there.
(The entrance hall is a place to display items. Note that an item put there
can never be used again.)

That is how the first stretch goes.
After that you go deeper into the Great Mausoleum, or make the journey to
distant Angband or Genbu Ravine, or take on other quests -- gaining levels,
building up your equipment and learning how to use the various items.
To go beyond floor 20 you will want free action, resistance to fire and see
invisible. For that, see the other help files.

Read a Scroll of Word of Recall deep in the dungeon and wait a while and you
return to the surface. And choosing "return to the dungeon" at the Divine
Spirit Mausoleum branch office in the Human Village sends you to the deepest
floor you have explored.

If it is too hard, try the "Munchkin" nature or the EASY difficulty.
Good luck to you."
