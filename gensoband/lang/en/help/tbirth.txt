=== Creating a character ===

Keine: "The adventure is about to begin, but something must come first.
What race are you, what class, and what powers do you have?
How hard is this adventure to be? Declare all of that for me."

***** <Difficulty>
=== The difficulty setting ===

Keine: "First, settle the difficulty of your adventure.
There are five to choose from: EASY, NORMAL, HARD, LUNATIC and EXTRA.
Let me go into them a little.

  "EASY" is a simple practice mode.
You are unnaturally strong, the enemies are weak, and the adventure is easy.
What is more, if you fall you may continue as often as you please.
A newcomer who knows nothing at all might well choose this difficulty.
Note that this mode is won by killing Yukari Yakumo on floor 50. There is
nothing beyond that. Your score falls pitifully low, too. That is the sort of
difficulty it is.

  "NORMAL" is the ordinary mode -- though it is a good deal easier than
Hengband. The enemies have only about 75% of Hengband's hit points.
Enemies do not cast spells or breathe twice in a row without letting you act
in between. And they pay no attention at all to which elements you are weak
against. Also, powerful enemies rarely appear in the dungeon until you have
killed Yukari Yakumo on floor 50.
This mode counts as won when you kill Yukari Yakumo on floor 50, but you may
go on beyond that if you wish.
Proceed carefully and you should win it fairly easily.

  "HARD" is set at roughly the same level as Hengband.
Enemies learn which elements work on you and stop using the ones that do not.
Now and then they may move suddenly fast and land a fierce run of attacks.
Enemies strong enough to kill you in a moment will turn up as well.
The score is twice that of NORMAL.

  "LUNATIC" is close to what Hengband calls "nightmare mode".
Every enemy gets +5 speed and has its HP doubled, up to a ceiling of 30000.
They are awake from the start and know your weak elements from the start.
That alone is hard enough, but on top of it powerful enemies from the deep
levels sometimes prowl the very shallowest floors of the dungeon.
If you meet one, there is nothing left but to say your prayers.

Furthermore, calamity falls upon you at midnight every day.
Sleeping at an inn brings nightmares and drives you mad.
Sustain sometimes fails, and a lowered stat is sometimes lowered forever.
Mutations sometimes cannot be cured.
Enemies that arrive by summoning or ambush catch you unawares more easily.
Digging ore sometimes brings the roof down on you.
Keeping pets costs twice as much.

Well -- once you are used to the game and start to miss being beaten
unreasonably, give it a try. The score is eight times that of NORMAL.

  "EXTRA" is a highly unusual mode with a great many rules of its own.
Explaining them in full would take too long, so they have a chapter to
themselves; in a word, how you handle supplies and equipment matters enormously.
It is the sort of difficulty to attempt after you have won a few times on
HARD. The score is four times that of NORMAL."


***** <Status_main>
=== The main stats ===

Keine: "Next you settle your race and class, but before that let me explain
the stats. A stat is, quite literally, one of your abilities -- your strength.

First the six main stats:
strength, intelligence, wisdom, dexterity, constitution and charisma.
How they are understood and what they do differ a little from Hengband.

- "Strength" is bodily power.
It affects your melee damage, how heavy the equipment you can carry is, how
many blows you get with a weapon, and how much you can carry.
It matters to a warrior, of course, but a caster cannot neglect it either --
equipment that is too heavy raises the MP a spell costs.

- "Intelligence" is quickness of mind and memory.
It affects your spell success rate, how many spells you can learn, and your
success rate with magic devices.
It matters a great deal to a caster and perhaps very little to a warrior.
Unlike Hengband, Gensoband decides spell success by intelligence for the
faith-based classes such as the Shrine Maiden as well.

- "Wisdom" is force of mind, or faith -- strength of heart, if you like.
It affects the size of your MP pool and your magic defence.
A pure warrior scarcely needs it, but every other class does.
For a pure caster it goes straight to fighting strength.

- "Dexterity" is deftness of hand and quickness of movement.
It affects how many melee blows you get and your ability to disarm traps.
It matters to a warrior and hardly at all to a caster.
Though if it is very low, a thief may steal something precious and leave you
in tears.

- "Constitution" is bodily toughness.
It affects the size of your HP pool and how quickly you recover from bad
status effects. It matters to a warrior and to a caster alike.
Without a decent pool of HP the middle depths are far too dangerous to enter.

- "Charisma" is beauty, or popularity.
It chiefly affects your dealings in the shops. With low charisma you will
struggle for money. Also, many of the unique-class abilities described later
grow stronger with high charisma.
"Danmaku is a contest of beauty", after all.

These stats sometimes rise when you gain a level, and they can be raised by
drinking magical potions.

The maximum for a bare main stat is 30.
With the adjustments from race, class, nature and equipment the maximum is 40,
and a temporary boost by special means can reach 50.

By the way: in Hengband a main stat above 18 was shown as "18/40" and so on,
reaching at most "18/***" -- it never got to 19.
That was a venerable notation founded on D&D's idea that "19 is the realm of
the gods", but Gensoband does not use it.
Well, in Gensokyo gods are rather thick on the ground..."


***** <Status_other>
=== The other parameters ===

Keine: "Now let me explain the parameters other than the main stats.
If you are a newcomer who knows nothing, skip this.
Come back when you can descend to about floor 30.

- "Stealth" is the ability not to wake sleeping enemies.
With low stealth, nearby enemies wake one after another and come at you, and
exploring the dungeon becomes a good deal harder.
Stealth is decided almost entirely by your race's base value, your class's
base value and your equipment.
Hardly any class raises stealth as it gains levels.

- "Speed" is the ability to move quickly. +10 is twice as fast, +20 three
times. It affects not only movement but everything you do. It matters greatly
when you fight a strong enemy.
Equipment that raises speed appears mostly from the middle depths on; early
on you must buy speed temporarily with Potions of Speed and the like.
To go deep you want +20 or better without help.
Beyond about +30, raising it further changes rather little.

- "Infravision" is the ability to sense warm-blooded creatures in the dark.
Each +1 lets you see an enemy ten feet -- one grid -- further off.
It is useful enough early on, but from the middle depths the enemies without
body heat, the demons and undead, grow more common.
By then you will need some other means of detection, such as ESP.

- "Digging" is the ability to dig through walls. The higher it is, the faster
you dig. To dig out the treasure buried in walls, wield a pick or the like to
raise your digging. From the middle depths on it will rarely be of use.

- "Searching" is the ability to find what is hidden.
High searching finds traps and secret doors easily and senses the feeling of
a floor quickly.
It also finds the materials each dungeon yields more readily.
The higher the better, but doing without is no great hardship.
It may matter, though, if you attempt the "EXTRA" difficulty.

- "Magic device" is the ability to use magical devices.
It affects your success rate with staves, wands, rods and the activation of
equipment. Early on you can manage without, but late in the game it matters a
great deal. In the deep floors, however strong you are, more enemies appear
than you can possibly handle, and powerful devices such as a Staff of
*Destruction* or a Staff of Healing become essential.
A pure warrior has a low magic device skill and must make it up from equipment
somehow.

- "Magic defence" is your defence against magic.
That does not mean a high value blocks every spell; it is your resistance to
"curses" and "mind attacks".
Resist successfully and you take no damage.
Powerful attacks grow more common from the middle depths, so the higher the
better. Magic defence is decided chiefly by race, class, level and wisdom.
Equipment that raises magic defence can raise it further.
Being "blessed" raises it a little.
Anti-magic equipment guarantees anyone a certain magic defence, but you can no
longer cast spells yourself.

- "Disarming" is the ability to disarm traps and pick locked doors.
It moves chiefly with race, class, level and dexterity. Some equipment raises
it. From the middle depths it is little used -- opening things with a magic
device is faster and safer.

- "Melee" is your ability to attack with fists or weapons.
It affects your chance to hit, and so it goes straight to your fighting
strength. It is decided almost entirely by class, level and equipment, though
early on race is not to be dismissed either.
The oni and the vampire are high; the magician and the kobito are low.

- "Shooting" is your ability to attack by shooting or throwing.
This too affects your chance to hit, and is decided almost entirely by class
and level.

- "HP" hardly needs saying. Go below zero and the game is over.
It is decided by level and constitution, and by race and class.
At the same class, level and constitution, a kobito and an oni have very
different HP.

- "MP" hardly needs saying either. Without enough of it you cannot cast.
MP is decided mostly by level, class and wisdom, and varies by a few per cent
with the wisdom adjustments of your race and class.
Spells always spend MP, but some class abilities spend HP rather than MP.
Racial powers normally spend MP, and fall back on HP when MP runs short.
A warrior has a little MP, but will scarcely use it except when wielding a
weapon of Force.
Spells, abilities and racial powers have a chapter of their own later.

- "Experience" will be familiar too. You need it to gain levels.
What the game calls your "life force" is the same number. Quite how to read
that is debatable; think of it as you like.
It is temporarily reduced by attacks of the hell element and by undead.
Drink a Potion of Restore Life Levels and it comes back.

- "AC" is short for armour class. The higher it is, the better you stand up to
an enemy's ordinary attacks. Above 150 the extra does rather less.

That is about all for the stats.
You can check these numbers with the 'C' command."


***** <Race>
=== Races ===

Keine: "Gensokyo is home to beings of a truly wide variety.
Choose here what race you are.
Your race greatly changes your abilities, your aptitude for the classes, your
resistances to enemy attacks -- everything.
While you are new to adventuring, a strong race that grows slowly, such as the
Oni, the Daiyoukai or the Tennin, is the safe choice.
A sharp-eared race such as the Warbeast or the Gyokuto is good too, since you
can tell where the enemies are.
Races with low abilities such as the Human, the Fairy and the Kobito, and
races with too much character such as the Vampire and the Youkai-Doll, are
better left until you are used to the game.
That said, choosing as the heart moves you without thinking too hard may well
be the greatest pleasure of all.

The races are described in detail in a chapter of their own."

***** <Class>
=== Classes ===

Keine: "The choice of class matters.
Your class makes your whole strategy in the dungeon a different thing.

With a warrior class the basics are to gather good weapons and to train your
strength, dexterity and constitution.
Early on you can kill most enemies simply by hitting them, but from the middle
depths, when enemies begin attacking in many different ways, you will often
struggle.

With a caster class you should raise intelligence and wisdom so you can cast
often. And you must judge your own strength against the enemy's and see
exactly when you can win.
A caster can answer the tangled situations of the middle depths in many ways,
but it takes practice.

There are also classes that use both weapons and magic tolerably well, and
classes that fight in some special way that is neither.
While you are new, a warrior class such as the Warrior or the Archer is a good
choice.

The classes are described in detail in a chapter of their own.

The broad features of a race or a class are shown as you choose it.
During the game you can see them at any time by choosing "information about
yourself" from the '~' command."

***** <UniqueClass>
=== What is a unique class? ===

Keine: "There are also special classes peculiar to Gensoband.
They are the girls whose names became known throughout Gensokyo through the
incidents that have happened again and again.
If you are one of them, say so.
A class of her own is set for you, and race, sex and name are fixed.
Such a class has its own parameters, magic realms, abilities and various other
traits, just as an ordinary class does.
Let us call these the unique classes, for convenience.
There are more than a hundred of them at present, and no doubt they will go on
increasing with every new work in the Touhou Project."

***** <Outsider>
=== What is an Outsider? ===

Keine: "What? You have never so much as heard of Gensokyo and you find
yourself here? I see -- so you are an Outsider, come from the world beyond.

Take my advice. If you want to get home alive, go to the Hakurei Shrine, far
to the southeast of the Human Village.
There is a shrine maiden there called Reimu Hakurei, and if you ask her she
will send you back outside.
And whatever you do, do not get it into your head to meddle with an incident."


***** <Personality>
=== Natures ===

Keine: "You may be a person of reason, or you may be quick to pick a fight.
Such a nature affects your stats slightly. Choose as you please.
Choosing the "Munchkin" nature strengthens everything about you absurdly, but
think of it as a practice mode.

Some unique classes can also choose a special nature of their own, and doing so
changes their abilities and their techniques utterly from the ordinary ones.

There are a few other clearly frivolous natures, which I do not recommend to
anyone unfamiliar with the game."


***** <Realm>
=== Choosing magic realms ===

Keine: "Some classes can learn spells.
By a spell I mean here the sort you obtain in a spellbook, learn, and then
cast with the 'm' command by spending MP while carrying the book.
A spell cannot be learned before a certain level, and fails easily if you are
not clever. There are other restrictions besides, and in return spells are
powerful and varied.
While you are new to adventuring a class that cannot cast is easier to handle,
but I hope you will master spells in time.

Spells are divided among three great realms -- Magic, Sorcery and Witchcraft --
and twelve realms.
Magic holds the [Element], [Foreseeing], [Enchant] and [Summon] realms;
Sorcery holds the [Mystic], [Life], [Punish] and [Nature] realms;
Witchcraft holds the [Transform], [Darkness], [Necromancy] and [Chaos] realms.
Each realm has four spellbooks from the first volume to the last, and each
book holds eight spells.

Anyone who has played Hengband will see at a glance that several of the realms
have been changed a great deal here.
Chaos is much the same as Hengband's Chaos realm.
Life and Nature are more than half the same.
Summon resembles Trump, Enchant resembles Craft, and Punish resembles
Hengband's Crusade.
The rest have mostly been rearranged heavily or built anew.

Of these twelve realms you may, as a rule, learn only the one you choose now.
But several classes -- the Mage, the Teacher, the Shrine Maiden -- can use two
realms. The second realm is learned a little more slowly, but in exchange it
can be changed at any time.
Change it once, however, and every spell of the old realm is forgotten.
The number of spells you may learn is limited, so changing realms again and
again is not to be recommended.
And if the second realm's great realm differs from the first's, a penalty
makes it harder to use.

Aptitude for a realm also depends on your class.
Suppose an Explorer and a Mage have both chosen the Element realm.
Even with the same intelligence, the Explorer needs a higher level than the
Mage to learn a spell. Casting costs more MP and fails more often.
Some of the higher spells the Explorer may not even be able to learn.

Race gives an aptitude too, though less than class.
Many youkai, for instance, are poor at Sorcery and good at Witchcraft. For the
Sennin and the Tennin it is the other way round.
The Magician is superb at Magic and reasonably capable in the rest.

Note that from version 1.1.08 a thirteenth realm called "Arcane" was added on
top of these. It is almost entirely unlike the Arcane realm of Hengband
proper, and the racial aptitude for it is the same for every race.

That is the outline.
Spells are described in detail in a chapter of their own."


***** <BirthOptions>
=== The birth options ===

Keine: "Quite apart from the game's difficulty, you may choose a number of
options that adjust the difficulty or place restrictions on your game.
Adventuring with a hard option turned on raises your score.
Press '?' at any option's setting and you can read a full description of it."


***** <BonusPoint>
=== Spending your bonus points ===

Keine: "Once race, class and nature are settled, your base main stats are
fixed. You may then add bonus points on top of your strength, intelligence,
wisdom, dexterity, constitution and charisma.

How many bonus points you get is purely a matter of luck.
If you are not satisfied you may reroll with the r key after allotting them.
Compared with a certain famous dungeon game the bonus is rather less inclined
to spike. Twenty-five is a fine result.
If you would rather not bother, a birth option fixes the bonus points at 20.
That raises your score a little."


***** <BackgroundEditor>
=== The history editor ===

Keine: "Where were you born, and what sort of life did you lead?
And with what in mind do you throw yourself into the dungeon?
If you wish to declare it, write it down here. Put as much of yourself into it
as you like.
Once written, it is not changed except in special cases.
If you never want it changed, I advise you not to put on that odd mask
inscribed "Ninja Slayer", should you find one."


***** <Name>
=== Your name and your class name ===

Keine: "Now then -- late in the day, but let me ask your name.
If you are a unique class your name is known already and you need not give it.
If your class is "Civilian" you may change the name of your class here, once
only.
If you started the game from "new", settle the name of your save file here too."


***** <QuickStart>
=== Quick start ===

Keine: "If your strength fails you and you fall, the game starts again from
the beginning, as I have said more than once.
But the details of this character creation are kept, and if you wish you can
begin a new adventure in exactly the state you began the last one."
