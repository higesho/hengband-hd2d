=== The map display ===

Keine: "Well then, the adventure begins at last.
You start at the entrance to the Human Village.
You can see something like a town drawn in colourful letters and symbols,
can you not? Let us begin with what those symbols mean.

The player (@)
The '@' in the middle is you yourself.
Press the direction keys or the number keys and you should move.

Ground (.)
White and green dots are ground and floor. You can walk on them.

Forest (#)
A green # is forest. Walking through it slows you down a little.

Wall (%)
On the game screen this appears as a solid orange block; in this text it is
written as %. White walls can be tunnelled through; orange walls can never be
broken.

Door (+)
In a town this marks the entrance to a building. Go in and you can use the
various facilities. In the dungeon, however, the same terrain is nothing more
than an obstacle that can be opened and closed.

Digits (1-9)
These are shop entrances. Go in and you can trade.
There are shops in the dungeon from time to time as well.

Letters (a-z, A-Z) and symbols
These various letters and symbols are monsters.
The f a little to the right of the player is one of them.
The one standing here is only a stray cat, but the dungeon will be swarming
with dangerous monsters.

To move, to attack, to do anything at all, you type a command on the
keyboard. The bare minimum of commands you need at the start is gathered in
the guide for newcomers. There are hardly any dangerous monsters in the
Human Village, so walk about for a while and try out the various commands.

Walking off the edge of the map takes you to the map next door, and the map
you left is reset when you do. Take care not to wander off to the next map
with something important still lying on the floor."


***** <Shopping>
=== The shops ===

Keine: "Next, the shops.
In a shop you can buy items and sell the ones you do not need.
The higher your charisma, the better the prices you are offered.
The numbers 1 to 9 in the Human Village are the shop entrances.
Now and then you will see an expensive item whose name differs a little from
the ordinary kind. Items I shall explain in a chapter of their own.

1: General Store
Here you can buy food, lanterns, flasks of oil to refill them, and picks for
digging through walls.

2: Armoury
Here you can buy clothing and armour.
If you cast spells, be warned that equipment which is too heavy, or ordinary
gloves on your hands, will get in the way of your magic.

3: Weaponsmith
Here you can buy weapons and ammunition.
A warrior class starts with a decent weapon already, but if yours looks
unreliable it is no bad thing to buy something suitable here.

4: Potion and Liquor Shop
Here you can buy potions and drink.
If you can spare the money, buy a few doses of cure while you are here.

5: Scroll Shop
Here you can buy scrolls.
Scrolls do all manner of things and no adventure can do without them.
Buy a Scroll of Teleportation first, for emergencies.
And when you set out for a distant dungeon, do not forget a Scroll of Word of
Recall.

6: Magic Shop
Here you can buy rings, amulets, staves and the like.
Staves and wands have a limited number of charges, but they are handy tools
that take up little room.

7: Black Market and the like
In one corner of a town stands a suspicious building known as the black
market or the thieves' market, and all sorts of goods with a history behind
them find their way there. Most are powerful items you will see in no other
shop -- but the prices they ask are beyond anything an honest shop would
dare. Once your funds allow it, it is worth looking in regularly.
Bargains seem to turn up rather more readily in some towns than in others.

8: Base of Operations
This building is yours to use freely. You can store several hundred kinds of
item you cannot carry.
There is no such building when your adventure begins, but if you settle the
troubles of the townsfolk, one or two towns may let you use a building as a
base of operations.

9: Bookshop
Keine: "Here you can buy spellbooks.
No very powerful book is sold here.
And the bookshop in the Human Village does not seem to stock books of
Sorcery."

10: Entrance Hall
Keine: "This becomes available together with your base of operations.
Several thousand items can be left here, but an item left here can never be
used again. When you find an item that is worthless yet too curious to throw
away, put it on show in the entrance hall."

Keine: "When you sell an item, the rule is to identify it first.
Sell an unidentified item and you will be beaten down cruelly on the price.

Also, a shop's stock is renewed several times a day.
If you want an item you cannot afford, run and raise the money before someone
else buys it."


***** <Buildings>
=== The other buildings ===

Keine: "The character '+' in a town marks the entrance to a building you may
enter. Each building lets you do something different. Let me name a few.

- Hieda Manor
Bring an unidentified item to the Hieda Manor in the Human Village and it
will be identified cheaply. For a rather steep fee you can also be told what
is known about a monster you name.

- Staying at an inn
Almost every town has an inn where you can take a meal or stay the night.
The inn has a bar as well, and there you can hear the rumours of the town.

- Quests
Someone has a favour to ask of you. More on this later.

- Blacksmith
Weapons can be enchanted, armour can be enchanted, and a weapon's properties
can be examined. For a fee, of course.

- Item research
Some powerful items do not reveal all their effects on an ordinary
identification. Take such items to Kourindou or to the library of the
Scarlet Devil Mansion and they will be studied in detail.
The fee for the trouble is somewhat high.

- Divine Spirit Mausoleum branch office
The saint Toyosatomimi keeps an office open in a number of towns.
She can recharge staves and other magical devices, move you to a town you
have already visited, or send you down to the deepest floor you have reached
in a dungeon. When you have her send you into a dungeon, do not forget the
means to come home again.

- Eientei
At Eientei almost anything can be cured: drained experience, lowered stats,
mutations. Getting there through the bamboo forest is rather a chore, mind.

- Hakurei Shrine
The shrine will restore lowered stats and remove curses from your equipment.
The fee is rather high, though. Could it not be brought down somehow?
In a corner of the grounds the kappa seem to be busy with their gambling.

- Guilds
Archers, engineers, apothecaries and others have organised themselves into
guilds, and if you belong to one of those classes you can receive services
reserved for members.

- Kirisame Magic Shop
Marisa keeps a shop in the Forest of Magic.
Gather materials and bring them to her and you build up points that can be
exchanged for prizes.
But until you are sure of your own strength, keep away from the Forest of
Magic.

- Grassroots Youkai Network
There seems to be a mutual-aid society for the small, weak youkai who belong
to no faction at all.


***** <FixedQuests>
=== The fixed quests ===

Go to certain places and you can take on a "quest".
Most quests are a matter of killing monsters, but some ask you to find an
item and bring it back.
Carry one out handsomely and the one who asked will reward you.

Some quests, however, are appallingly dangerous.
The danger is shown as a dungeon depth, so use that as your guide.
Unless you can walk that floor of the dungeon with room to spare, it is not
safe. The Ctrl-q command shows the outline and the danger of each quest again.

Look at the entrance to a quest dungeon with the 'l' command and you can see
which quest that entrance belongs to.
Take care not to blunder into a dangerous quest by mistake.

Taking on a monster-killing quest will usually make a dungeon of its own
appear in that town. Go in only once you are ready.
If you truly cannot kill the enemy you can escape by going up the stairs, but
running away means the quest has failed and you get no reward.

Only the quests concerning your base of operations may be attempted again
after you run away. The items on the floor are gone on the second attempt,
however.

Note also that *Destruction*, Genocide and similar magic cannot be used in a
quest dungeon."


***** <WorldMap>
=== The wilderness map ===

Keine: "Press the '<' command on the surface and a wilderness map appears,
showing the whole of Gensokyo from above.
The map you were on is reset then as well, so mind the items on the ground.
You can walk about the wilderness map just as you do the ordinary map, but
time passes faster. Think of every single step on the wilderness map as
walking an ordinary map from one edge to the other.

Besides the Human Village there are various other towns, and you will see
dungeons here and there. Your goal is to conquer the sinister cave west of
the village, the one named Angband.
While you are still a beginner, though, it is better to go to Myouren Temple
just southeast of the village and train in the easy dungeon there, the Cave
of the Great Mausoleum.

While you travel the wilderness map, monsters will sometimes ambush you.
When they do, you are returned to an ordinary map with several monsters
lurking nearby. Do not fight them -- simply run to the next map.
The strength of the monsters is decided largely by the terrain.
Enemies in forest (#), river (~) or mountain (^) are far beyond anything you
can face when you have only just set out.
Keep to the roads (the white dots) for a while yet."
