=== Spells, class abilities and racial powers ===

Keine: "Most classes learn "abilities".
These are the various techniques you pick up as you master your class.
Once you reach the required level you can use them with the 'J' command.

Some races also learn "racial powers".
These are the bodily traits you were born with, put to use.
Once you reach the required level you can use them with the 'U' command.
Polymorph magic and mutations can change which racial powers you have.

And a few classes can learn "spells".
You study them by reading an item called a spellbook, and cast what you have
learned with the 'm' command.
Some of you will think of Royal Flare or Master Spark when you hear the word
"magic", but those are implemented as abilities belonging to the unique
classes alone. A handful of classes, such as the Esper, use the 'm' command
for their abilities as well.

Take the Kappa monk, for example.
The 'J' command gives an ability that restores MP through meditation,
the 'U' command gives a racial power that stretches out an arm to attack,
and the 'm' command casts the spells that have been learned.

None of these can be used while you are confused.
Spells cannot be cast while you are blind or without a light source.

When you want to know what an ability, a racial power or a spell does, the
'b' command shows a short description."


***** <MagicUse>
=== Learning and casting spells ===

Keine: "Let me go into learning and casting spells a little further.
Some of this came up during character creation, but let us go over it again.

First -- obviously -- you cannot learn spells unless your class can cast.
And even a casting class can only learn spells belonging to the "realms" it
has chosen. Realms I shall explain shortly.

Once you have a spellbook of your own realm, the 'G' command learns a spell
from it. Spells you can learn -- where your level and aptitude suffice -- are
shown in blue; choose one and you learn it.
That is one spell added to your repertoire.

Spells you have learned are cast with the 'm' command.
The book that contains the spell must be to hand when you do.
Normally you keep it in your pack, but you can cast from a book lying at your
feet as well. Type 'm', choose the book, then choose a spell you have learned,
and it is cast.

Spells have a difficulty, however, and a hard spell can fail.
The difficulty comes from the spell's base difficulty together with your
aptitude, your proficiency in the spell, your level and your intelligence.
There is equipment that lowers spell difficulty.
Wearing gloves or gauntlets raises it, but arm equipment with a special
enchantment -- free action, a dexterity bonus, a magic device bonus -- carries
no such penalty. High dexterity also reduces the penalty from arm equipment.

You also cannot cast without enough MP.
The MP cost comes from the spell's base cost together with your aptitude and
your proficiency in the spell.
There is equipment that lowers the MP cost.
Equipment heavy enough to slow you down raises the MP cost.
High strength reduces that penalty.

As I said, you cannot read a spellbook while confused, while blind, or with
your light gone out."

***** <MagicBlast>
=== The blast radius of attack spells ===

Keine: "When you read the description of an attack spell you meet words like
"bolt", "ball" and "beam". Let me explain them.

- Bolt
  A spell that hurls a magical arrow or shot. Common among the lower spells.
  It damages the first enemy it strikes and does not pass through.
  It will also be turned back by an enemy that "reflects bolt spells".
  Some spells fire a volley of bolts around the target.

- Ball
  A spell that bursts. Sometimes a projectile flies out before it bursts, but
  that projectile passes through enemies and does not strike them.
  Taking the point of the burst as 100%, the next grid takes 50%, the one
  beyond it 33%, and so on -- the power falls off with distance from the
  centre.

- Beam
  A laser, if you like. It passes through enemies and is never reflected.
  Some spells fire a broad beam.

- Rocket
  Something between a bolt and a ball.
  A projectile flies out, and where it strikes an enemy it bursts on the spot.
  It is not reflected.

- Curses and the like
  You name one target in your line of sight and the effect falls on that enemy
  alone. Think of it as a ball spell with a radius of zero.

- Attacks on everything in sight
  These strike everything in your line of sight. Mind the items on the floor.

- Adjacent
  After casting you choose a direction with the direction keys.
  If a monster stands there, the effect falls on it.

- Breath
  Something between a beam and a ball.
  It damages every target along the line of fire, and deals reduced damage to
  those standing near the line as well."


***** <Realm>
=== The magic realms ===
Keine: "Now then, let me explain the magic realms.
Gensoband has fourteen of them.
Most realms have four spellbooks, each holding eight spells.
At character creation, a casting class chooses one or two of the fourteen.
The first is called the first realm and the second the second realm, and the
second realm is a little harder to learn.

I said you can only learn spells from the realms you chose at the start; for
a class that may choose two realms, however, the second realm can be changed
at any time. Changing it makes you forget every spell of the old second realm.
The number of times you may learn a spell is limited, so I do not recommend
changing without a plan. Reading the rare item called a Scroll of Spell does
raise the number of spells you may learn by one.

There is an exception: a few classes such as Patchouli and the Librarian have
already learned every spell. They need no study, but neither can they change
realm.

The fourteen realms are Element, Foreseeing, Enchant, Summon, Mystic, Life,
Punish, Nature, Transform, Darkness, Necromancy, Chaos, Occult and Incident.
Each belongs to one of the four great realms: Magic, Sorcery, Witchcraft and
Arts.
Element, Foreseeing, Enchant and Summon belong to Magic;
Mystic, Life, Punish and Nature belong to Sorcery;
Transform, Darkness, Necromancy and Chaos belong to Witchcraft;
Occult and Incident belong to Arts.

If your first and second realms belong to different great realms, the level
at which you learn the second realm's spells and their difficulty both rise
sharply. Be warned.

Let me describe the fourteen realms briefly.
If you are a dedicated caster such as a Mage or a High-Mage, the safe course
is to make your first realm one that is rich in attack spells.

- Element (Magic)
  A realm rich in attack spells of the elements -- acid, lightning, fire and
  frost. The damage is comparatively high and the MP cost comparatively low.
  But the elements are the basic ones, so many enemies resist them, and you
  must judge what an enemy resists and pick the right spell.
  Besides attack spells it holds defences against the elements and a few
  supporting spells.

- Foreseeing (Magic)
  Extremely rich in detection: monster detection, terrain detection, temporary
  telepathy and so on. In the end you learn outrageous spells that meddle with
  causality itself, but until then you may as well assume you can do nothing
  but detect.

- Enchant (Magic)
  Extremely well provided with defensive and supporting spells.
  It suits a class that can also fight hand to hand, such as the
  Magic-Knight. It is not entirely without attacks, but relying on them alone is
  quite hard.

- Summon (Magic)
  Rich in summoning and teleport spells.
  A dedicated caster makes a fine summoner.
  But when a summoning fails, the monster you tried to summon attacks you.
  Bear in mind that as a rule you gain neither the experience nor the items
  from enemies your monsters kill.

- Mystic (Sorcery)
  It cannot attack, but it excels at a wide range of supporting spells and is
  good at detection. It suits a class that can fight hand to hand, such as the
  Monk.

- Life (Sorcery)
  Well provided with healing, and capable of a fair amount of defence and
  support. Learning it will make you much harder to kill.

- Punish (Sorcery)
  Many attack spells that are highly effective against youkai, undead and
  demons. It has a fair number of defensive spells as well.
  The god-summoning arts in the last book are all powerful, but only one of
  them can be in effect at a time.

- Nature (Sorcery)
  A balanced realm: attack, defence, detection, support and healing.
  The attack spells are somewhat weak, though, and fighting with them alone
  may prove hard.

- Transform (Witchcraft)
  A varied realm centred on attack, but its most distinctive part is the
  polymorph magic. While transformed your physical powers rise sharply, but
  you cannot read scrolls and some armour stops working.
  Choose your moment with care. The spells of this realm also cost a great
  deal of MP in general.

- Darkness (Witchcraft)
  Many dark-element attacks and sinister spells that borrow the power of
  demons. It holds many aggressive supporting spells and suits a class that
  can fight hand to hand.
  You will do particularly well against living creatures and the forces of
  order. Learning this realm makes you a little weaker to evil-breaking
  attacks. And failing a spell of this realm can bring something unpleasant
  upon you.

- Necromancy (Witchcraft)
  Many attacks of the hell element and of cursing, and sinister spells that
  borrow the power of the dead. Compared with Darkness it has rather more
  defence and summoning. It is strong against living creatures but at a real
  disadvantage against the lifeless.
  Learning this realm makes you a little weaker to evil-breaking attacks.

- Chaos (Witchcraft)
  Well provided with attack spells, and the MP cost is rather low for the
  damage. Many of them use higher elements such as mana, so the enemy hardly
  matters -- but items on the floor are easily destroyed.
  It has a few supporting spells too, unreliable but very useful.
  Failing a spell of this realm can bring something unpleasant upon you.

- Occult (Arts)
  Implemented in version 1.1.08 as "Arcane" and renamed "Occult" in version
  2.1.1. It treats as magic the various occult phenomena that have begun to
  take shape in Gensokyo.
  Many attack and summoning spells, with a few supporting spells among them.
  A good number are awkward to use: some will not work unless a particular
  condition is met, others demand some price of you.
  As for racial aptitude, humans are slightly better at it and the other
  races seem to be about average.

- Incident (Arts)
  Implemented in version 2.1.1, and belonging with Occult to the great realm
  of Arts. You learn its spells by studying the "incident stones" that have
  come to be found in Gensokyo.
  Unlike the other realms it has eight kinds of incident stone, each of which
  makes four spells available.
  Most of what you learn are powerful attack spells, with reasonable defensive
  spells and a little detection.
  The incident stones are somewhat rare, however, so early in your adventure
  you may not find the stone you want and your choice of spells will be
  limited. And a pack filling up with stones, leaving no room for anything
  else, is a drawback of its own.

That is about all."

=== Secret books ===
Keine: "There are also books resembling spellbooks called "secret books", but
these are special volumes for the Samurai and the Chemist alone.
A Samurai who reads a secret book of swordsmanship learns a technique, and
unlike a spellbook it need not be to hand when the technique is used.
A Chemist who reads a secret book of compounding can look up recipes, and
carrying the book matching a given medicine raises the chance of compounding
it successfully."



***** <RealmApt>
=== Aptitude for magic ===

Keine: "Magic does not come to everyone alike.

A High-Mage, for instance, learns spells sooner than a Mage and casts them for
less MP. An Explorer or a Ranger, on the other hand, learns slowly, and there
are powerful spells whose books they cannot even read, let alone learn.

Race matters too.
A Youkai has a hard time of it learning Sorcery, but takes to Witchcraft
readily enough. For a Sennin or a Tennin it is the other way round; a Tennin
in particular will find the Darkness and Necromancy realms wholly beyond
reach.

Your aptitudes are shown during character creation, so use them as your guide.
Of course, if you would rather chase a dream and take a difficult realm on
purpose, I shall not stop you."

***** <MagicSkill>
=== Spell proficiency ===

Keine: "Just after you learn a spell it fails easily and costs a great deal
of MP. Cast it again and again, however, and your proficiency in it rises and
it grows easier to use.
The "[Un]", "[Ex]" and similar marks in the spell list are that proficiency.
"[Un]" is unskilled, "[Be]" is beginner, "[Sk]" is skilled, "[Ex]" is expert
and "[Ma]" is master.
A '!' beside the mark means it can rise no further in your class.

Note also that learning a spell you already know raises its proficiency by one
step. You must of course watch the limit on how many times you may learn, but
the late spells cost so much MP that they are hard to practise, so buying
proficiency by learning is one option worth considering."
