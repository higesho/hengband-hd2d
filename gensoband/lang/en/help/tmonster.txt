=== The monsters of Gensoband ===

Keine: "Gensoband holds more than a thousand kinds of monster, and most of
them will come at you meaning to kill you.
Let me explain these monsters.
Note that in this game everything is called a monster, be it a human or a god.

***** <MonsterSymbols>
=== Monster classes and symbols ===

Keine: "I touched on which symbol shows which monster when we went through
the map, but let us go over it again.
Several symbols have changed their meaning from Hengband.
't' is now tengu, 'S' is deities, 'K' is kappa, 'y' and 'Y' are youkai, and
'F' is fairies.
Spiders have moved to 'c', beetles to 'I', and yeti to the 'H' symbol.
Yeeks and townspeople do not appear in Gensoband at all.

a (ants)          Ants. Not very strong.
b (bats)          Bats. Not very strong.
c (spiders, centipedes) Vermin. Not very strong, though a few are formidable.
d (dragons)       Smallish dragons. Fairly tough, and they breathe at you.
e (eyes)          Many are nasty things that attack you with sleep. Some in
                  the deep floors are quite strong.
f (cats, feline beastmen) Many are quick and hit fairly hard.
g (golems)        Golems and dolls. Mind attacks and weapon slays usually do
                  almost nothing to them.
h (humanoids)     Creatures resembling humans. Many shoot or cast.
i (icky things)   Mysterious slimy creatures. Not very strong.
j (jellies)       Flowing creatures. Not very strong, though something
                  outrageous turns up rarely in the deep floors.
k (kobolds, wolf beastmen) Most simply come and hit you. Not very strong.
l (aquatic creatures) Fish, octopuses and the like. Surprisingly troublesome
                  -- some use nasty spells and special attacks, and the deep
                  floors hold enormous ones.
m (molds)         They do not move; they throw spores. Not very strong, but
                  some are dangerous.
n (nagas, ningyo) Not very strong. Some cast.
o (orcs)          They come at you in packs. Weak alone, but not to be taken
                  lightly early on.
p (humans)        Human enemies. Some are good at magic, some at the sword;
                  they vary greatly.
q (quadrupeds)    Horses, bears and so on. Not very strong, though the deep
                  floors hold powerful sacred beasts.
r (rodents, rabbits) Not very strong.
s (skeletons)     Bony undead. Some in the deep floors cast fierce spells.
t (tengu)         Tengu. Many are quick and strong -- formidable enemies.
u (minor demons)  Demons, if minor ones. Many attack nastily with magic.
v (vortices)      Vortices charged with magic. They usually drift about and
                  breathe.
w (worms)         Weaklings early on, but from the middle depths you meet
                  worms you have to look up at.
(There is no 'x'. Depending on your options it is used for a floor with an
 undetected trap.)
y (lesser youkai) Small youkai. Not very strong.
z (zombies)       Walking corpses. Not very strong, though some cast
                  powerful spells.

A (angels)        From the middle depths they are quite formidable. They make
                  heavy use of evil-breaking attacks.
B (birds, winged creatures) Many attack quickly. From the middle depths some
                  monstrous ones appear.
C (dogs, wolves)  They attack in packs with real discipline. The deep floors
                  hold legendary great beasts.
D (ancient dragons) Truly formidable. The breath from those enormous bodies
                  is overwhelming.
E (elementals)    Their magic is nasty. From the middle depths some monstrous
                  ones appear.
F (fairies)       Not very strong, but they loose danmaku in groups, so do
                  not let your guard down.
G (ghosts)        Many are invisible and pass through walls. Their magic is
                  dangerous too.
H (chimerae and the like) Creatures that look like several beasts joined
                  together. Many have high hit points and hit hard.
I (insects)       Assorted vermin. Not very strong, though a monstrous one
                  turns up occasionally.
J (snakes)        Snakes. Not very strong, though a mighty one can appear in
                  the deep floors.
K (kappa)         Kappa. They often attack with water.
L (liches)        Powerful undead spellcasters. Nasty attacks, all of them.
M (many-headed reptiles) Hydras. The more heads, the stronger. A few are
                  outrageous.
N (mysterious shapes) Shapes of unknown identity. Probing magic and the like
                  can see through them.
O (ogres, oni)    Many hit hard physically. An oni is a formidable enemy.
P (giants)        Many are formidable. Some have terrifying strength, others
                  cast powerful spells.
Q (quylthulgs)    Mysterious mounds of flesh that teleport and summon
                  constantly.
R (reptiles, amphibians) Lizards and frogs early on, but from the middle
                  depths come monsters with dangerous breath.
S (deities)       Divine spirits and stray gods are not very strong, but
                  there are fierce vengeful gods among them.
T (trolls)        Large humanoid monsters, hard to kill and found in groups.
U (major demons)  Many attack with fire and hell. The deep floors hold quite
                  formidable ones.
V (vampires)      Their magic and their draining are nasty. The deep ones hit
                  fiercely in melee as well.
W (wights)        Something more sinister than a zombie. Many cast powerful
                  spells.
X (xorns)         Odd creatures that come through walls.
Y (greater and humanoid youkai) Youkai of real power. They attack in many
                  different ways.
Z (zephyr hounds) A kind of elemental in the shape of a dog. They appear in
                  packs and breathe.

# (trees, tentacles, mist and so on)  Hard to sum up in a word.
% (wall monsters) Moving walls. Leave them alone and they swell before you
                  notice.
* (spheres)       Exploding fireballs, floating magic circles and the like.

Besides these there are mimics that take the shape of an item or of ordinary
terrain, and even a symbol that is usually weak can hold a powerful unique in
the deep floors.
The same symbol and the same colour do not mean the same monster.

You can tell what kind a monster is at once by looking properly with the look
command. Get into the habit of checking anything you see with the look
command. There is also a sub-window that lists the monsters in your line of
sight; use that too.
See the chapters on the commands and the options for details."


***** <MonsterLevel>
=== Monster levels ===

Keine: "A monster's level is expressed as "normally appears on floor N".
A monster of level 60 will normally not appear before floor 59.
But in special structures such as vaults, and in special generation such as
hordes, enemies of a higher level than the floor can appear.
And even ordinary monster generation sometimes rolls the level upward.
Muenzuka in particular seems to produce strong enemies readily. Never let your
guard down."


***** <MonsterAbl>
=== Monster abilities ===
Some monsters have various abilities and traits. Let me name a few.

- Uses spells intelligently
  This monster is clever: when it attacks you with a spell and judges that the
  spell had little effect, it tries a different one next time.
  This does not apply below the HARD difficulty.

- Reflects bolts
  A formidable enemy that turns back bolt-type abilities and spells.
  Shooting with the 'f' command and throwing with the 'v' command are not
  reflected. Bullets from an Engineer's machine are. Be careful.

- Invisible
  This monster is unseen even when it comes close, and your chance to hit it
  is much reduced. See Invisible makes it visible as usual.

- Mindless
  Golems, lower creatures, or singular beings from another world.
  Even with telepathy you cannot sense this monster.
  Mind attacks and similar techniques do almost nothing to it.

- Powerful
  The spells this monster casts do more damage than usual.
  It seems to resist several spells more strongly as well.

- Drives you mad
  A creature of another world whose shape is horrifying beyond imagination.
  Seeing one may confuse you, root you to the spot, or -- if things go badly
  -- show you hallucinations or damage your brain.
  Confusion and paralysis are stopped by their resistances, hallucination by
  resistance to chaos. Resistance to insanity softens the whole of it.

- Multiplies
  A monster that splits from time to time. The new one has full hit points.
  They grow geometrically, and once there are too many you cannot cope.

- Huge
  A monster over about ten metres tall.
  It is hard to stun and to afflict with bad status, and it recovers quickly.
  Some techniques and spells also work less well on it.

- Follows teleports
  When this monster stands beside you and you teleport, it teleports with you.

- Picks up (or destroys) items
  Unpleasant creatures that pick up or destroy items lying on the floor.
  An item that was picked up is yours again once you kill the thief.
  Artifacts are never picked up or destroyed.

- Aquatic
  This monster appears only on water terrain and cannot leave it.

- Flies
  This monster comes at you across lava, dark pits and the like.

- Passes through (or breaks) walls
  This monster comes at you through walls, or by breaking them.

- Changes into another monster
  Only the youkai tanuki and the youkai fox have this.
  The tanuki merely changes its appearance to another monster's, but there
  are several kinds of tanuki and some are strong.
  What you take for a weakling may turn out to be something else.
  The fox changes its abilities completely as well, and you cannot tell until
  you kill it. It may even change into another monster mid-fight.

There are others, but those are the ones that are hard to make out."

***** <MonsterMelee>
=== Monster melee attacks ===

Keine: "Most monsters standing next to you will attack.
The chance to hit is decided by the monster's level and by your AC.
The damage is decided by the attack's dice and by its effect.

For an enemy that "hits to burn for 8d8", for instance, "to burn" is the
effect. Let me give a few examples.

- hits
  An ordinary attack. High AC reduces the damage.

- crushes
  The attack sometimes causes an earthquake. Rocks may fall on your head and
  do extra damage. The basic damage is reduced by AC.

- hits fiercely
  The attack sometimes lands as a "terrible blow" and the damage leaps up.
  The basic damage is reduced somewhat by AC.

- wounds
  The attack cuts you.
  The basic damage is reduced somewhat by AC.

The attacks below are not reduced by AC.

- spits acid, burns, freezes and so on
  The attack takes an element and its damage is reduced by the matching
  resistance. Without immunity or double resistance the element also destroys
  things in your pack.

- confuses
  Without resistance to confusion you are confused. Extremely dangerous.
  With resistance you are not confused, but the damage is not reduced.
  From the middle depths the giant-type enemies often use confusion attacks
  that do heavy damage.

- paralyses
  Without resistance you are paralysed.
  Once paralysed you are attacked over and over and the game is as good as
  over. Enemies with this attack begin to appear around floor 20.
  I say it again: get free action equipment before then, whatever it takes.

- lowers a stat (strength, intelligence, wisdom and so on)
  It lowers one of the main stats a little, for a time. Bad in a long fight.
  Sustain for that stat prevents the loss, but the damage is not reduced.

- disenchants
  Without resistance to disenchantment your equipment loses power.
  Not only the AC bonus but possibly the stat bonuses as well.
  Dreadfully, even an artifact's powers can be lowered.
  Without the resistance, never go near an enemy with this attack.

- drains mana
  This attack damages your MP rather than your HP.

- drains charges
  This attack does more than damage: it empties one of the staves or wands in
  your pack and heals the monster by that much.
  An extremely unpleasant attack. High undead and spellcaster enemies use it
  often.

- steals gold
  It steals about a tenth of the money you carry and teleports away at once.
  Awkwardly, killing the thief does not get it back.
  It flees at once, so landing a blow is difficult too.

There are many other attacks, but those are the main ones.
Even an ordinary blow can stun or cut you if it lands badly."


***** <MonsterPower>
=== Monster spells and the rest ===

Keine: "Some monsters have various spells and abilities. Let me name a few.
Most spells do more damage the higher the enemy's level.

- Breath
  A powerful cone of some element -- fire, acid and so on.
  Almost every dragon has one breath or more.
  A breath's damage depends solely on the monster's current HP, up to a
  ceiling. From about floor 40 the enemies' hit points rise enormously and
  breath becomes a real threat.

- Elemental (acid, lightning, fire, frost) bolts, balls and storms
  Standard elemental spells such as "fire ball".
  They do no great damage, but early on, when your resistances are patchy,
  they are not to be scoffed at.
  The highest of them, "storm of fire", hurts a good deal without double
  resistance. Having your items burnt or melted is nasty as well.

- Higher-element bolts, balls and storms
  Higher spells such as "storm of darkness", "hellfire ball", "mana bolt" and
  "disintegration". All do comparatively high damage, and an enemy that has
  one deserves care.

- Confusion, blindness, fear, paralysis
  Spells that try to inflict a bad status on you.
  All are troublesome without an answer, but paralysis is dangerous in a class
  of its own. Enemies casting paralysis begin to appear here and there from
  about floor 25.

- Mind attacks, brain smashes and the like
  Spells that attack your mind directly.
  Resist successfully and you take nothing, but with low magic defence you
  will be hit often.
  Besides the damage, being hit can confuse or slow you.
  There is said to be an outrageous technique that cannot even be resisted
  without resistance to insanity.

- Curses
  Magical attacks that curse you.
  Resist successfully and you take nothing, but with low magic defence you
  will be hit often.
  Besides the damage, a curse can curse your equipment.
  Some powerful curses halve your HP.

- Exorcism
  A magical attack that tries to purify you.
  Resist successfully and you take nothing, but with low magic defence you
  will be hit often.
  If you are not a being weak to evil-breaking attacks it does nothing at all,
  but a youkai takes a considerable blow.
  Being hit can stun you in proportion to the damage.

- Teleports and the like
  Spells of instantaneous movement, of many kinds.
  Some teleport you rather than themselves; some teleport to your side from
  out of sight.

- Summoning
  They summon monsters of all kinds -- from a single weak one to a ring of
  powerful dragons in an instant.
  Almost every formidable enemy from the middle depths can summon, and you
  cannot simply stand and fight. You will need some answer to it.

- Haste, healing, invulnerability
  A monster sometimes casts on itself.
  Against an enemy with such spells a short fight is what you want.

- Shrieking, alarms
  Troublesome techniques that wake the enemies around with a shout or an
  alarm. An alarm in particular hastes every monster in sight for a while.
  Take care when you fight a monster that has one.

- Dispel magic
  It removes the various temporary effects of your spells and potions.
  Invulnerability, a grand transformation -- anything at all, without appeal.
  Higher spellcasters, demons and deities use it from time to time.

- Rocket
  A powerful weapon.
  The enemy called the Cyberdemon, in the deep floors, uses it often, and it
  does as much as 800 damage.
  Because of them, resistance to shards is essential deep down. Even with it
  you take 400.

- Sword of light
  A powerful beam attack. It ignores every resistance and every protective
  spell.

- Final Spark
  I need hardly say who uses this. A fierce beam of disintegration.
  It is fired even from behind a wall, straight through it.

- Special actions
  A very few monsters have techniques entirely their own.
  Every one is distinctive -- and powerful.

That is the gist of it."


***** <MonsterMemory>
=== Memories and probing ===

Keine: "So each of these thousand-odd kinds of monster has its own abilities
and traits. Ordinarily there would be no hope of remembering them all.

But do not worry. You have, in fact, a prodigious memory.
As you fight a monster you will see it cast some spell, or make some
particular physical attack, or shrug off some element.
Each time, you remember that fact, and you never forget it.

Even if you fall short and the game ends, this memory alone is not erased.
Start a game carrying on the same save data and everything you learned in
your adventures so far is carried over as well.
Adventure again and again and you will hold nearly everything about nearly
every monster in your head. That is a great help.

You need not watch every action of a monster: "probing" tells you almost
everything, its abilities and resistances included.
To probe, use a Staff of Probing, sold in the shops.
There are also several spells with the probing effect, and the Maid, the
Ranger and the Hakurou-Tengu can learn probing as an ability.

What you have learned about a monster can be reviewed at any time: target it
with the 'l' (look) command and press the 'r' key.
You can also show the information about your current target in a sub-window.
See the chapters on the commands and the options for details."


***** <Uniques>
=== Unique monsters ===

Keine: "Among the monsters are formidable enemies called "unique monsters".
Only one of each appears, and once killed it never appears again during that
adventure.
Unique monsters resist bad-status magic, resist many actions, and can never be
killed by anything but you.
And many are plainly stronger than their level suggests. Fight them with your
wits about you, and if you cannot win, flee without hesitating.

The danmaku girls of Gensokyo will all stand in your way as unique monsters.
The name of a unique monster is usually written in the form
"(title) 'Name'"."

--- Special unique monsters ---
Keine: "I said just now that a unique monster once killed never appears again;
in fact there are two exceptions. The shrine maiden and the magician who have
settled so many incidents.
However often they fall they appear before you again, and their strength
varies with the floor you are on.
They are not strong early, but deep down they may have grown into terrible
enemies.

--- Random unique monsters ---
Keine: "There is a still more unusual kind of unique monster: one you truly
can never meet again once you have killed it or fled from it.
So its abilities are wholly unknown until you meet it and fight, or probe it.
The name gives you a rough idea of its strength and of what techniques it is
likely to have, but do not trust that too far."


***** <Pets>
=== Pets ===

Keine: "You can make a monster your pet and have it fight for you.
Take a powerful monster as a pet and fighting becomes far easier at a stroke.
A pet obeys you under considerable compulsion; even if you attack it, it will
not turn on you.

Pets are genuinely useful, but there are things to note.

- As a rule you gain neither experience nor items from the enemies your
  monsters kill. Think of it as their wages -- though a few pets are
  exceptions.

- Having pets slows your MP recovery.
  With many pets your MP falls instead of rising, and when it reaches zero you
  must release enough of them on the spot.
  Compared with Hengband, Gensoband makes the penalty for high-level pets
  heavier. High charisma lets you keep more of them.
  A released pet vanishes from the floor. It does not turn on you.

- When you move between maps or floors, pets that are not close by are left
  behind.

- Attacking your own pet gains you neither experience nor items.
  You do gain a little proficiency. Training on a pet is all very well, but
  do not overdo it.

Now let me tell you how to make a monster a pet.
The standard ways are to summon a monster or to charm one.
The Summon, Necromancy and Nature realms, among others, hold summoning and
charming spells. Some classes and unique classes may have such abilities too.
There is also a scroll that summons a random monster as a pet.

Next there is the "figurine".
Now and then you find a curious item called a "Magical Figurine of X".
Throw it somewhere with the 'v' command and the figurine turns into that
monster and becomes your pet.
Throw a cursed figurine, though, and it turns hostile and attacks you.

Finally there is the "Capture Ball".
A Capture Ball is a book written about youkai, and a monster can go inside it.
Weaken an enemy until it is near death, wield the Capture Ball and activate
it, and the enemy is drawn inside and becomes your pet.
It draws in humans as well as youkai; please do not think about that too hard.
A monster that is already your pet can be drawn in without weakening it.
As a rule you will carry your favourite pets about in a Capture Ball.

Capture Balls are sold now and then at the bookshop in the Human Village.
By the way, in Hengband this item was called a "monster ball"."


***** <Riding>
=== Riding ===

Keine: "Some pets, such as horses and dragons, can be ridden.
When you ride, you stand on the pet's grid and it moves as you move.
A pet you are riding still acts and attacks on its own.
Riding has these further features.

- Speed
  Your speed becomes about the same as the monster you ride.
  In other words you need not buy speed with equipment. That is a great
  advantage. With low riding proficiency you are a little slower, but with
  high proficiency you move faster than the monster itself.

- Attacks are divided
  An enemy's attack falls on either you or the pet you ride.
  Simply by riding you will feel the enemy's attacks much reduced.
  Breath and the like, however, hit both of you.

- Aura damage
  If the monster you ride is wrapped in an aura of fire or the like, you take
  damage as well. Riding is hard without immunity to that element.

- Being thrown
  An enemy's attack can throw you from the saddle.
  I said you need not buy speed with equipment while riding -- but being
  thrown deep in the dungeon turns that on its head at once.
  How easily you are thrown depends on the damage taken, on your riding skill
  and on the level of the pet you ride.
  The higher the pet's level, the harder it is to ride well.
  If mounting gives you the impression that you have it completely in hand,
  you may take it that no enemy attack will throw you.
  But if the monster you ride is killed, down you go, of course.

- A penalty to weapons, a bonus to some
  While riding, your attacks land a little less easily.
  A weapon that is "easy to use while riding", however, has little penalty.
  Almost every spear and polearm is easy to use while riding.
  And the lance and the heavy lance, being riding weapons proper, gain a bonus
  to their weapon dice when used mounted.

- The reins
  One hand is taken up with controlling the monster you ride.
  You can let go with the order command described below, but with low
  proficiency there is no telling where the monster will go.
  Letting go also makes you a little easier to throw.

Riding is extremely useful, but sadly few classes have an aptitude for it.
Aptitude B or so should serve well enough even in the deep floors.
Raising your riding proficiency also takes considerable effort.
You must ride the weaker monsters first and train in a planned way."


***** <Order>
=== Commanding your pets ===

Keine: "You can give your pets various orders with the 'p' command.
Let me name a few.

- Release a pet
  A released pet vanishes from the floor. It does not attack you the moment
  it is freed.

- Set a target
  Use it when you want them to go after a particular enemy.

- Stay close / keep your distance
  You can order how far from you they keep.

- Open doors, pick up items, use teleport spells, and so on
  You decide whether a particular action is allowed or forbidden.

- Include the player
  Normally a pet will not use a breath or a spell that would hit you.
  But if, say, you have immunity to fire and the pet is a behemoth that
  attacks with nothing but fire, it is as well to give this order and let it
  attack freely. Do not forget to turn it off when you change equipment.

- Mount / dismount
  Use it when you want to ride a pet.

- Name a pet
  Use it when you want to give a pet a name.
  A named pet is treated a little more carefully by the game and is less
  likely to be released or left behind by accident.

- Hold your weapon in both hands / one hand
  While riding, one hand is normally used to control the monster.
  You can stop doing that and hold your weapon in both hands.
  You are then easier to throw, and with low riding proficiency you cannot
  control the monster at all.
  In this variant, at riding proficiency 25 the mount stops wandering off when
  you let go, and at 45 you can control it freely with your hands off."



***** <Friendly>
=== Friendly monsters ===

Keine: "Some monsters are friendly.
A friendly monster will not attack you and may attack your enemies alongside
you. Unlike a pet it needs no MP to keep, but neither can you give it orders.
Take care not to be caught in a friendly monster's attacks.
And note that if you attack one, it and every other friendly monster nearby
grow angry and turn hostile."
