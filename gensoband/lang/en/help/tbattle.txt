***** <Energy>
=== Turn order and energy ===
Keine: "Let me begin with the order in which things act.
One of the marks of a roguelike is that you and the enemy act in turn.
That is not quite true of Hengband or of Gensoband, however.
You may certainly take as long as you like to think when it is your turn, but
two beings of the same speed do not necessarily act alternately.

What follows is a little detailed.
Every character, yourself included, needs a certain amount of time after
acting before it can act again. That time is worked out from the "energy
required", the "speed" and a random number.

The energy required depends on the action you have just finished. It is the
same for almost everything, but a very few actions use a different figure.
Dropping an item, shooting or throwing quickly, breaking off an action on a
warning -- these use little time before your next action.
Walking through forest without levitation, on the other hand, costs more.
There are also classes that spend more energy than usual on a powerful attack,
and classes that make small attacks to spend less.

As for speed: the higher it is, the shorter the time to your next action.
Think of +10 as twice the speed of no bonus, and +20 as three times.
But +30 is not four times. From about +25 the benefit weakens, and between +40
and +50 there is scarcely a difference at all.

Then the random number. It rarely troubles you, but now and then it makes a
surprising difference. Even at +30 speed an enemy at +10 can act twice in a
row. Whether you find that thrilling or destructive of strategy is a matter of
taste; either way, that is how the order of actions is decided here.
Bend your mind not to "how do I scrape through a fight on the edge" but to
"how do I gather my strength and always fight in perfect condition".

Note also that the speed of a non-unique monster varies by up to about 50%
from one individual to another. Even within one kind of monster there may be
one that is unusually fast and strong.
When you want a pet to ride, aim for the fastest you can find."


***** <Meleeweapon>
=== Melee weapons ===


Keine: "Now let me explain attacking an adjacent enemy.
As I have said more than once, typing the direction of an adjacent enemy with
a movement command attacks it automatically.
Attacks with an adjacent weapon such as a sword or a spear come first.

One command gives you as many blows as your number of blows.
The number of blows is decided by class, strength, dexterity, how light the
weapon is, and any extra-blows bonus from equipment.
Each class has its own ceiling on blows.
Holding a single weapon in both hands makes blows easier to gain.
A dagger cannot be held in both hands, but a dagger gains a bonus to blows at
high proficiency.

A great many things bear on your chance to hit.
A high level or a high AC on the enemy makes hitting harder.
A race, class or nature suited to melee makes it easier.
High strength and dexterity make it easier.
Your chance rises as you gain levels, though how much depends greatly on class.
Bonuses to hit from your weapon and other equipment raise it, of course.
Temporary effects from buffs, penalties from bad statuses and much else come
into it too.
Your chance to hit bears heavily on your total damage; do not neglect it.

The damage of one blow comes first from the weapon's damage and the bonuses of
your other equipment. High strength then raises it.
And, importantly, from how well the weapon's brands and slays suit the enemy.
For brands and slays, look again at the chapter on items.

The 'C' command shows your ordinary melee damage; the blacksmith can tell you
the detailed damage including slays."

--- Two weapons ---

Keine: "You may hold a weapon in each hand. Your damage is that of both.
It looks strong, but it is not so easy: fighting with two weapons carries a
considerable penalty to your chance to hit.
Against an enemy with a high AC you will barely land a blow without real skill.

For a Warrior or a Samurai with a high aptitude for two weapons it can be a
real choice. Some equipment reduces the two-weapon penalty; using that is
another way."

***** <Shootingweapon>
=== Shooting ===

Keine: "Next, bows and crossbows.
In Hengband a bow or crossbow went into a slot of its own; in Gensoband you
simply hold it in your hands, in both as a rule.
You may hold a shield or a melee weapon in the other hand, but that carries a
penalty to your chance to hit and to your shots.

You shoot with the 'f' command. After the command, choose which missile to
fire, then set a target, and the shot is made.
A missile you have fired can be picked up and used again, though it sometimes
breaks and is lost.

Typing the command for every shot is tiresome, so a macro is usually used.
Macros are explained in a later chapter.

The damage of a single shot is decided almost entirely by the quality of the
launcher and the missile.
While a launcher is wielded, the missile shows the damage
(damage per shot / damage per turn).
The chance to hit is worked out much as it is for melee, but with the shooting
skill rather than the melee skill.
A crossbow gives a slightly better chance to hit than a bow.
Your chance to hit and your shots can be checked with the 'C' command.

To raise your shots, raise your proficiency with the launcher, or wield a bow
or a ring with the "extra shots" property.
The "trigger-happy" nature raises your shots a little, but it lowers your
constitution badly and I do not recommend it.

Shots and melee blows work very differently.
A melee attack uses one ordinary unit of energy for all your blows, whereas
each single shot uses (one ordinary unit of energy / your number of shots).
With 4 shots you fire once in a quarter of an ordinary action's time.
This property in fact makes shooting far more favourable than melee.

As I explained, the order in which you and the enemy act is not fixed in
Gensoband. One of the reasons for defeat deep down is an unexpected run of
enemy actions. Even with 800 HP and more, if an enemy happens to act twice in
a row and both are fierce breaths or spells, the game is simply over.
But when you fight by shooting with a high number of shots, you choose what to
do after every single shot, so you almost never take a run of enemy actions.

When you are used to the game and aiming at your first win, I recommend a
class good with a bow, such as the Ranger or the Archer.
You should be able to fight steadily even against the dangerous enemies of the
deep floors."


***** <Martialarts>
=== Martial arts ===

Keine: "Martial arts means attacking bare-handed. It is used when you wield no
melee weapon. A dedicated martial class, and some unique classes, can strike
harder bare-handed than with a weapon.

Your number of martial blows is decided chiefly by level, dexterity and
martial arts proficiency.
Those with a high aptitude for it also have a higher ceiling on blows.
Extra-blows equipment raises martial blows too, of course.

The damage comes from your strength, from bonuses on rings and other
equipment, and from the kind of martial blow.
The kind of blow means how you strike -- punch, kick, claw and so on.
Each kind has its own dice, weight and extra effect, so the more often the
strong kinds come up, the more damage you do.

What decides the kind of blow is chiefly level, race and class.
If your race is Oni, for instance, an ordinary punch comes out as a "mighty
iron fist" and does as much as a decent martial class.
A ningyo's tail hits hard as well.
If a mutation gives you horns or a tail, martial blows using them can appear.
And if some special magic transforms you, your martial blows change to suit.

The 'C' command shows your martial damage, but as I said the calculation is
far too tangled to give an exact average, so it shows the mean of a hundred
rolls. The figure changes every time you press 'C'; that is how it works, and
I ask your understanding."

--- Extra martial blows during a weapon attack ---

Keine: "You can in fact use martial arts during a weapon attack as well: when
you have a tail or horns, when your martial arts proficiency is high, or when
your shield proficiency is high.
With those abilities and traits you sometimes land a martial blow after a
weapon attack. Holding a staff-type weapon in both hands seems to bring out
extra martial blows more readily."


***** <Throwing>
=== Throwing ===
Keine: "You can also attack by throwing an item at an enemy with the 'v'
command. With a high throwing proficiency you can throw faster than an
ordinary action.

Throwing anything but a melee weapon does little damage.
The exception is a special potion, such as a Potion of Explosion.

The range of a throw is not long, and with low strength it barely carries.
Throwing damage is decided by the kind of item, by how well the weapon's slays
and brands suit the enemy, and by your strength.
Casting Mighty Throw, or wearing a Ring of Throwing, raises it.

The chance to hit comes from throwing proficiency, shooting skill and
dexterity.
A thrown potion shatters and its contents fly everywhere.
A thrown weapon that is not an artifact sometimes breaks and is lost.

Some weapons -- knives, javelins and the like -- are "good for throwing", and
throwing one lands more easily and does more damage.
An artifact good for throwing in particular can be expected to do a great deal.

Rarely you see a weapon that "returns when thrown".
It does not always return, and even when it does you may fail to catch it and
drop it at your feet.
The nimbler you are, the less often that happens, and with a very high
throwing proficiency you will never fail at all.
Any class can throw a little, but not many are good at it."


***** <Critical>
=== Critical hits ===
Keine: "Any of the attacks described above can land as a "critical hit".
When it does, the damage -- the dice part in particular -- leaps up.
As a rule, the heavier the weapon, the more often criticals come."




***** <Skilllevel>
=== Weapon proficiency ===
Keine: "Your skills with weapons, shields and so on have a "proficiency".
Raise it and you handle them more skilfully, to your advantage in a fight.
The ceiling on proficiency is set by your class's aptitude, and you cannot
normally pass it.
To see your present proficiency and aptitude, press '~' and then 'e'.

As a rule, raising proficiency raises your chance to hit with that thing, but
some skills bring other benefits besides. Let me set them out.

- Martial arts
  Blows come more easily when you carry no weapon.
  And the chance of stunning or confusing what you strike rises.

- Shields
  Raise your shield skill and, when you attack an adjacent enemy, you bash
  with the shield as well, up to twice more.
  The heavier the shield the more damage it tends to do, and it can stun.

- Riding
  Raise your riding skill and you are less easily thrown from a high-level
  monster, and your speed while mounted rises to match the monster you ride.
  Past a certain point, letting go of the reins -- to hold a weapon in both
  hands, say -- no longer leaves the mount wandering off.
  In the end you can control the mount freely with your hands off.

- Two weapons
  A weapon in each hand carries a heavy penalty to your chance to hit; the
  two-weapon skill softens it.
  At a very high skill the penalty is all but ignored.
  With weapons or gauntlets suited to two-weapon fighting you may even hit
  better than with one weapon.

- Throwing
  Raise your throwing skill and a throw takes less time.
  As with a bow, you can cut the turn finely and attack while avoiding runs of
  enemy actions.
  Throwing a weapon good for throwing cuts the time further.
  And with a high throwing skill you will always recover a weapon that returns
  when thrown.

- Daggers
  Raise your dagger skill and your blows increase, and the dagger's dice part
  gains +1 for more damage.
  If the Soldier class has taken "dagger fighting", however, that dice bonus
  takes precedence.

- Long swords
  With long swords, the rise in your chance to hit per level of skill is a
  little larger.
  Plain compared with the other skills, but even those with little aptitude
  get some benefit.

- Katana
  Raise your katana skill and the chance of a fierce first stroke rises.
  Such a stroke can do heavy damage, or kill an enemy outright, much like a
  ninja's ambush.

- Hafted weapons
  Raise your hafted skill and the chance of stunning what you strike rises.
  It can stun even an enemy that resists stunning.
  A stunned enemy fails at what it tries again and again. Extremely powerful.

- Staves (polearms of the staff kind)
  Raise your staff skill and you use the staff skilfully to raise your AC.
  Holding it in both hands doubles the effect.
  Attacking with a staff also raises the chance of an extra martial blow.

- Axes
  Raise your axe skill and an enemy you strike is sometimes left with its
  defence lowered.
  For details see "temporary effects on monsters" below.

- Spears
  Raise your spear skill and, when an enemy attacks you in melee, you strike
  first. This is a single blow, but it costs no energy and lands as a critical
  hit more readily.

- Polearms
  Raise your polearm skill and you sometimes flinch an enemy you strike,
  delaying its next action slightly.
  Not enough to beat on it one-sidedly, but with luck you take fewer runs of
  enemy actions.

- Bows and crossbows
  Raise your bow or crossbow skill and you shoot faster.
  Up to version 1.1.93 the rise in shooting speed per level was set separately
  for each class; from 1.1.94 shooting speed rises chiefly as a proficiency
  bonus.
  If you mean to fight with a bow in the end, train the skill in a planned way.
  Some classes gain a further shooting-speed bonus on top of the proficiency
  bonus.

- Guns
  Raise your gun skill and, besides a better chance of a successful shot, the
  gun you wield reloads faster.

That is about all.
Proficiency rises more easily when you fight high-level enemies.
I shall not stop you beating on a pet to train, but that will not carry you
past a certain level."


***** <Buff>
=== Buffs ===

Keine: "Fighting a formidable enemy with no preparation is reckless.
Gensoband gives you many ways to raise your own strength for a time.
Let me name a few of the basic ones. Most come from potions or spells.

- Heroism
  Your spirits are high. Your HP and AC rise by 10 and your melee lands a
  little more easily. It cures fear and prevents you becoming afraid.
  You gain it from spells and potions, and also by drinking liquor.
  But drink too much liquor and you make yourself ill.

- Blessing
  You are blessed by a god -- which one I do not know.
  Your AC rises by 10 and your melee lands a little more easily.
  Your magic defence also rises slightly, so curses and the like are less
  likely to take.
  You gain it from a blessing spell or from a Scroll of Holy Chant.
  The scroll shop sells them, so keep some if you can spare the money.

- Resist fire / cold / lightning / acid / poison
  You have temporary resistance to an element.
  It cuts the damage of that element, or of poison, to one third.
  I have explained this more than once, but it matters, so once more:
  gain a temporary resistance on top of a resistance from equipment and you
  have "double resistance", which cuts the damage to one ninth.
  That holds only for the five listed above (fire, cold, lightning, acid,
  poison). There are spells that give temporary resistance to chaos, hell and
  so on, but they do not give double resistance to those.

  Temporary resistances come chiefly from spells and potions.
  If your class cannot cast them, buy every resistance potion you see in the
  black market.

- Confusing touch
  Read a Scroll of Confuse Monster and your hands glow with this.
  Attack an enemy in this state and you may confuse it.
  Many strong early enemies can be confused, so use it freely.
  From the middle depths it can hardly be relied on.

- Haste
  Your speed rises by +10. That is twice the speed of no bonus, so it is all
  but essential against a formidable enemy.
  Potions of Speed come fairly readily from early on, but they are consumable.
  Get a Staff of Speed as soon as you can.
  There are several spells that haste you as well.

- Stat boosts
  Spells such as Strength raise the matching base parameter.
  The ceiling on a base parameter is normally 40, but there are powerful boost
  spells that break through it.

Those are the basics. There are more than a hundred temporary effects; for the
details, read the description of each ability or spell."


***** <BadStatus>
=== Bad status effects ===

Keine: "An enemy's attack and other things may leave you temporarily unwell.
Let me explain those states.
Most bad statuses wear off with time, but let me also tell you how to cure
them at once.

- Poison
  Your HP and MP stop recovering with time, and you take damage little by
  little.
  A Potion of Neutralize Poison, of Cure Critical Wounds or of Healing cures
  it. Resistance to poison prevents being poisoned.

- Fear
  You panic and cannot attack an adjacent enemy.
  Your spell failure rate rises and your shooting lands less easily.
  A potion or liquor that gives heroism cures it.
  Resistance to fear prevents becoming afraid.

- Confusion
  Your head is in a panic and you cannot move or attack in the direction you
  choose. You also cannot use spells or abilities, cannot read scrolls, and
  fail with staves and other devices almost every time.
  A Potion of Cure Serious Wounds or better cures it; resistance to confusion
  prevents it.

- Blindness
  You cannot see, and cannot make out what is around you.
  You also cannot use scrolls or spellbooks, nor items lying at your feet.
  You have not forgotten the terrain you learned, and telepathy and other
  detection work as usual.
  A Potion of Cure Serious Wounds or better cures it; resistance to blindness
  prevents it.

- Paralysis
  I have explained this often enough. You cannot move for several turns, and
  usually the game is over.
  Free action prevents paralysing attacks, but starvation, a failed attempt to
  break a door and the like can paralyse you even with it.

- Hallucination
  Everything you see turns into something strange.
  You can make out neither monsters nor items.
  Somehow you can still read spellbooks and scrolls normally.
  Curing hallucination needs a Potion of Curing, a staff or some other rather
  special means. It often does not wear off quickly with time.
  Resistance to chaos prevents it.

- Slowness
  Your speed falls by -10. Fighting on in that state is dangerous.
  It wears off with time, and it cancels out against temporary haste.

- Petrifaction
  The same as paralysis, but you cannot move even with free action.
  Ancient malice, and the blood curse described later, rarely cause it.
  Very high magic defence can prevent it.

- Stat loss
  An enemy's attack and other things lower a base stat for a time.
  This does not heal with time, but the matching Potion of Restore X brings it
  back. Sustain for a stat prevents its loss.
  Stat loss from a time-reversal attack, however, is prevented only by
  resistance to time and space.

- Experience loss
  Hell attacks, experience-draining blows, chaos attacks and so on reduce your
  experience, and can lower your level.
  This does not heal with time, but a Potion of Restore Life Levels brings it
  back. Resistance to hell greatly reduces experience loss.

- Cuts
  Left alone, they damage you every turn.
  You are cut by an enemy's slashing attacks, by curses, by the shard element
  and so on, and a very bad wound can do more than 100 damage.
  A Potion of Cure Critical Wounds or better heals any cut in an instant.
  There is no resistance that prevents cuts, though some races cannot be cut.

- Stunning
  Your spell success rate falls.
  An enemy's blows, sound attacks, water attacks, plasma attacks and so on
  stun you. Stunning accumulates, and past a certain point you black out and
  fall where you stand.
  You go on being attacked, of course, and a game over is all but unavoidable.
  A Potion of Cure Serious Wounds or better clears stunning.
  Resistance to sound, to water and so on prevents stunning by those elements.
  There is no resistance that prevents stunning from blows, though some races
  cannot be stunned.

- Damage (to the body)
  A few special races such as the Youkai-Doll and the Haniwa take "damage"
  rather than cuts and stunning.
  In this state their abilities fall, and at heavy damage they are in no state
  to fight.
  Damage mends a little at a time, and a racial ability can sometimes repair
  the body.

That is roughly all.
The Potion of Cure Critical Wounds heals a fair amount and cures many bad
statuses -- a truly excellent potion. It is sold in the shops, so if your
funds and your weight limit allow, carrying dozens at all times is no bad
thing."



***** <Monster-TimedStatus>
=== Temporary effects on monsters ===

Keine: "Monsters have temporary buffs and bad statuses too. Let me set them out.

- Haste
  A hasted monster gets +10 speed.
  You then take runs of attacks more easily, which is dangerous.
  If a formidable enemy is hasted, dispel its magic or run about until the
  haste runs out.
  A monster with the Haste spell can haste itself.
  A monster with the Alarm spell wakes every monster around it and hastes them
  all. And waving a Wand of Haste Monsters, or shattering a Potion of Speed
  nearby, hastes a monster.

- Invulnerability
  Almost nothing works on the monster.
  A powerful monster with the "globe of invulnerability" spell can make itself
  invulnerable.
  An attack gets through to an invulnerable monster one time in thirteen, and
  the "sword of light" element, or the attacks of some powerful unique classes,
  pierce invulnerability.
  Casting "dispel magic" also removes a monster's invulnerability.
  Otherwise, once a monster is invulnerable there is usually nothing for it but
  to run about until it runs out.

- Sleep and holding
  Most monsters are asleep when you first come down to a floor.
  A held monster likewise does not move.
  A monster in either state does nothing, but land a single attack or hostile
  spell on it and it wakes and attacks.
  With low stealth, wandering nearby wakes them more easily.
  Some monsters sleep deeply and some lightly; as a rule a monster that
  "watches for intruders" wakes easily and one that "pays no attention to
  intruders" wakes with difficulty.
  The difference between sleep and holding is whether the monster's resistance
  applies. Sleep attacks do nothing at all to a monster that resists sleep,
  but a holding attack may still work.


- Slowness
  A slowed monster gets -10 speed. It becomes much easier to kill.
  Spells such as Slow, and attacks of the slowing element or of some mind
  elements, can slow a monster.
  Slowing a high-level monster is quite hard, mind.
  Slowness wears off with time, and a high-level monster recovers quickly.

- Confusion
  A confused monster cannot move straight and cannot cast. A considerable
  weakening.
  The Scroll and Wand of Confuse Monster sold in the shops, and attacks of the
  confusion element, of mind elements and of chaos, can confuse a monster.
  It does nothing to a monster that resists confusion, and almost every late
  monster does.
  Confusion wears off with time, and a high-level monster recovers quickly.

- Stunning
  A stunned monster sometimes does nothing, and very often fails when it tries
  to cast. Another considerable weakening.
  Sound attacks, water attacks, force attacks and some mind elements can stun
  a monster.
  As a rule it does nothing to a monster that resists stunning.
  But melee with martial arts or a hafted weapon can stun even a monster that
  resists it.
  Stunning wears off with time, and a high-level monster recovers quickly.

- Fear
  A frightened monster does not attack in melee and runs about.
  It also uses teleport and healing spells rather than attacks.
  Besides spells that cause fear, heavy damage can frighten a monster.
  A monster that resists fear is never frightened.
  Fear passes with time or as the monster heals, and a monster cornered in a
  dead end can recover as well.

- Lowered attack (from v1.1.94)
  A monster with lowered attack does about 25% less melee damage and no longer
  lands "terrible blows".
  Potions and spells with the lowering effect, and attacks of the shard,
  causality or petroleum elements, can lower a monster's attack.
  A plainer effect than slowness, confusion or stunning, and for that reason it
  takes somewhat more readily on a high-level monster.
  It wears off with time, and a high-level monster or one that regenerates
  quickly recovers fast.

- Lowered defence (from v1.1.94)
  A monster with lowered defence has about 25% less AC, and its saving throws
  against bad statuses and mind attacks are slightly weakened.
  Potions and spells with the lowering effect, and attacks of the
  disenchantment or causality elements, can lower a monster's defence.
  Otherwise it is as for lowered attack.

- Lowered magic (from v1.1.94)
  A monster with lowered magic has its spell level reduced by 25% and its
  spell success rate lowered.
  Potions and spells with the lowering effect, and attacks of the causality
  element or of many mind elements, can lower a monster's magic.
  Otherwise it is as for lowered attack.

- Bound (from v1.1.95)
  A bound monster can neither move nor teleport, and fails at anything it
  tries with a small probability.
  Certain special spells and abilities bind a monster.
  Binding wears off with time.
  A huge monster will break out of it at once.

- Berserk (from v1.1.95)
  A berserk monster can no longer cast, but its melee does roughly twice the
  damage.
  Besides sealing an enemy's magic, you can strengthen a melee pet by making
  it berserk.
  Throwing a Potion of Berserk Strength, or certain abilities and spells, makes
  a monster berserk.
  It wears off with time, and a high-level monster or one that regenerates
  quickly recovers easily.

Attacks that bring on these bad statuses take poorly on unique monsters, on
deities and on powerful monsters.
In Hengband bad statuses hardly worked on unique monsters at all; in Gensoband
they work on them to some degree.
So the Slow Monster and Confuse Monster devices you find early on may still
have their uses.

Some unique classes can also give monsters special temporary effects that are
none of the above."


***** <Attack_type>
=== Damage elements ===
Keine: "The spells and techniques of Hengband carry various elements.
Here let me explain chiefly the elements of the attacks the enemies use.
Some of this repeats what I said about monster abilities and about equipment
resistances, but it matters, so let me gather it in one place.

- Acid
  An attack of strong acid, reduced by resistance to acid.
  It can damage the armour you wear and dissolve staves, scrolls and the like
  in your pack.
  Double resistance or immunity reduces the damage further and stops items
  dissolving entirely.
  When acid damages your armour, the damage to you is reduced a little.

- Lightning
  An electrical attack, reduced by resistance to lightning.
  It can break rings and wands in your pack.
  Double resistance or immunity reduces the damage further and stops items
  breaking entirely.

- Fire
  An attack of fire, reduced by resistance to fire.
  It can burn staves, scrolls and the like in your pack.
  Double resistance or immunity reduces the damage further and stops items
  burning entirely.

- Cold
  An attack of cold, reduced by resistance to cold.
  It can shatter potions in your pack.
  Double resistance or immunity reduces the damage further and stops potions
  shattering entirely.

- Poison
  An attack of poison. It can poison you.
  Resistance to poison reduces the damage and prevents being poisoned.
  Double resistance reduces the damage further.

- Water
  An attack of torrents or masses of water, reduced by resistance to water.
  Taken without the resistance it can stun you and shatter potions.

- Holy (evil-breaking)
  A holy attack. Resistance to holy halves the damage.
  If your race is weak to it -- a youkai, an undead -- the damage is greater.

- Light
  An attack of dense light rays. It can blind you.
  Resistance to light reduces the damage and prevents blindness from light.

- Dark
  An attack of some mysterious dark power. It can blind you.
  Resistance to dark reduces the damage and prevents blindness from dark.

- Shards
  An attack of hard objects flying at speed.
  It cuts you and shatters potions.
  Resistance to shards reduces the damage, prevents cuts, and prevents
  "almost" all potion breakage.

- Sound
  An attack of shock waves. It can stun you and shatter potions.
  Resistance to sound reduces the damage and prevents potion breakage from
  sound. It also prevents stunning from sound and several other elements.

- Nether (hell)
  An attack with the power of death, used often by the undead.
  Being hit costs you part of your experience for a time, and can lower your
  level. Resistance to hell reduces both the damage and the experience loss.

- Chaos
  An attack, it seems, with the disordered energy of before the making of the
  universe.
  Being hit brings on confusion, experience loss, hallucination, mutation and
  item destruction -- all manner of unpleasant things.
  Confusion is stopped by resistance to confusion; experience loss by
  resistance to chaos or hell; hallucination and mutation by resistance to
  chaos. Item destruction is mostly stopped by resistance to chaos, but not
  entirely. An awkward element in every way.

- Disenchantment
  An attack of a special energy that drains magic and vitality.
  Being hit lowers the quality of your equipment, and can even disenchant an
  artifact.
  Resistance to disenchantment reduces the damage and prevents equipment being
  disenchanted.

- Plasma
  An attack of great heat with lightning in it.
  It counts as both fire and lightning. Without resistance to sound it also
  stuns.

- Nuclear heat
  A compound of fire and light. Half of the power is reduced by each
  resistance.

- Pollution
  A compound of poison and disenchantment. Half of the power is reduced by
  each resistance.

- Whirlwind
  An attack of fiercely swirling wind. It blows you away and shatters potions.
  There is no resistance to it, but a heavy pack keeps you from being blown
  away and greatly reduces the damage.
  With levitation, being blown away does somewhat less damage and potions are
  not shattered.

- Gravity
  An attack that seems to make a gravity field.
  Being hit blows you away, and can stun you, slow you or shatter potions.
  Resistance to time and space reduces the damage and prevents the extra
  effects.
  Even without it, levitation prevents most of the extra effects.

- Time reversal
  An attack, it seems, that reverses time to weaken you.
  Being hit lowers your stats and experience greatly, for a time.
  Neither sustain nor resistance to hell prevents this; resistance to time and
  space does.

- Causality
  A strange and powerful attack that seems to meddle with the laws of time and
  space.
  Being hit does heavy damage and brings on all sorts of odd things.
  You may be teleported somewhere, you may mutate, and at worst your
  parameters may be swapped about.
  Resistance to time and space reduces the damage and prevents the odd effects.
  That resistance is hard to come by, but happily almost no enemy uses
  causality attacks.

- Slowing
  An attack that hinders your movement -- slime or sticky thread, perhaps.
  Being hit slows you (-10 speed) for a time.
  If you were hasted, the two cancel out and the effect is lost.
  No resistance prevents it, but the damage is never very great.

- Force
  A release of life energy, it seems.
  No resistance reduces this attack. Without resistance to sound it also stuns.

- Mana
  A radiation of pure magical power.
  No resistance reduces this attack.

- Disintegration
  A powerful attack that breaks matter apart and destroys it.
  No resistance reduces this attack.

- Meteor
  An attack of great mass flying at speed. It can burn your possessions and
  shatter potions.
  No resistance reduces this attack. Resistance to shards very likely prevents
  the item destruction.

- Sword of light
  Condensed energy, it seems, born of tremendous mental power.
  No resistance reduces this attack, and it pierces every defence -- magical
  armour, becoming ghostly, invulnerability and the rest.

- Hellfire
  An extremely dangerous attack. No equipment gives resistance to it, and if
  you are a Tennin, a Lunarian or a Gyokuto the damage is fiercer still.
  Very few enemies use it. You will meet it in the deepest floors.

That is about all.
I say it again: as a rule, do not fight an enemy whose attacks use an element
you have no resistance to."


***** <Weak_point>
=== Weak points ===

Keine: "You may have a particular element as a weak point.

The best-known is a weakness to evil-breaking attacks.
Many youkai have it, and take 33% more damage from such attacks.
For an undead or a demon race it rises to 66%, and learning Darkness or
Necromancy magic adds another 25% to 33%.
If Remilia has learned the Darkness realm, for instance, she takes twice the
damage in total from an evil-breaking attack.
Deep down, with the resistance missing, a single blow from a shrine maiden or
a Silver Wyrm may end the game.

There are other weaknesses besides: Cirno and Letty are weak to fire, a
Tsukumo or a Youkai-Doll is weak to disenchantment, and so on.
You can see them at once by showing the resistance screen with the 'C'
command; the elements in question are printed in red.
Exactly how much the damage rises, though, you can only learn by taking it.
Whatever else, keep as far as you can from enemies that use your weak element."


***** <Tactics1>
=== Tactics -- shallow levels ===

Keine: "From here let me set out a few tactics that suit each stage of
exploring. First the shallow levels -- down to about floor 20."

--- The prospector ---
Keine: "Short of money early on?
Then shouldering a pick and setting to work mining is one way.
First buy a Pick at the general store in the village and a Scroll of Treasure
Detection at the scroll shop. It costs a little more, but a Staff of Treasure
Location from the magic shop will do as well.
Then go in and out of a nearby dungeon (the cave at Myouren Temple will do)
until you find a place where several veins show in the walls, and detect
treasure. A great deal of treasure buried in the walls around you will appear.
Wield the pick and dig those walls with the 'T' command to make money.
Stick at it a while and you can build up several thousand gold.
But mind the monsters nearby. If white mice start multiplying you are in
trouble."

--- Buffs you can use from early on ---
Keine: "While your equipment is still thin, even a slightly strong monster is
a formidable enemy. Let me name a few buffs you can use from the start.

- Scroll of Holy Chant
  Read it and you are blessed, raising your defence and your attack. Sold at
  the scroll shop.

- Scroll of Confuse Monster
  Read it and your next physical attack confuses the enemy once.
  It works on most early monsters, which do not resist confusion. Sold at the
  scroll shop.

- Liquor and Potions of Heroism
  Drink liquor or a Potion of Heroism and you gain heroism, raising your
  attack, defence and HP and stopping fear.
  Liquor is sold in the shops, but too much is dangerous, so go steadily.
  Be particularly careful not to drink some tremendously strong-looking liquor
  that no shop sells.

- Potion of Speed
  The trump card of the early game -- a useful potion that lets you move twice
  as fast.
  The ordinary potion shop does not stock it; the black market does now and
  then. It is often found on the first floor of a dungeon as well, so going in
  and out from the surface and using Scrolls or Staves of Object Location to
  look for one is another way.

- Devices that inflict bad statuses on monsters
  Confuse Monster, Slow Monster and the like.
  They used to do nothing at all to a slightly higher-level monster or to a
  unique, but from version 1.1.90 they work to some degree on early monsters.

- Stat-lowering potions
  Potions of Weakness, of Clumsiness and so on, which lower your stats if you
  drink them.
  From version 1.1.94 you can throw them at a monster to weaken it for a time."

--- Torches and crossbows ---
Keine: "If you are poor both at melee and at magic, I recommend torches and
crossbows.
A torch is a light source, but throw one at an enemy with the v command and it
does a fair amount of fire damage.
And a crossbow is a missile weapon that lands fairly easily even at low skill.
Keep your distance with Scrolls of Phase Door and you can hold your own against
a reasonably formidable enemy."

--- Teleport Away ---
Keine: "Let me tell you the strongest spell of all: Teleport Away.
Wave a Wand of Teleport Away and a beam comes out that forcibly teleports
whatever it strikes.
It will serve you from the beginning of the game to the end for avoiding a
fight you do not want.
It takes rather poorly on an enemy that resists teleportation, and does nothing
at all to a unique monster that resists it."


***** <Tactics2>
=== Tactics -- middle levels ===
Keine: "Let us call floors 30 to 60 or so the middle game.
This is when the character of each class begins to show.
Your equipment fills out, but the resistances you need grow as well, and
finding the right combination will tax you."

--- The badger hole ---
Keine: "Surely nobody is reckless enough to fight surrounded by formidable
enemies in a great hall?
When there are several enemies the rule is to back into a corridor and take
them one at a time, but if they have breaths and spells they will attack you
one after another from behind.
Then dig a zigzag with a Wand of Stone to Mud.
Drawn out it looks like this.
#######
#@#Z#D#Z
##Z#Z#ZZZ
########Z
#########
This greatly reduces the number of enemies that can attack you.
It will not serve, mind, if there are enemies with disintegration attacks,
earthquake attacks or the ability to move through walls."

--- The line-of-sight trick ---
Keine: "Are you used to targeting for shooting and spells yet?
There is a useful way to use that targeting.
You cannot target an enemy hidden by the corner of a corridor, but from the
targeting state you can press '*' to enter manual targeting, then move the
target a little behind the enemy so that the line of fire just clips it.
Fire from there and the attack strikes the enemy on the line.
That lets you attack before the enemy comes into view.
This works only for shooting, bolts, beams and the like -- not for balls or
curses."

######p**O
######

(By aiming at the O, you hit the p that cannot normally be targeted)

--- The Poison Needle ---
Keine: "There is a weapon called the Poison Needle, familiar from a certain
famous RPG series.
Wield it and stab an enemy and you sometimes do 9999 damage.
Yes -- even a huge dragon with 5000 HP dies at a stroke.
Take a Poison Needle to the Dragon's Lair and aim at a wyrm or another
formidable enemy.
Stab a few times and, if it does not work, flee before your own HP runs out.
With luck you gain tens of thousands of experience and a great many fine items.
It is dangerous, of course, and it needs a certain amount of HP, double
resistances, a means of detection and other preparation and care.
Some people dislike this as "scumming" -- skimming off the good part -- but it
is not against the rules.
If you are stuck in the middle game, trying it at your own risk is one option.
And for the avoidance of doubt: the Poison Needle does nothing to a unique
monster."

--- Doping ---
Keine: "Building up your equipment matters, but without raising your base
stats the middle game is hard to break through.
To raise them, look for Potions of Strength, of Intelligence and so on and
drink every one you find.
Floors 30 to 45 or so are where they turn up most.
An enemy that drops only "good items" will not yield them.
Using nothing but the Poison Needle in the Dragon's Lair may in fact slow your
progress."


***** <Tactics3>
=== Tactics -- deep levels ===
Keine: "By the time you set foot below floor 60 or 70 you will be strong
enough to face the great figures of Gensokyo.
Now comes the stage of levelling to the ceiling and gathering your final
equipment for the last battle.
By now strategies differ from player to player, so let me set out only what is
dangerous not to know."

--- *Destruction* ---
Keine: "The deep floors often produce great numbers of enemies.
And most of the formidable ones have powerful summoning magic.
You cannot deal with them one by one. This is what *Destruction* is for.

When *Destruction* goes off, an area of radius 12 to 17 around you is wrecked.
In detail: every monster and item vanishes, and the terrain becomes a random
shape of rock and floor.
You and the grid you stand on are untouched.
Think of the vanished monsters as being blown to some floor above or below.
A unique monster caught by *Destruction* will turn up again somewhere in time.
*Destruction* comes from a staff, a scroll, or certain spells -- the Chaos,
Foreseeing and Punish realms have them.

Scrolls are consumable, so you will mostly rely on the staff; but the Staff of
*Destruction* is quite hard to use as a device.
A Warrior or a Samurai, poor with devices, will struggle deep down without
equipment that raises the magic device skill."

--- Means of emergency escape ---
Keine: "Deep down, enemies with powerful ranged attacks are usually awake and
a careless teleport is dangerous -- especially when your HP is low.
But there will be times when you must risk it: when a summoning surrounds you
just after a fierce spell has hit you in a fight with a formidable enemy.

At such a time, give up the fight altogether and flee to another floor.
You lose the experience and the items of the enemy you had nearly killed, of
course, but while there is life there is hope.
For fleeing I recommend the Scroll of Teleport Level.
Read it and you are thrown to another floor, where you can start afresh.
The black market sometimes sells them in a batch, so carry a few when you go
deep.

The Scroll of *Destruction* can serve as an escape as well, but they are few
and it is a waste.
You may wonder about the Staff of *Destruction*; it cannot be relied on.
The reason is that staves and other devices sometimes fail.
A class good at magic rarely fails even with a difficult device, but a bad roll
at the crucial moment and a game over is a common enough story.

Once you have avoided the fight by these means, heal at once, even if you can
see no enemy nearby. There is no telling what happens in a dungeon.
And it hardly needs saying: confusion and blindness stop you reading scrolls,
so mind those resistances too."

--- The Amberites and the blood curse ---
Keine: "There are beings called the Amberites.
They look no different from humans, but they are extremely long-lived, have
astonishingly tough bodies, and can walk between parallel worlds at will.
Twelve Amberites appear in Gensoband.
They will stand as great obstacles late in your adventure.

...Nobody was rude enough to think "so it is a parade of the author's own
characters", I trust?
Let me explain, just in case.
The Amberites are the family that appears in the series called the Chronicles
of Amber, by the American writer Roger Zelazny.
They were implemented in Zangband, which came from Angband, and they hold an
important place in Hengband, which came from Zangband in turn.
The Japanese translation of the Amber series is out of print, but do read it if
you get the chance. It is a fantasy of overwhelming scale and a masterpiece of
the con game.

To return to the point.
Every Amberite is quite strong, but the greatest threat is their final attack,
the "blood curse".
When you kill an Amberite, half the time they lay a terrible curse on you.
When cursed, something like a stronger version of the ancient malice I
described earlier happens.
With bad luck you are surrounded by enemies and petrified, and the game is
over.
As a countermeasure: with magic defence of about "legendary[2]" petrifaction
does not happen.
A high-level shrine maiden has that much magic defence unaided; with another
class it may be worth securing it even with anti-magic equipment.
By the way, killing each Amberite raises your score a little."
