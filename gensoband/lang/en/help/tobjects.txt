=== Items ===
Keine: "Gathering items and using them well is one of the most important
things in a roguelike.
Without powerful equipment, a great many healing potions and scrolls, and the
free use of all sorts of magical devices, you cannot fight your way through a
dungeon. Let me explain the various systems that concern items."


***** <EquipInvent>
=== Inventory and equipment ===

Keine: "You carry an adventuring pack that holds 23 kinds of item.
One ration of food and ninety-nine suits of plate mail each count as one kind.
Most items stack, several to a kind, but staves with different charges,
equipment with different properties and a few other special items cannot be
stacked.

Besides the pack there is your "equipment".
It hardly needs saying that a weapon or a piece of armour does nothing unless
it is worn. There are twelve equipment slots:
right hand, left hand, ribbon, right finger, left finger, neck, light source,
body, cloak, head, arms and feet.

The right and left hands hold weapons or a shield.
When you wield a weapon other than a dagger and the other hand is free, you
automatically hold the weapon in both hands and gain a bonus to your damage.
You may also carry a bow, or a weapon in each hand.
Some special items, such as the Capture Ball, are wielded before use.

The rest of the equipment is plain enough to place.
You need not take off your gloves to put a ring on or off, and wearing full
plate mail leaves the head, feet and other slots free.

When you take a piece of equipment off it goes into your pack automatically.
If the pack is full it falls at your feet.

A few classes have item slots that are neither the pack nor the equipment: the
Secondhand-dealer's tool box, the Engineer's backpack, Rin Kaenbyou's cart.
Use them and you will see.

If what you carry is too heavy you slow down.
Early on this cuts your fighting strength badly, so take care.
And, as I said before, equipment that is too heavy makes spells harder to
cast: the MP a spell costs rises in proportion to how far over the limit you
are. Casters in particular should watch it.
The higher your strength, the more you can carry comfortably."


***** <UseItem>
=== Using items ===

Keine: "Some items have an effect when you use them. Let me explain how.
The commands I name are those of the default keyset; if you have set another,
see the chapter on the commands.

You can use an item at your feet exactly as you use one in your pack.
When asked which item, the prompt shows "floor '_'", so type '_'.

- Staves '_'
  A staff is used with the 'u' (use) command. Using one affects you or your
  surroundings. It has a set number of charges and cannot be used when they
  run out. Staves with different charges do not stack.
  You can restore the charges at a Divine Spirit Mausoleum branch office or
  with recharging magic. Recharging by spell or scroll can fail and destroy
  the item.

- Wands '-'
  A wand is used with the 'a' (aim) command.
  Most of them fire something in the direction you choose after the command.
  Wands stack regardless of their charges.
  The inventory shows the total charges of all the wands you hold.
  These too can be recharged at a Divine Spirit Mausoleum branch office or
  with recharging magic. Recharging by spell or scroll can fail and destroy
  the item.

- Rods '-'
  A rod is used with the 'z' (zap) command.
  Some are aimed and fired; others simply take effect when used.
  A rod has no charges: once used it is "recharging", and after a while it can
  be used again. How long that takes varies. Rods stack whatever their state.
  A Divine Spirit Mausoleum branch office or recharging magic can finish the
  recharge at once. Recharging by spell or scroll can fail and destroy the
  item.

- Ability cards '#'
  Some ability cards are used with the 'u' command; others take effect merely
  by being carried.
  A very few are had as the reward for particular quests.
  There is said to be a card exchange at the Moriya Shrine by night.
  A few classes need to buy these cards up at a high price to strengthen
  themselves.

  Staves, wands, rods and ability cards all call on your "magic device" skill.
  Try to use a high-level device with a low skill and you will fail almost
  every time. And while confused or berserk they are all but useless.

- Scrolls '?'
  A scroll is used with the 'r' (read) command. Read it once and it is gone.
  You cannot read while confused or blind, or without a light.
  Reading a scroll never fails -- unless your asthma is very bad indeed.

- Food, mushrooms, sweets ','
  Food and mushrooms are eaten with the 'E' (eat) command. They are gone once
  eaten. An ordinary race fills its stomach by eating.
  A Tsukumo or a Youkai-Doll can eat one charge from a staff or a wand, and a
  Golem can eat ores.

- Potions, liquor and other drinks '!'
  Potions and liquor are drunk with the 'q' (quaff) command. They are gone
  once drunk.
  Some potions are dangerous poisons, so do not drink one whose effect you do
  not know.
  Drinking liquor raises your morale and drives off fear, which has its uses,
  but drink too much and all sorts of odd things happen; keep drinking beyond
  that and you fall down at last.
  Some drinks are strong enough to fell you with a single bottle, so take care.

- Flasks of oil '!'
  When your lantern runs low you must refill it or the light goes out.
  Refuel with the 'F' command.
  A magical lantern and other fine light sources need no fuel.

That is about all.
I say again: mind that some things cannot be used while confused or blind."


***** <Activate>
=== Activating equipment ===

Keine: "Some pieces of equipment can be "activated" for a magical effect.
Activation uses the 'A' command, works only on equipment you are wearing, and
fails easily with a low magic device skill. You cannot activate while confused.

Activate a Ring of Flames, for instance, and it looses a fireball and makes
you temporarily stronger against fire.
After activation it recharges over time like a rod, and after a while it can
be activated again. It does not recharge while unworn."


***** <Ident>
=== Identifying items ===

Keine: "As a rule you identify an item you have picked up before using it.
A staff or a scroll comes with its name unknown and its effect hidden when you
first find one, and a piece of equipment worn carelessly may carry a powerful
curse. There are several kinds of identification.

- Pseudo-identification
  Carry a piece of equipment for a while and you get a rough sense of its
  quality: "your Long Sword feels good", and so on.
  It is vague and applies only to equipment, but it is enough to sort out what
  you do not want.
  Pseudo-identification varies with class: as a rule a warrior is good at
  judging arms and armour, a caster at judging rings and such.
  A class that is poor at it judges slowly and gets a vaguer message.
  A Warrior carrying arms and armour soon tells "average", "good", "excellent"
  and "special", while a Mage takes longer and can tell only "average" and
  "good or better".

- Identification
  The standard sort.
  Do this to an item and the true nature of an unknown potion or staff is
  revealed. Once you know what a kind of item is, every later one of the same
  kind is known as well.
  Identify a piece of equipment and you learn its quality and its basic
  values, and if it is excellent or special you learn its kind as well.
  Not all the powers of an excellent or special item are revealed, however.

  There is no shortage of ways to identify.
  The shops sell Staves and Scrolls of Identify, and the Hieda Manor will
  identify for you. Many classes have identifying spells or abilities.

  When an enemy's magic or some special effect makes you "amnesiac" you forget
  part of what identification told you.

- *Identification*
  The strongest sort.
  Do this and you learn everything about an item.
  The 'I' command then shows the item's full description, and all the powers
  of an excellent or special item are revealed.
  What is more, amnesia can no longer make you forget it.

  For *identification*, take the item to the library of the Scarlet Devil
  Mansion or to Kourindou and have it studied. There are also powerful scrolls
  and spells that *identify*.
  Note that every item sold in a shop, and every item you begin the adventure
  with, is already *identified*."


***** <CursedItem>
=== Cursed items ===
  Keine: "A piece of equipment you pick up may be cursed.
Once worn it cannot be taken off until the curse is removed, and it may have
some bad effect besides.

There are three degrees of curse.
- An ordinary curse
  It can be taken off by Remove Curse. It sometimes carries a mildly
  unpleasant effect.
- A heavy curse
  Ordinary Remove Curse will not shift it; a Scroll of *Remove Curse*, or the
  shrine, will. It carries a decidedly unpleasant effect.
- A permanent curse
  No remove curse of any kind will shift this.
  You can only destroy the item itself with special magic or a scroll.

Here are some of the effects a cursed item may carry.
- It sometimes damages your HP or MP
  It will rarely kill you outright, but you had better remove it all the same.
- It makes you hungry faster
  Dangerous in some situations. Best go home and remove it soon.
- It draws dragons
  A summoning goes off by itself from time to time and dragons attack. Quite
  dangerous.
- Aggravation
  It angers everything around you: every sleeping enemy nearby wakes the
  moment you approach, and every friendly being turns hostile.
  In some summoning arts and some quests this is disastrous, so take care.
- Random teleportation
  It teleports you at random. Dangerous in the deep floors in particular.
  Note that random teleportation without a curse can be controlled.
- Ancient malice
  Also known as "the curse of Topi Ylinen".
  When it goes off, outrageous things happen: paralysis that ignores your
  resistance, teleportation you did not ask for, powerful monsters all around
  you -- and several of them may follow one after another.
  There are extremely powerful items that carry ancient malice, but using such
  an item is a considerable gamble.

A Tsukumo or a Tsukumo-master can take off a cursed item without removing the
curse.

Normally only equipment is cursed, but an item called a "Magical Figurine of
X" can be cursed as well. Throw a cursed figurine and it becomes a hostile
monster and attacks you."


***** <EgoObjects>
=== Ego items and artifacts ===

Keine: "Some pieces of equipment are plainly more powerful than the ordinary
sort. These are the ego items and the artifacts."

--- Ego items (named goods) ---

Keine: "An ego item is a powerful piece of equipment made with magic laid on
it. If you are good at pseudo-identification it is judged "excellent".
Identify it and it carries an unusual name, such as "Long Sword of the Hero".
Wear one and you gain all manner of benefits -- a raised stat, a defence
against an element, and so on.
But there are cursed ego items as well. Put one on carelessly and you are in
serious trouble.
For the full details, *identify* it and look at it with the 'I' command."

--- Artifacts (legendary equipment) ---

Keine: "An artifact is a legendary item of which there is only one in the
world. If you are good at pseudo-identification it is judged "special".
Artifacts are mostly stronger than ego items and have more varied effects.
Identify one and it carries a star at the head of its name, as in
"*Long Sword 'Sword of Hisou'" or "*Bishamonten's Pagoda".
These have all but fixed properties and are called "fixed artifacts".
Some are rare enough that you may never lay eyes on one in a lifetime.

There are also "random artifacts".
These carry a different mark at the head of the name, and what they can do is
wholly unknown until you *identify* them.
You may just find one stronger than the strongest fixed artifact.

Artifacts are never destroyed by an enemy's attack.
But sell one to a shop, or leave one lying on the ground and forget it, and it
is gone for good.

Note that there are cursed artifacts too.
Put one on carelessly and you really are in serious trouble."


***** <DestroyItem>
=== Destroying items ===

Keine: "Items lying on the floor can be destroyed when magic or a breath
washes over them.
Staves and scrolls burn in fire and dissolve in acid.
Swords and armour dissolve in acid.
Rings and wands are broken by lightning.
Potions shatter under cold, force or shards.
And attacks of chaos, disenchantment, mana or disintegration destroy anything
at all. Attacks of warping and of pollution can destroy anything too.

The items in your pack can be destroyed by an enemy's attack in the same way.
Unlike items on the floor, the chance is a few per cent.
Still, if you carry dozens of scrolls, one blast of fire may burn several.
Destruction of your possessions by an element -- acid, lightning, fire, cold --
is prevented by double resistance to that element, or by immunity.
Immunity is a special piece of equipment that takes no damage at all from the
element; double resistance means having both a resistance from equipment and a
temporary resistance from a spell or potion at the same time.
Destruction by a higher element is usually prevented by the matching
resistance. There is no resistance to disintegration or mana, but be assured
that these elements do not destroy your possessions.

An item with the element-proof property is protected from destruction by that
element. And an artifact is never destroyed.
If a robe survives where a breath of acid burst, that robe is an artifact or
something excellent.

The equipment you are wearing is never destroyed by an enemy's attack.
Its bonuses can be lowered by acid or by disenchantment, however.
Disenchantment is prevented by equipment with resistance to disenchantment,
and corrosion by acid by equipment with immunity to acid.
An artifact is never harmed by acid.

Finally, some enemies pick up or destroy the items at their feet as they walk.
An item that was picked up is yours again once you kill the thief.
An artifact is never picked up and never destroyed."

***** <DestroyItem2>
=== Items that vanish ===

Keine: "Items can vanish without being destroyed.
I said a moment ago that artifacts are never destroyed; they can still vanish,
so take care. This is when it happens.

- When you leave items on the floor and go to another dungeon
	Besides moving between dungeons by recall or by a staircase, every item
	on the floor vanishes when you walk to the next map on the surface or
	switch to the wilderness map.
        Do not make the mistake of meaning to put items in your home and droppin
        g
	them in the doorway instead, only to lose them when you change maps.

- When an earthquake catches them
	When an earthquake strikes inside a dungeon and ground becomes wall, the
	items that were there vanish.
	Ground with an artifact on it never becomes wall, however.

- When they are *Destroyed*
	*Destruction* is a powerful spell that wrecks the dungeon.
	Use it and every item within range vanishes -- artifacts included.

- When you drop an item where items cannot lie
        Items cannot lie on a wall, on deep water or lava, on a closed door, on
        a
	trap or on a rune.
	Drop one in such a place and it rolls to a nearby grid where items can
	lie. But if there is nowhere near it where items can lie, the item
	vanishes.
	The same holds for a thrown weapon and for what a slain enemy drops.
        Bear it in mind when you kill an aquatic enemy or an enemy that casts tr
        ap
	magic.

What follows is less about play than about the system -- unusual ways in which
items vanish. It may be hard to understand, but I should like you to remember
it if you can.

- When the map is redrawn by quest processing
	When you take on a quest in a town, or report its completion, the map is
	rewritten in an instant.
	Items left on the floor then vanish.
	If a quest reward is left on the floor and you take on the next quest
	before picking it up, the reward is gone.

- When you leave an identified fixed artifact on the floor and change floors
	Moving between dungeons clears the floor of items, but merely taking a
	staircase to another floor of the same dungeon does not.
	Identified fixed artifacts are the one exception.
        Leave a fixed artifact on the floor, go down the stairs and come back, a
        nd
	somehow the artifact is gone.
	This is an unnatural behaviour that comes from the system. Take care.
	(From v1.0.75 it probably no longer vanishes.)

- When the floor's item data is compressed
	Kill so many enemies that the floor is covered with items and the
	worthless ones furthest away begin to vanish, one after another."


***** <Object_param>
=== How equipment is written down (the basics) ===

Keine: "Suppose you have found a piece of equipment and identified or
*identified* it. Seeing the writing on it for the first time, you will make
nothing of it. Let me explain how to read it.

A melee weapon first.

	Claymore (2d8) (+4,+3)

Claymore is the name of the weapon.
The (2d8) that follows is its base damage, meaning "roll two eight-sided dice,
each 1 to 8". You may wonder why dice appear out of nowhere; it is because the
system is inherited straight from the venerable tabletop role-playing games.

The average of the dice is (sides + 1) * number / 2.
For 2d8 the average is 9. You learned that at the village school, did you not?
Next, the (+4,+3) means (bonus to hit, bonus to damage).
So attacking with this sword adds +4 to the to-hit calculation, and a hit does
12 points of damage on average.
Note that this does not mean your chance to hit rises by 4%; the to-hit
calculation is quite involved and I shall not go into it here.

Next, a missile weapon.

	Long Bow (x3) (+4,+5)
	Steel Bolt (3d4) (+6,+4) (49/98)

The (x3) is the strength of the bow, the multiplier applied to the missile's
damage. The higher the better.
The (49/98) part appears only when you wield a launcher that matches the
missile. The 49 on the left is the damage per shot.
The average of (3d4) is 7.5; adding the damage bonuses of bow and bolt gives
16.5, and the x3 multiplier makes 49.
The 98 on the right is the expected damage per turn. A bow can fire several
times in a turn. Shots per turn are explained in the chapter on combat.

Next, armour.

	Partial Plate Armour (-3) [21,+7]

The (-3) is short for (-3,0). It means -3 to your to-hit calculation when you
attack. Heavy armour gets in the way of movement.
The [21,+7] is the armour class: 21 is this armour's base value and 7 the
bonus of a good piece.
The higher your armour class, the more easily you avoid a monster's melee
attacks. A high armour class also reduces the damage of a hit to some degree.
It does not reduce the damage of a melee attack with an element attached, such
as one that burns or poisons.

Next, a fine piece of armour.

	Oni's Great Armour (-3) [36,+18] (+3) {StAcElFiCoWa(St}

After the armour class bonus you can see (+3).
That is a bonus not to hit or damage but to a stat.
Wearing this armour raises your strength by 3.
Wearing it also grants resistances and abilities.
What they are is written inside the braces; how to read that string I shall
explain a little further on."


***** <Magic_weapon>
=== The special effects of weapons ===

Keine: "If you are a complete newcomer you may leave what follows for a while.
Come back once you are a little used to adventuring.

From here I explain the special effects of equipment in turn.
What is in brackets is the text written automatically inside the braces when
you *identify* the item.
Weapons first.

- Weapon brands (|A |E |F |Co |P)
  There are five brands: acid, lightning, fire, cold and poison.
  Attack with a weapon carrying one of these and, if the enemy has no
  resistance to that element, you do much more damage.

  In detail, the dice part of the weapon is multiplied by 2.5.
  Attacking with a (3d5)(0,+10){|F} weapon, for instance, does 19 points on
  average to an enemy that resists fire, and 32.5 points to one that does not.

- Slays (/* /C /Z /p /L /D /S /U /K, or /X followed by the same letter)
  A slay is just that -- "a sword that slays such-and-such".
  Attack with a weapon carrying a slay and, if the enemy belongs to that
  class, the damage rises.

  /* is x2 against the forces of chaos,
  /C is x2 against the forces of order,
  /Z is x2.5 against animals,
  /p is x2.5 against humans and x2 against humanoids,
  /L is x3 against the undead,
  /D is x3 against dragons,
  /S is x3 against deities,
  /U is x3 against demons,
  /K is x3 against youkai
  -- that is, the damage of the weapon's dice rises so far.
  There are also weapons that do even fiercer damage, written /Xp and the like.

  When several brands and slays apply to one attack, only the highest
  multiplier is used. They do not, sadly, all multiply together.

- Vorpal (|S, or |XS)
  A keen-edged weapon. It is only put on weapons with a blade.
  Attack with one and it cuts sharply, sometimes for extra damage.
  The exact calculation is a little involved, but on average the dice are
  multiplied by 1.22.
  Very rarely you see a |XS weapon of tremendous keenness, whose dice are
  multiplied by 1.67 on average.
  The vorpal multiplier is applied on top of the brand or slay multiplier.

- Force (|M)
  A weapon that draws on the wielder's own mind for tremendous damage.
  Attack with one and the dice are multiplied by (brand/slay multiplier * 1.5
  + 2). This too stacks with the brand or slay multiplier and with vorpal.
  A force weapon spends MP with every attack, however.
  Once your MP runs out it is no different from an ordinary weapon.

  So a weapon's dice come to
  base dice x (highest brand/slay multiplier) x force x vorpal.
  A little tangled, so let me give examples.
  |M alone gives x3.5 dice;
  a /K|M weapon striking a youkai gives x6.5 dice;
  and a /XD|M|XS weapon cutting down a dragon gives x16 dice!

- Impact (|Q)
  A weapon that causes an earthquake when it strikes. It is only put on
  weapons without a blade.
  When an earthquake strikes, the dungeon collapses and the terrain changes,
  and falling rock can damage the enemy.
  But holes open in the walls and other enemies come to join in, and items
  scattered on the ground get buried.
  Honestly, it is not very convenient.

- Chaotic (|Ca)
  A sinister weapon steeped in the power of chaos.
  Do not confuse it with /*, which strikes at the forces of chaos.
  Attack with one and all sorts of strange things happen: it drains HP from
  the enemy, confuses the enemy, teleports the enemy, transforms the enemy, or
  causes an earthquake.
  The first two are all very well; the last three are honestly unwelcome.

- Vampiric (|V)
  A weapon that thirsts for blood.
  Attack a living creature with it and you drain a little HP.
  It goes straight to how long you can keep fighting, so it is genuinely
  useful. From the middle depths, though, demons and undead grow common and it
  sees less use.

- Good for throwing (Th)
  Perhaps the shape suits throwing, or perhaps such magic is laid on it.
  Either way, throwing this weapon does more damage than throwing an ordinary
  one. Throwing an artifact that is good for throwing doubles the damage
  again.

- Returns when thrown (C+)
  Some weapons come back to your hand after you throw them. Thor's great
  hammer is the famous one.
  It does not always come back, and you can fail to catch what does and drop
  it at your feet. The nimbler you are, the less often you fail.

So much for melee weapons; now the missile weapons.

- Extra might (Xm)
  Missiles fired from this bow or crossbow do more damage.
  It does not double it: the weapon's multiplier (x3 and so on) rises by one.

- Extra shots (Xs)
  You can loose missiles quickly from this bow or crossbow.
  It raises your damage and lets you fight steadily, so it is useful.

Those are the special effects that go on weapons."


***** <Resist>
=== Resistances to elemental attacks ===

Keine: "Next, the resistances to the various elemental attacks.
Some races and classes have a few resistances of their own, but you will gain
most of them from equipment.

- resist acid (Ac), immunity to acid (*Ac)
- resist lightning (El), immunity to lightning (*El)
- resist fire (Fi), immunity to fire (*Fi)
- resist cold (Co), immunity to cold (*Co)

  Acid, lightning, fire and cold together are called the elements, and these
  resistances each cut the damage of that element to one third.

  If you wear equipment with immunity, that element does no damage at all and
  cannot destroy anything in your pack.

  Elemental attacks generally do a great deal of damage.
  The fire breath of a Lava Wyrm, for instance, does as much as 1600 without
  resistance.
  You want fire at the very least by floor 20, and every element by floor 35.

  This matters, so remember it well: for the elements and for poison, a
  resistance from equipment can be laid over a temporary resistance from a
  potion or the like to cut the damage to one ninth.
  This is called double resistance.
  Piling up several resistances from equipment does nothing; it must be
  combined with a temporary resistance.


The elements below are usually called the higher elements.
As a rule, do not fight an enemy that attacks with an element you are not
prepared for. You can learn what techniques an enemy has by "probing", so
until you are used to adventuring, carry a Staff or Rod of Probing.
The Hakurou-Tengu, the Maid and the Ranger have probing as an ability.

- resist poison (Po)
  Cuts the damage of poison attacks to one third, and prevents being poisoned.
  Rather harder to fill than the elemental resistances, but you want it by
  floor 40 all the same.
  As I said, poison too can be taken to one ninth by double resistance.

- resist light (Li)
  Cuts the damage of light attacks to 4/(7+1d4).
  It also prevents being blinded by a light attack.
  Gensoband has more enemies that attack with light than Hengband does.
  From the middle depths, if it is not filled, beware shrine maidens and
  magicians.

- resist dark (Dk)
  Cuts the damage of dark attacks to 4/(7+1d4).
  It also prevents being blinded by a dark attack, and reduces some curse
  attacks.
  Not many enemies use dark, but those that do are mostly dangerous.

- resist shards (Sh)
  Cuts the damage of shard attacks to 6/(7+1d4).
  It also halves the damage of rocket attacks.
  And it prevents the cuts a shard attack inflicts.
  Almost useless early on, but you must fill it deep down.

- resist sound (So)
  Cuts the damage of sound attacks to 5/(7+1d4).
  It also prevents the stunning from sound, rockets, extreme cold and so on.
  Better to have than not, but not a high priority.

- resist nether (Nt)
  Cuts the damage of hell attacks to 6/(7+1d4).
  It also reduces some curse attacks, and reduces the experience drained by
  draining attacks.
  Demons and undead use hell often, so fill it by the middle depths.

- resist chaos (Ca)
  Cuts the damage of chaos attacks to 6/(7+1d4).
  It also prevents mutation from a chaos attack, and prevents hallucination
  from all sorts of causes.
  Another you want filled at all times from the middle depths.

- resist water (Wa)
  Halves the damage of water attacks.
  It also prevents the stunning, confusion and item destruction they cause.
  And it stops you drowning when you enter water heavily laden.
  Useful early on; late in the game you may manage without it.

- resist disenchantment (Di)
  Cuts the damage of disenchantment attacks to 6/(7+1d4).
  It also prevents your equipment being disenchanted.
  Not essential, but without it you must be quite careful from the middle
  depths.

- resist holy (Ho)
  Halves the damage of evil-breaking attacks.
  Enemies with such attacks appear often from beginning to end, so if you are
  a youkai, a necromancer or anything else weak to them, fill this as early as
  you can. Equipment with it is rather uncommon, mind.

- resist time and space (Ti)
  Prevents an enemy's Teleport Away a certain proportion of the time.
  Holds gravity damage to 2/3 and prevents its teleporting and item
  destruction.
  Holds time-reversal damage to 4/(7+1d4) and prevents its stat and experience
  loss.
  Holds warping damage to 4/(7+1d4) and prevents its stat loss and teleporting.
  Holds causality damage to 6/(7+1d4) and prevents its various extra effects.
  A useful resistance that stops many attacks.
  Equipment with it is quite uncommon, though.
  Happily, without it the attacks other than causality do no great damage.

What follows is a little different from a resistance: these prevent particular
bad status effects.

- free action (Fa)
  Prevents paralysis and sleep.
  Paralysis or sleep means several turns of being attacked without reply.
  That amounts to a game over.
  Fill it by floor 20 whatever it takes, and never leave it open for a moment.

- resist fear (Fe)
  Prevents becoming afraid.
  While afraid you cannot attack an enemy directly, and your shooting and
  spells fail more often.
  Even without the resistance, liquor, a spell or a potion that raises your
  morale prevents fear.

- resist blindness (Bl)
  Prevents becoming blind.
  While blind you cannot see the terrain or the enemies, and you cannot use
  spellbooks or scrolls.
  A Potion of Cure Serious Wounds and the like cure it at once, but a single
  turn's delay can put you in danger.

- resist confusion (Co)
  Prevents becoming confused.
  While confused you cannot walk where you mean to, and you cannot use spells
  or scrolls; magic devices fail almost every time.
  This too is cured at once by a Potion of Cure Serious Wounds, but a good many
  enemies inflict confusion with their ordinary attacks.
  Standing next to one of those without the resistance is extremely dangerous.
  Fill it by the middle depths.

- resist insanity (In)
  A resistance to meeting the loathsome beings that bring cosmic horror.
  Go deep without it and your mind will be wrecked again and again.
  Some enemies have fierce mind attacks that nothing but this resistance
  reduces.
  If your brain is damaged, Eientei will cure it.
  Awkwardly, equipment with this resistance is rather uncommon.


That is about all.
I say again: as a rule, do not fight an enemy that attacks with an element you
are not prepared for."


***** <Pval>
=== Effects that raise your abilities ===

Keine: "Next, the effects that raise a parameter.
For what each parameter does, remember the explanation at character creation.

- strength (St)
- intelligence (In)
- wisdom (Wi)
- dexterity (Dx)
- constitution (Cn)
- charisma (Ch)
  Bonuses to the base parameters. Constitution matters particularly.

- stealth (Sl)
  A bonus to stealth.
  At about "heroic" it becomes far easier to enter a vault.

- speed (Sp)
  A bonus to how fast you act.
  You will not gather much early on, but deep down you want +20 in total.

- magic device (Md)
  A bonus to using magic devices.
  A pure warrior class can hardly use a powerful device without this.

- digging (Dg)
  Of little use except for a pick early on.
  It goes only on weapons.

- infravision (If)
  The range at which you sense warm-blooded creatures.
  Useful enough early on; from the middle depths you should have a better
  means of detection.

- searching (Sr)
  A bonus to searching. It finds traps and secret doors and senses the feeling
  of a floor.
  In this variant it also affects the rate at which you find materials, so for
  some classes it matters a good deal.

- extra blows (At)
  You get more melee blows.
  It goes, as a rule, only on weapons and rings.

- magic defence (Sv)
  A bonus to magic defence.
  With it high, you more often turn aside curses and mind attacks entirely.

- disarming (Da)
  A bonus to disarming traps.

That is about all for the parameters."

***** <Misc>
=== The other effects ===

Keine: "Well, we have covered weapon effects, resistances and parameters.
Let me finish with a few effects of equipment that belong to none of them.

- telepathy (~Tele)
  A useful means of detection that senses the enemies around you at all times.
  It cannot sense the lifeless, or beings without an ordinary mind.

- ESP (~Evil ~Good ~Z ~p ~L ~D ~o ~U ~T ~Nolv ~Uniq)
  It senses at all times the beings that belong to a particular class.
  "Nolv" means the lifeless and "Uniq" means unique monsters.

- sustain ( (St (In (Wi (Dx (Cn (Ch )
  An enemy's attack and various other causes can lower a stat temporarily.
  These prevent it, each for its own stat.

- reduce spell difficulty (Es)
- reduce mana cost (Dm)
  They lower a spell's difficulty or its MP cost a little.
  A caster is much easier with one or the other.

- reflection (Rf)
  It turns an enemy's bolt spells aside or sends them back.
  It fails now and then, so do not rely on it too far.
  In this variant, with so many enemies loosing danmaku, it is quite useful --
  but much harder to come by early on than it used to be.

- levitation (Lv)
  With this you can fly.
  You cross water, pits, mountains and trapdoors, and gravity and whirlwind
  attacks do less.
  Never travel to the Tengu Village or the Former Hell Road without it.

- see invisible (Si)
  You can see invisible enemies.
  You want it by about floor 20 if you can.

- regeneration (Rg)
  Your natural HP and MP recovery is twice as fast.
  Useful, but you also grow hungry faster, so mind it on a long journey.

- slow digestion (Sd)
  You grow hungry more slowly.
  If you are in trouble without it, plan your outings a little better.

- light source (Lu)
  Equipment with this shines brightly and adds 1 to the radius you light.

- warning (Wr)
  With this, when there is a trap where you are about to step, or a chance of
  heavy damage where you are about to step, it trembles and warns you.
  Convenient, since you need not keep detecting traps.
  Note that cancelling an action on a warning still uses a very little time.

- blessed (Bs)
  Equipment with this cannot be cursed.
  If you are a Shrine Maiden or a Priest it may bear on what you may wield.
  For a Paladin it gives a bonus to damage.

- teleport (Te)
  Wearing equipment with this lets you follow an adjacent enemy that teleports.
  It is not to be relied on.
  But if this equipment is cursed you begin teleporting of your own accord.

- two weapons (To)
  It goes only on arm equipment. Wearing it reduces the to-hit penalty for
  fighting with a weapon in each hand.

- fast movement (Sp)
  Wearing this raises your movement speed.
  It can double it at high levels.
  Useful, but rather hard to come by.

- auras ( [F [C [E )
  With this, when an enemy hits you in melee it takes a little elemental
  damage in return. Small comfort against a formidable enemy.
  A fire aura also adds 1 to your light radius.

- anti-magic ([M)
  Wearing equipment with this raises your magic defence enormously, but you
  can no longer cast.
  Not only spellbooks but many spell-like abilities become unusable.
  Nobody but a pure warrior would normally use it, but bear in mind that high
  magic defence prevents the forced paralysis of "ancient malice".

- anti-teleport ([T)
  Wearing equipment with this makes teleportation impossible.
  An enemy's teleport magic can no longer move you, but the drawback is far
  too great.

That is about all.
Finding a combination of equipment that satisfies both your resistances and
your parameters is the very heart of this game.
"The resistance puzzle" is a fair name for it."


***** <Inscriptions>
=== Inscriptions ===

Keine: "You can inscribe an item with the '{' command.
You may type any text you like -- a memorandum, in effect.
But a memorandum is not all it does.
Inscribe an item in a special form and you can make the various actions of
your adventure far more efficient.

From here on it is much the same as Hengband proper, so I borrow Hengband's
help. Skip it if you do not follow it.
Come back once you are used to adventuring and want to explore more
efficiently."


-----------------------what follows is quoted---------------------------

You may add text to any item at all with the "inscribe" command.
There is no limit on the length of an inscription, though a very long one may
not fit on the screen. The inscription is shown after the item's name, wrapped
in { }.
An inscription belongs to the particular item (or bundle) you inscribe; it is
not written automatically on every item of the same kind (there are two
options concerning this -- see them).

In certain circumstances the game inscribes a "fake" inscription on an item at
a suitable moment. "empty" means the item has no magic left in it.
"tried" is inscribed on an item you have used but whose effect you did not
learn. An item known to be cursed is inscribed "cursed". A broken item is
inscribed "broken". And an item bought at a discount in a shop -- meaning it
may have something slightly wrong with it -- carries its discount, as in
"25% off".

Besides those, Hengband often inscribes the "impression" your character has
formed of an item's quality. Every character gains an impression of a
particular item by carrying it for a while.


--- Useful inscriptions ---

Some players use inscriptions to record where and how they found an item, or
to note the properties an item has. Hengband can examine an item's properties
and inscribe them for you automatically.

Besides that, some inscriptions carry a special meaning of their own.
Many of Hengband's commands ask you to name an item from your pack: the quaff
('q') command asks which potion to drink, the use ('u') command asks which
staff to use. Inscriptions are a powerful tool for saving that labour.


----------------------------------------------------
Automatic property inscription '%'
----------------------------------------------------

Inscribe {%} with the '{' command, or by automatic inscription from the
auto-picker settings, and the inscription is at once replaced by a short
abbreviated string describing the item's properties.
Inscribe '%' on a random artifact with resistance to fire and poison and a
bonus to strength, for instance, and it reads {St;FiPo}.
From then on you can see the artifact's resistances at a glance without using
the 'I' command.
Naturally, if you do not know the item's properties -- if you have not
*identified* it -- inscribing '%' does nothing.

For a fixed artifact or an ego item the name tells you its resistances (if you
remember them!), so only the resistances and abilities added at random are
inscribed. Boots of Levitation with an added resistance to hell, for instance,
are simply inscribed {Nt}; the levitation is not written.
If you do not care for that, inscribe '%all' instead and the added properties
and the original ones are all inscribed without distinction. The boots above
then read {Nt;Lv}.

In the Japanese game, inscribing '%%' instead of '%' (or '%%all' instead of
'%all') writes the properties in two roman letters rather than single kanji.
In English the roman letters are used either way.

----------------------------------------------------
Inscriptions of the form {"@" + command letter + tag}
----------------------------------------------------

Inscriptions of the form

"@" + command letter + tag

such as {@f0} or {@ma}, are probably the most used of all. The command letter
is the single letter of a command; the tag is a digit, or any letter, used to
name the item. While running the command named by the command letter, press
the tag key and the game searches your pack for an item inscribed
{"@" + command letter + tag} and runs the command on it.
Inscribe {@q1} on a Potion of Healing, for instance, and pressing "q1" drinks
it. An item's label (the single letter from 'a' to 'w') changes whenever items
leave or join your pack, so being able to choose an item regardless of its
label is a great convenience.

Note 1: the command letter in an inscription must be the letter of the
original keyset. Even if you have chosen the roguelike keyset, the inscription
must use the original keyset's letter. The command to fire a bow is 't' in the
roguelike keyset, but inscribing {@t0} on your arrows will not let you choose
them with the digit '0'. You must inscribe {@f0}. Inconvenient -- but it does
mean that changing your keyset partway does not affect your item selections or
your macros.

Note 2: using a letter as the tag means the item that originally had that
letter as its label can no longer be chosen. I recommend using letters only
for particular purposes, such as fixing the labels of your spellbooks.

You may inscribe the same tag on several items. In that case, when you choose
the digit during the named command, the command runs only on the first item
found in the search, not on every item with that inscription.

You may also put several such inscriptions on one item. A spellbook inscribed
{@ma@ba@Ga} answers all three of the commands that take a spellbook: cast
('m'), browse ('b') and gain ('G'). You may also give one command several
tags, inscribing {@E1@E2} on food, for instance.

Example 1:
    Suppose you carry a main weapon and a shovel. Usually you wield the
    weapon, but you use the shovel when digging out treasure. Inscribe {@w0}
    on the main weapon and {@w1} on the shovel, and typing "w1" wields the
    shovel and "w0" wields the main weapon again, wherever they are in your
    pack.

    Note: you may instead inscribe {@w0} on both and always type "w0" to swap.

Example 2:
    To cast the first spell of the first spellbook in your pack you normally
    type "maa", and for the first spell of the second book, "mba". But if the
    first book is stolen or destroyed the labels shift, and "mba" no longer
    casts the spell you wanted. Miscast spells often come from this. To
    prevent it, inscribe {@ma} on the first book and {@mb} on the second and
    no mistake can happen. Losing another book will not make you cast the
    wrong spell.

Example 3:
    Suppose you carry rations of food and Elvish waybread. Normally you want
    to eat the food first and keep the waybread's healing for an emergency.
    Inscribe {@E1} on the food and {@E1@E2} on the waybread. The food comes
    above the waybread in the pack, so pressing "E1" eats the food first and
    only eats the waybread when the food is gone. When you deliberately want
    the waybread, type "E2". You can do the same with healing potions.

----------------------------------------------------
Inscriptions of the form {"@" + tag digit}
----------------------------------------------------

An inscription of the form {"@" + tag digit} (the digit being 0 to 9) is like
{"@" + command letter + tag}, but works for every command. Only digits may be
used as the tag.

----------------------------------------------------
The inscription {!x}
----------------------------------------------------
An item inscribed {!x} (x being any command) always asks you to confirm when
it is selected. An item inscribed {!f!k!d}, for instance, is very unlikely to
be lost by accident, because throwing ('f'), destroying ('k') and dropping
('d') it all ask whether you really mean it.

And an item inscribed "!*" asks for confirmation on selection for every
command, so you may be as careful with it as you please. Note that "selling"
and "dropping" both use the "d" command, so {!d} guards against both.

----------------------------------------------------
The inscription {^x}
----------------------------------------------------
Inscribe {^x} (x being any command) on a piece of equipment you are wearing
and you are asked to confirm when you use that command. The common example is
inscribing {^<^>} on your boots, which makes you confirm before going up or
down a staircase.

----------------------------------------------------
The inscription {=g}
----------------------------------------------------
An item inscribed {=g} is picked up automatically when you step on it.
This takes priority over every auto-pickup option. Its main purpose is to be
inscribed on arrows and crossbow bolts so that they are easily gathered again
when you have fired them across the floor.


----------------------------------------------------
The inscription {#name} (a fake artifact)
----------------------------------------------------

An inscription is normally shown wrapped in { }, so that it is plain at a
glance that the user wrote it. Sometimes, though, you want to give an item you
are fond of a name of your own, as though it were an artifact.
Then put a '#' in the inscription and write your name after it.
Inscribe "#Masamune" on a "Katana of Slaying (4d4) (+9,+9)", for instance, and
the game shows "Katana of Slaying 'Masamune' (4d4) (+9,+9)".
This form of inscription has no effect on a real artifact, which already has a
name.

----------------------------------------------------
The inscription {!!}
----------------------------------------------------

Inscribe {!!} on a rod or on an artifact that can be activated, and a message
tells you when the item has finished recharging and can be used again.
