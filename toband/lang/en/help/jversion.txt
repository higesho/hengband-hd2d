     TOband2 Version History

2004/09/?? TOband2-0.0.1pre
  Skills now rise automatically when you gain a class level.
  Various item changes.
  Various bug fixes.

2003/01/?? TOband0.0.3fix99
  Levels are now split into a race level and a class level.
  Skill points are no longer lost completely on a class change; they are now
    accumulated as an independent value for each class. They cannot be carried
    over to another class, however. On reincarnation, the points of all classes
    are reset to 0.
  The character dump now lists the classes you have experienced.

  Weakened the racial powers "Throw Pebble" and "Throw Grave".
  The Pumpkinhead racial power "Pumpkin Wars" is no longer subject to skills.
  Adjusted the pet-keeping skill.
  Fixed the description of the Wind magic spell "Deadshot", which gave the
    wrong element.
  Fixed a bug where open/broken doors were not destroyed by the Magery magic
    spell "Trap & Door Destruction".
  Various other fixes.
2003/02/01 TOband0.0.3fix10
  Improved the handling of unfavored weapons for Ninjas and Swordmasters.
  Made it easier for Ninjas to raise their Rapier and Small Sword skills.
2003/02/01 TOband0.0.3fix9
  Weapon skill levels for direct attack can now be raised at the Fighters Hall.
  Bow skill levels can now be raised at the Archers Guild.
  Unified the base purchase limit of the General Store to $1000, and that of

  The purchase limits of the stores in the Royal City of Heim are affected by
    the Chaos Frame.

  The Earth magic spell "Earth spike" now cancels levitation including that of
    levitating pets.
  Classes that cannot cast spells now gain twice as much SP from experience.
  Strengthened fixed artifact weapons across the board.
  Wearing the Dark Magic Device set grants immunity to darkness and nether.
  Added the skill/special move categories as auto-pickup keywords.
  The auto-pickup keyword "missile weapons" now stands for all weapons that go
    in the shooting slot.
  A confirmation is now asked when you accept a quest.
  Fixed a bug where, when a hostile monster was inside a wall, the check for
    the range that does not catch the player was wrong, so that the player was
    caught in a pet's ball spell.
  Fixed a bug where Mermaids and Divine Knights did not stop singing when they
    cast a spell.
  Fixed a bug where, with easy_floor enabled and a full inventory, automatic
    identification of the item underfoot did not work. In that case,
    inscriptions and the like use the rules for unidentified items.
  Fixed the learning of the player's resistances by each monster.
  Fixed a bug in the monster spell "Summon Spirit".
  Various other fixes.
2003/01/25 TOband0.0.3fix8

2002/12/29 TOband0.0.3
  Abolished the fixed values for weapons and skills and changed to a skill
    point/skill level system. Magic was also changed to a magic skill
    point/skill level system.
    - A character starts with all skills at novice.
    - Skill points and magic skill points are gained by leveling up or by
      gaining experience.
    - Classes that cannot cast spells do not gain magic skill points.
    - Unlike the former fixed values, skills affect not only the to-hit rate
      but also damage and the number of attacks.
    - Magic skills affect the energy cost of casting, the failure rate and the
      MP cost.
    - Skill points and magic skill points are lost on a class change, but skill
      levels remain forever.
    - For carried-over data, skill points/magic skill points are calculated
      from the current maximum experience and the classes you have experienced.
  Added the new class "White Knight".
  Once you change class to either the Templar or the White Knight, you can no
    longer change class to the other.
  Added the Scroll of Doppelganger.
  Merged in the code of Hengband 1.5.1. However, the change that makes Rations
    (Elvish Waybread) equivalent to satisfy hunger and sells them in stores is
    not adopted.
  Greatly strengthened bare-handed attacks. They become even stronger when you
    remove your shield.
  The special move selection menu of the Templar now shows
    level/cost/weapon/description.
  In the information about yourself, the descriptions of special moves learned
    from Scrolls of Special Moves and as a Templar are now shown, in addition
    to race and so on.
  The number of bare-handed attacks while riding is limited to 1.
  Deleted the auto-pickup editor.
  Unholy/blessed status that does not come from the base item, ego or artifact
    level is removed by Scrolls of Bless Weapon/Unholy Weapon.
  Adjusted artifacts.
  Fixes related to fluctuations of the Chaos Frame.
  Various other fixes.

---------------
Version History
---------------

2002/12/24 Ver. 1.5.1 (Development branch)
  Added the visual editor 'v' to the lists of known ~. (From Unangband.)
  Fixed the Bard's Clairaudience so that it does not detect veins.

  Bug fixes:
    - Monsters wake up when you go up and down stairs.
    - A bug in error handling at save time.
    - The save command corrupts monster data, leading to a crash.
    - The identity of a mysterious monster is revealed after saving.
    - Climbing out of an up-going mine lands on a floor shallower than the top
      floor.

  Plus all the changes of 1.4.1.

2002/12/24 Ver. 1.4.1 (Stable branch)
  Elvish Waybread now nourishes as much as Satisfy Hunger. It can be bought at
    the General Store.
  Salt water has no effect on undead and the like.
  Received descriptions of weapons, armor and other items from OAngband. The
    translation is still in progress.
  When an item inscribed {!!} is recharged, the game now disturbs you.
  Improved the display of tiles in the info command and the '%' command in
    Bigtile mode.

  Bug fixes:
    - Vault with no entrance. Arena while in wraithform. Crash at (a) of the
      info command.
    - Smith's items could not be activated. Special foods while shapechanged.
      Monsters appearing in the Mountain.
    - Name of the "Staff" in the English version. Random teleport in the
      monster arena.

2002/12/09 Ver. 1.5.0 (Development branch)

  Implemented the saved-floors specification
    - If you go back along the stairs you came by in a dungeon, you return to
      exactly the same floor.
    - When you return to the surface, all saved floors disappear.
    - Level Teleport / Trap Doors / the stair creation spell changed. (Each now
      leads only to a specific floor directly above or below.)
    - Abolished the dungeon_stairs and confirm_stairs options.
    - Added the confirm_quest option, which warns about one-way stairs.
    - Increased the probability that treasure is buried in lava walls and
      quartz walls.
    - Added walls containing treasure that cannot be seen until you detect
      treasure or actually dig.
    - Removed the ability to detect treasure inside walls from Detection. (Too
      many were detected, which was annoying.)

2002/12/09 Ver. 1.4.0 (Stable branch)

  Abolished the Scroll of Satisfy Hunger and the Berserker's
  Satisfy Hunger racial power
    - Androids can drink flasks of oil.
    - Balrogs can sacrifice corpses of 't', 'p' and 'h' with the Eat command.
    - Golems, Zombies, Skeletons and Spectres can eat the charges of staffs or
      wands.
    - Ents can get enough nourishment from the water in potions. Potions of
      Water can be bought at the General Store.

  Ported the lists of known monsters/objects of Eyangband. (Thanks to So-Miya.)
  In the look command and when selecting a target, the shift key moves the
    cursor quickly.
  When easy_floor is on, made piles of items on the floor easier to view in the
    look command.
  Introduced a service at the Weaponmaster that shows an evaluation of your AC.
  Set the auto-inscription for the magic device domination flag to {+Dev}{Md},
    as it had none.
  Fixed the HP bar so that only the mount's HP is not displayed in two columns.

  Options added/deleted
    - disturb_high added: disturb when a high-level monster moves
    - allow_debug_opts added: allow/forbid the use of debug/cheat options
    - The birth option munchkin_death became the cheat option cheat_save.
    - The birth options terrain_streams and stupid_monsters were abolished.

  Bug fixes:
  Unable to load with a large number of items, crash at the edge of the
    wilderness, stop time in a wilderness forest, hidden door in deep water,
    walkable walls, True Warding, runes not disintegrating, new traps not
    appearing, Blue-Mage unlearning, Amber resurrected by the Curse of Blood,
    mirror magic energy cost, multiple Chameleon Kings, a bug that disabled
    macros, etc.

  Corrected English text, typos and so on. (Thanks to John Mikula.)

2002/10/31 Ver. 1.3.1 (Development branch)
  Added a feature to the Windows version to keep sub-windows on top.
  The result of the resistance auto-inscription {%} now changes dynamically.
  Changed the weak pseudo-identification inscription from {good} to {uncursed}.
  Special trapped rooms: added the Piranha trap and the Armageddon trap.
  When a Rune of Protection or Rune of Explosion is broken, the original
    terrain returns.
  Undetected traps are not removed by earthquakes.
  Fixed a bug where a trapped pit was isolated inside walls.
  Abolished little boy and fat man. The data itself is to be deleted in a
    future version.
  New unique 'Sheer Heart Attack'.
  Changed how autoconf is configured, making acconfig.h unnecessary.
  Fixed a bug where Musou Sandan (Triple Strike) let you burrow into walls.
  Plus all the changes from 1.2.0 to 1.2.2 below.

2002/10/31 Ver. 1.2.2 (Stable branch)
  In the Cloning Pits, Borshin appears in place of little boy.
  Fixed it so that the Samurai's death poem remains in screenshots.
  As with ordinary light sources, the radius of light sources of darkness is
    now shown numerically when *identified*.
  Fixed it so that the light radius becomes 0 even with a fiery aura plus a
    light source of darkness.
  Fixed a bug where Rush Attack could cross Pattern terrain.
  Fixed many small bugs reported by the TOband team.
  Other bug fixes (an enemy unique of the same kind could appear while a unique

items on lines beginning with "!" are destroyed automatically. Items nearer the
top of the autopick file are now judged with higher priority. For example, if
you use the 'M' command to show only the items that will be picked up, and
several items overlap, the item at the top of the list is displayed, and
likewise for the item name display at the right edge. Also, if you write
!(empty)
!ransacked chest
chest
then empty chests and ransacked chests are destroyed, and all other chests are
picked up. Since the number of items to be destroyed grows in the late game, I
increased the maximum number of entries to 512 for pickup and destroy combined.

around that. :) I also made a Monk-only artifact (I changed the source quite a
lot, so it may disappear in a future upgrade of the English version), and the
'C' command now shows the Monk's average damage.

official English ZAngband. I intend to feed the improvements made in the

=== The History of Zangband ===

This document describes the history of Zangband. It summarizes only the main
events in the history of development, with comments from the previous authors
and maintainers. For the detailed development history, see z_update.txt.


=== Zangband 2.2.0 ===

This version introduced the expanded wilderness and fixed quests (from
Kangband), among other things. Towns, quests and the wilderness can easily be
changed by editing text files. The following review is by Zangband's original
author, Topi Ylinen.

--- Version 2.2.0 (by Topi Ylinen) ---

Zangband 2.2.0 is arguably the version that has changed the most so far. The
historical versions (Angband-- and Zangband 1.*) mainly changed monsters and
items, and versions 2.0.* to 2.1.* added new races and elements, but Zangband
2.2.0 changes the very foundation of the game dramatically.

The new concepts are taken from Kangband: the wilderness, new terrain, and
customizable quests and maps. Some people may object to these changes, saying
that randomness is reduced and more becomes predictable, but in fact nothing is
lost from the game by the new specification. Everything has been *added* to the
old system. The "random quests" of the popular Zangband 2.1.1 are still there,
although the floors on which they appear are no longer random.

That said, the pre-programmed parts are limited to maps and quests. At present
the wilderness map is relatively small and there are not many quests, so
players should get used to it quickly. Towns and quests can be added simply by
adding definition files to lib/edit, and no recompilation is needed, so players
of the new version can create their own quests and share them with all Zangband
players!

Besides the quests and the wilderness, Zangband 2.2.0 also adds new monsters
(nearly 300, some of them taken from Vanilla), several new weapons and armors,
and more than 52 new mutations. The mutations now total 98 kinds. In other
words, there will be plenty of new discoveries and experiences even for
Zangband veterans. To implement more than 850 monster races in total, the
16-bit memory management code had to be removed. Zangband 2.* does not support
16-bit code, so this has no effect.


Other, less conspicuous changes include improved pet AI and s-lang support. The
latter is not used yet, but it will make it possible to customize items,
monsters, quests and so on very powerfully. Every function can be called from
editable files, and no recompilation is needed.


=== Zangband 2.1.0d ===

This is the first version released after Robert Ruehlmann became the new
maintainer of Zangband. In his own words...

"ZAngband 2.1.0c was Topi's last version. He has got a job and doesn't have

=== Versions 1.0 - 2.1.0c ===

The following is a record of development by Topi Ylinen.

The seeds of Zangband lie in an obsolete and long ago vanished PC
variant (somewhat misleadingly) dubbed Angband--. The variant was
written by a hopeless Angband addict (previously Moria veteran and
winner) who got bored with the standard monsters and wanted to
introduce some new monsters. Angband-- was based on the PC Angband 1.31
sources, and it was set in Roger Zelazny's 'Amber' universe.

Later this individual got a better computer and learned to code, and
produced the PC Zangband, and most Angband-- monsters survived into
PC Zangband 1.0. PC Zangband 1.0 was the first PC Angband to introduce
(simple, font-based) graphics, which were also used in the graphical
PC Angband 1.40.

Yet this individual was still not cured of his addiction... his almost
as strong addiction to the Civilization style fantasy strategy game
'Master of Magic' inspired him to write a new magic system. The current
version of Zangband (2.*) incorporates this magic system, as well as
the best features from Angband-- and PC Zangband 1.0. It is based on
the Angband 2.8.1 sources (by Ben Harrison), and is therefore portable
to other systems (unlike the earlier versions which were for dos-pc's
only).

Incidentally, this person (me, Topi Ylinen) also thought that the
standard Angband monsters were too easy, which led him to introduce
such monsters as Death swords, Cyberdemons and Great wyrms of power...

=== Brief Version History (of standard Angband) ===

First came "VMS Moria", by Robert Alan Koeneke (1985).

Then came "Umoria" (Unix Moria), by James E. Wilson (1989).


You can find details of the history of "moria", the direct ancestor of Angband,
in various places, and this file also carries a message from its author, Robert
Alan Koeneke. Note that "moria" has been ported to various systems, has its own
newsgroup, and has its own fans.

In 1990, Alex Cutler and Andy Astrand, with the help of other students
at the University of Warwick, created Angband 1.0, based on the
existing code for Umoria 5.2.1. They wanted to expand the game, keeping
or even strengthening the grounding in Tolkien lore, while adding more
monsters and items, including unique monsters and artifact items, plus
activation, pseudo-sensing, level feelings, and special dungeon rooms.

Over time, Sean Marsh, Geoff Hill, Charles Teague, and others, worked
on the source, releasing a copy known as "Angband 2.4.frog_knows" at
some point, which ran only on Unix systems, but which was ported by
various people to various other systems.

Then Charles Swiger (cs4w+@andrew.cmu.edu) attempted to clean up the
mess, resulting in several versions, starting sometime around November,
1993, with Angband 2.5.1 (more or less) and leading up to Angband 2.6.2
in late 1994. Several people ported (the primarily Unix/NeXT centered)
Angband 2.6.1 to other platforms, including Keith Randall, who made a
Macintosh port that added support for color usage. Some of the changes
during this period were based on suggestions from the "net", PC Angband
1.40, UMoria 5.5, and some of the Angband "variations", such as
FAngband.

Angband 2.6.1 was written primarily for Unix/NeXT machines, and required the
use of low-level "curses" commands for all screen and keyboard manipulation.
Porting to a new system required fundamental changes to the source in every
version, and usually the creation of screen-handling source that behaved like a
"curses" emulator. Among the ports made that way was "Macintosh Angband 2.6.1"
by Keith Randall, which added support for color and formed the basis of the
first release of Angband 2.7.0.

In the latter half of 1994, I (Ben Harrison) was playing around with the
Angband source, mainly because I was considering whether I could build a kind
of automatic player for Angband, like the old "rogue-o-matic" program for the
game "rogue". The difficulty of compiling the Macintosh version and the
complexity of the source made that impossible, so I began tidying up the source
in various ways for my own use.

At the end of 1994, Charles Swiger announced that he could no longer support
Angband because he had taken a job. This caused considerable dismay in the
Angband community, represented by the Angband newsgroup, and various people
tried to set up a "committee" to continue supporting Angband. The committee
produced nothing but confusion (for example, someone suggested thinking about
COBOL), so on the first day of 1995, when I published my own source, called it
"Angband 2.7.0", and said that I would basically be the new maintainer of
Angband, there was almost no opposition. Or at least, that is how I felt.

Angband 2.7.0 was a very clean (though very buggy) rewrite of the source which,
among other things, made porting to many platforms, starting with the Macintosh
and UNIX, extremely simple. After spending a great deal of time on debugging,
it became Angband 2.7.2, which included X11 and various IBM machines. Angband
2.7.4 was released to the "ftp.cis.ksu.edu" site and quickly won popularity,
helped perhaps by the OS2, Windows, Amiga and Linux ports. Angband 2.7.5 and
2.7.6 added important features such as macros and user preference files, and
continued to tidy up the source. Angband 2.7.8 was designed to supply another
"stable" version that we can all give to our friends. In addition to new help
files and spoiler files for the online help, it added a variety of minor tweaks
and some new features.

After Angband 2.7.8 was released, I set up a web site to record all the changes
in each version (with a few omissions), and I became able to use a new
developer ftp server to support the Official Mirror Server. This web site now
permanently lives at the Official Angband Home Page (http://www.phial.com).
Unfortunately, the following six versions are numbered Angband 2.7.9v1 to
Angband 2.7.9v6, but in fact each of them was a major upgrade. Angband 2.8.0
and Angband 2.8.1 were released in an attempt to use ordinary version numbers.
Angband 2.8.2 and Angband 2.8.3 added a few random features, cleaned up the
source a little, supported graphics, and supported several new systems.

The Official Angband Home Page ("http://www.phial.com/angband/") provides not
only the latest news about Angband, but also lists of the changes between
versions, the changes planned for the next versions, and lists of various
Angband-related email addresses and web sites.


=== Some of the changes from Angband 2.6.1 to Angband 2.7.9 ===

It is very difficult to pin down what was changed, and when, between 2.6.1 and
2.7.8. Every version contained very many changes from the previous one, so many
that the diff files became longer than the source itself. With the exception of
the creation of new "main-xxx.c" files for the various platforms, and a few
other exceptions noted in the source, I wrote most of the changes myself. Some
were spontaneous, but most were made as a result of suggestions and comments
from Angband players.

The most important modification was a massive "code level cleanup" that
made all of my other modifications much simpler and safer. This cleanup
was so massive that in many places the code is no longer recognizable,
for example, via "diff -r", often because it was rewritten from
scratch.

The second most important modification was the design of a generic "z-term.c"
package, which allows Angband to be ported to a new machine with as few as 50
lines of code. Angband 2.7.9 thus runs without modification on many machines,
including Macintosh, PowerMac, Unix/X11, Unix/Curses, Amiga, Windows, OS2-386,
DOS-386, and even DOS-286.

It would be difficult to list all of the changes between Angband 2.6.1
and Angband 2.7.9, because many of them were made in passing during the
massive code level cleanup. Many of the changes are invisible to the
user, but still provide increased simplicity and efficiency, and
decreased code size, or make other more visable changes possible. For
example, the new "project()" code that handles all bolts, beams, and
balls, the new "update_view()" code that simplifies line of sight
computation, or the new "generate()" code that builds new levels in the
dungeon. Many changes have been made to increase efficiency, including
the new "process_monsters()" and "update_monsters()" functions, and
the new "objdes()" and "lite_spot()" routines. The generic "Term"
package yielded efficient screen updates, and enabled the efficient use
of "color".

But anyway, here are a few things that come to mind, in no particular
order, and with very little time or effort. Somehow I managed to put

=== A Letter from the Original Author ===

From: koeneke@ionet.net (Robert Alan Koeneke)
Newsgroups: rec.games.roguelike.angband,rec.games.roguelike.moria
Subject: Early history of Moria
Date: Wed, 21 Feb 1996 04:20:51 GMT

I had some email show up asking about the origin of Moria, and its
relation to Rogue.  So I thought I would just post some text on the
early days of Moria.

First of all, yes, I really am the Robert Koeneke who wrote the first
Moria.  I had a lot of mail accusing me of pulling their leg and
such.  I just recently connected to Internet (yes, I work for a
company in the dark ages where Internet is concerned) and
was real surprised to find Moria in the news groups...  Angband was an
even bigger surprise, since I have never seen it.  I probably spoke to
its originator though...  I have given permission to lots of people
through the years to enhance, modify, or whatever as long as they
freely distributed the results.  I have always been a proponent of
sharing games, not selling them.

Anyway...

Around 1980 or 81 I was enrolled in engineering courses at the
University of Oklahoma.  The engineering lab ran on a PDP 1170 under
an early version of UNIX.  I was always good at computers, so it was
natural for me to get to know the system administrators.  They invited
me one night to stay and play some games, an early startrek game, The
Colossal Cave Adventure (later just 'Adventure'), and late one night,
a new dungeon game called 'Rogue'.

So yes, I was exposed to Rogue before Moria was even a gleam in my
eye.  In fact, Rogue was directly responsible for millions of hours of
play time wasted on Moria and its descendents...

Soon after playing Rogue (and man, was I HOOKED), I got a job in a
different department as a student assistant in computers.  I worked on
one of the early VAX 11/780's running VMS, and no games were available
for it at that time.  The engineering lab got a real geek of an
administrator who thought the only purpose of a computer was WORK!
Imagine...  Soooo, no more games, and no more rogue!

This was intolerable!  So I decided to write my own rogue game, Moria
Beta 1.0.  I had three languages available on my VMS system.  Fortran
IV, PASCAL V1.?, and BASIC.  Since most of the game was string
manipulation, I wrote the first attempt at Moria in VMS BASIC, and it
looked a LOT like Rogue, at least what I could remember of it.  Then I
began getting ideas of how to improve it, how it should work
differently, and I pretty much didn't touch it for about a year.

Around 1983, two things happened that caused Moria to be born in its
recognizable form.  I was engaged to be married, and the only cure for
THAT is to work so hard you can't think about it; and I was enrolled
for fall to take an operating systems class in PASCAL.

So, I investigated the new version of VMS PASCAL and found out it had
a new feature.  Variable length strings!  Wow...

That summer I finished Moria 1.0 in VMS PASCAL.  I learned more about
data structures, optimization, and just plain programming that summer
then in all of my years in school.  I soon drew a crowd of devoted
Moria players...  All at OU.

I asked Jimmey Todd, a good friend of mine, to write a better
character generator for the game, and so the skills and history were
born.  Jimmey helped out on many of the functions in the game as well.
This would have been about Moria 2.0

In the following two years, I listened a lot to my players and kept
making enhancements to the game to fix problems, to challenge them,
and to keep them going.  If anyone managed to win, I immediately found
out how, and 'enhanced' the game to make it harder.  I once vowed it
was 'unbeatable', and a week later a friend of mine beat it!  His
character, 'Iggy', was placed into the game as 'The Evil Iggy', and
immortalized...  And of course, I went in and plugged up the trick he
used to win...

Around 1985 I started sending out source to other universities.  Just
before a OU / Texas football clash, I was asked to send a copy to the
University of Texas...  I couldn't resist...  I modified it so that
the begger on the town level was 'An OU football fan' and they moved
at maximum rate.  They also multiplied at maximum rate...  So the
first step you took and woke one up, it crossed the floor increasing
to hundreds of them and pounded you into oblivion...  I soon received
a call and provided instructions on how to 'de-enhance' the game!

Around 1986 - 87 I released Moria 4.7, my last official release.  I
was working on a Moria 5.0 when I left OU to go to work for American
Airlines (and yes, I still work there).  Moria 5.0 was a complete
rewrite, and contained many neat enhancements, features, you name it.
It had water, streams, lakes, pools, with water monsters.  It had
'mysterious orbs' which could be carried like torches for light but
also gave off magical aura's (like protection from fire, or aggravate
monster...).  It had new weapons and treasures...  I left it with the
student assistants at OU to be finished, but I guess it soon died on
the vine.  As far as I know, that source was lost...

I gave permission to anyone who asked to work on the game.  Several
people asked if they could convert it to 'C', and I said fine as long
as a complete credit history was maintained, and that it could NEVER
be sold, only given.  So I guess one or more of them succeeded in
their efforts to rewrite it in 'C'.

I have since received thousands of letters from all over the world
from players telling about their exploits, and from administrators
cursing the day I was born...  I received mail from behind the iron
curtain (while it was still standing) talking about the game on VAX's
(which supposedly couldn't be there due to export laws).  I used to
have a map with pins for every letter I received, but I gave up on
that!

I am very happy to learn my creation keeps on going...  I plan to
download it and Angband and play them...  Maybe something has been
added that will surprise me!  That would be nice...  I never got to
play Moria and be surprised...

Robert Alan Koeneke
koeneke@ionet.net
