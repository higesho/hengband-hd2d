=== The Town and Wilderness ===

After you have created your character, your TOband adventure begins in the
town called Golyat. Symbols appearing on your screen represent the walls,
floor, objects, features, and creatures lurking about. In order to direct
your character, you enter single-character commands (see jcommand.txt [b]).

Races that live and breathe must carry plenty of food when they leave town.

--- The Vast Wilderness --- ( The '<' command lets you see all of it. )

Since this world is very large, it is convenient to enter the global map by
pressing '<' when you leave town, and then return to the normal map by
pressing '>' when you reach your destination. In the wilderness, roads and
grasslands are relatively safe, but lava fields and the open sea are home to
powerful monsters and are dangerous. You will have to grow stronger before
you can go to such places.

Many dungeons await brave adventurers, such as the "Palace of the Dead".
The ultimate goal of this game is "Dorgalua, the King", who dwells at the
bottom of the "Ancient Ruins". The way to the Ancient Ruins opens only after
you have conquered the "Hanging Gardens".


***** <MapSymbols>
=== Town and Wilderness Symbols ===

Symbols on your map can be broken down into three categories: terrain
features such as walls, floor and doors, and trees, water and lava; objects
which can be picked up such as gold, weapons and magical devices; and
creatures which may or may not move about, but are mostly harmful to your
character's well-being. Symbols specific to the town and wilderness are
shown below. Note that many creatures and objects from the dungeon may also
appear in town, so you should also review the comprehensive listing of all
the various symbols found in the dungeon section (see jdungeon.txt#MapSymbols
[c]).

  Terrain                                   Shops

  # (Green)       A Tree                    1   Entrance to General Store
  ~ (Light Blue)  Shallow Water             2   Entrance to Armoury
  ~ (Dark Blue)   Deep Water                3   Entrance to Weaponsmith
  ~ (Orange)      Shallow Lava              4   Entrance to Temple
  ~ (Red)         Deep Lava                 5   Entrance to Alchemy Shop
  ^ (Brown)       Mountains                 6   Entrance to Magic Shop
  . (White)       Floor / Road              7   Entrance to the Black Market
  . (Brown)       Dirt                      8   Entrance to your Home
  . (Green)       Grass                     9   Entrance to Bookstore

  < (Purple)      Upward Dungeon Entrance

Trees, water and lava may also appear inside the dungeon.
Remember that mountains on the surface can be crossed if you can levitate.


***** <TownLevel>
=== The Town Map ===

Your adventure begins in town. At first it is daytime. The sun rises and
sets as time passes, and the town grows dark at night. Fortunately, the
various shops and buildings are open 24 hours a day.

The vast world of TOband contains six towns and many dungeons. By entering
buildings in the towns, you can accept fixed quests (see [d] below) or aim
to become champion in the arena.

***** <WildMap>
--- The Overworld Map ---

The overworld map is a vast world divided into 129x64 blocks. Each block is
the size of a standard TOband dungeon level and contains natural terrain
(mountainous areas, oceans, plains, volcanoes and so on). It holds six towns
connected by roads, and dungeons with various characteristics are scattered
around the world.

The wilderness is not uninhabited, and it can be dangerous indeed for the
unwary. Wander into the ocean by mistake and you will be attacked by hydras
at once; enter a volcano and you will suffer severe burns before you know
it, and either one can kill you in an instant.

To avoid getting lost in the vast wilderness, use the climb command '<' to
move to the global map, travel to your destination, and then return with
'>'. However, you may suddenly be attacked while moving on the global map.
When hostile monsters are nearby you cannot escape with '<', so you will have
to walk to the edge of the screen to get away.

Each town contains a number of stores where you can buy what you need and
sell your spoils. There are also special buildings that offer services for a
fee. The services available and their prices vary from town to town, so it
is worth hunting around for the best deal.

Buildings that offer various fixed quests are also generated in the towns.


***** <Townspeople>
=== Townspeople ===

The town contains many different kinds of people. There are the street
urchins, young brats who will mob an adventurer for money, and who seem to
come out of the woodwork when excited. Blubbering idiots are a constant
annoyance, but not harmful. Public drunks wander about the town singing, and
are of no threat to anyone. Sneaky rogues who work for the Black Market are
always greedily eyeing your pack for potential new "purchases"... And
finally, what town would be complete without a gang of half-drunk warriors,
who attack or pester people just for fun.

Most of the townspeople should be avoided by the largest possible distance
when you wander from store to store. Fights will break out, though, so be
prepared. Since your character grew up in this chaotic town, no experience
is awarded for killing the townspeople, though you may acquire treasure.

One word of warning, however: occasionally a creature that would normally
inhabit only the wilderness and dungeons may wander into the town. These
should generally be handled with much more caution than the ordinary
townspeople.


***** <Shopping>
=== Shopping in Town ===

Your character will begin his or her adventure with a few basic items and
some money to buy things at the stores. You may enter any of the stores,
represented by the numbers 1 to 9, by moving onto its entrance, and barter
with the shopkeeper over the items you would like to buy.

Once inside a store, you will see the name and race of the shopkeeper, the
name of the store, the maximum amount of money the shopkeeper will pay for
any one item, and the store's goods along with tentative prices. Once you
have haggled an item down as far as the shopkeeper will go, its price becomes
"fixed" (see [e] below).

You will also see an (incomplete) list of available commands. Note that many
of the commands which work in the dungeon work in the stores as well, but
some do not, especially those which involve "using" items.

Stores do not always have everything in stock. As the game progresses,
shopkeepers may put new items on their shelves, so check from time to time.
Also, if you sell a store an item, it may be sold to someone else while you
are adventuring, so don't always expect to be able to buy back everything
you have sold. If you want the goods on sale to change, you need to spend
some time elsewhere. If you have a lot of spare gold, you can buy up every
item in a store, which will induce the shopkeeper to bring out new stock, and
perhaps even make him or her think of retiring.

Shopkeepers will not buy harmful or useless items. If an item is
unidentified, they will pay you its base price for it. Once they have bought
it, they will immediately identify it. If it is a good item, they will add it
to their stock; if it was a bad bargain, they simply throw it away. In either
case, you will gain some knowledge of that kind of item.

***** <Bartering>
--- Bartering ---

When bartering over an item, you enter the amount you are willing to pay (or
accept). You can either enter the amount directly, or precede a number with
a plus or minus sign to enter the difference from your previous offer. But
be warned that shopkeepers do not like to be made fools of, and if you do it
too often they may even throw you out of the store.

If you consistently bargain well in a store, that is, you very often manage
to draw out the "final offer", the shopkeeper will eventually recognize you
as a superb haggler, and will go directly to the final offer instead of
haggling with you. Items that cost $10 or less do not count, as haggling
well over these is usually either very easy or almost impossible. The more
expensive the item, the less likely the shopkeeper is to take you for a good
haggler.

You can also choose, with an option, not to haggle at all, but then a 10%
sales tax is always added to every purchase. This disadvantage is how the
shopkeeper tries to lead you into bartering. (See the options page for
details.)


***** <ObjectPricing>
--- Object Pricing ---

Each shopkeeper has three primary attributes: the maximum amount he or she
will pay for an item, race, and how greedy he or she is. Each of these
affects the price of the goods and what the shopkeeper will pay for what you
sell. Only the first two can be known by the player. The final factors
affecting prices are the race and charisma of the player.

Each item has a base price which is written in the game's source code. That
price is modified by the shopkeeper's greed (greedy shopkeepers charge more
and pay less), your charisma (charismatic players pay less and can charge
more), and the feeling toward your race held by the people who rule the town
in which the shopkeeper lives.

Finally, when you sell an item, the selling price is limited by the state of
the shopkeeper's purse. Even a charismatic player of a friendly people cannot
sell an item for more than this cap. Since the cap applies per item, though,
when you sell several items at once you may receive more than the cap.


***** <TheShops>
=== List of Shops ===

The General Store ("1")
     The General Store sells foods, drinks, cloaks, torches, lanterns, flasks
     of oil, shovels, picks, spikes, arrows, bolts, scratch cards and magic
     dolls. All of these items and some others can be sold back to the
     General Store for money.

The Armoury ("2")
     The Armoury is where the town's armor is fashioned. All sorts of
     protective gear may be bought and sold here.

The Weaponsmith's Shop ("3")
     The Weaponsmith's Shop is where the town's weapons are fashioned. Hand
     and missile weapons may be bought and sold here, along with arrows and
     bolts.

The Temple ("4")
     The Temple deals in healing and restoration potions, scrolls of
     Blessing, scrolls of Remove Curse, scrolls of Word of Recall, priestly
     weapons and so on. It sometimes sells magic dolls too.

The Alchemy Shop ("5")
     The Alchemy Shop deals in many kinds of potions and scrolls.

The Magic Shop ("6")
     The Magic Shop deals in many kinds of rings, wands, staffs, amulets and
     magic dolls.

The Black Market ("7")
     The Black Market will sell and buy any kind of item at extortionate
     prices. However, it occasionally has VERY rare and valuable items. Items
     are also sometimes sold in large lots, which is handy when you want to
     buy a lot of a useful item at once. The shopkeepers are not known for
     their tolerance...

Your Home ("8")
     This is your house, where you can store items that you cannot carry on
     your travels or will need at a later date.

The Bookstore ("9")
     The Bookstore deals in all sorts of magic books, which you can buy and
     sell here.

The Museum ("0")
    Here you can donate items you own, to be put on display. When you have
    grown rich enough, and come across a rare item you have no need to use,
    you may want to leave it here as a memento instead of selling it.

***** <Buildings>
=== Special Buildings ===

In addition to the stores, special buildings appear in town. The names of
these buildings and the services they offer differ from town to town. Each
town has an inn, where the player can have a meal (if food does the player
any good) and stay the night. Each town also has the house or castle of the
person who rules the land.

Besides the inn and the ruler's house or castle, there are also libraries
where you can research items and monsters, various guilds, casinos,
temples, healers and more.


***** <FixedQuests>
=== Fixed Quests ===

In each town there are buildings that offer quests. Quests fall into several
types: kill a certain number of a certain monster (for example, kill 16
wargs), kill all the monsters on the quest map, retrieve a certain item, or
escape from the quest map.

When you accept a quest, you will be given a brief description of the quest
and your objective, and also an indication of its danger level (the dungeon
level it is equivalent to). Note that the danger indication can be somewhat
misleading: quest maps are always the same however many times you play them,
so you can repeat the same situation over several attempts and work out an
effective strategy. When you take a quest for the first time, assume it is
harder than the danger level shown. Also, because the maps are fixed, quest
designers can set up some particularly nasty ambushes!

Once you have accepted a quest, a quest entrance will appear and you may
enter the quest map. Note that you do not have to enter the quest right away;
you may go in whenever you like as you progress through the game. Many quests
can be attempted only once per game, so you should not enter until you are
confident of succeeding.

Whether you complete a quest or fail it, upon leaving the quest map, report
to the person who gave you the quest. If you succeeded, you will receive a
reward. It will normally be waiting for you outside the building, though it
may take other forms. Note that a reward left outside the building will
vanish when you accept the next quest. If you failed, you will be told how
useless you are and be abused. Note that you cannot accept another quest from
the same person until you have reported your success or failure.

Note: Genocide, Mass Genocide and *Destruction* do not work inside fixed
quests, with very rare exceptions.
