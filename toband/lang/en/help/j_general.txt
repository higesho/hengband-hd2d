About the dual license
----------------------
At the suggestion of Robert Ruehlmann, an effort has been under way to make
Angband and its variants distributable under the GPL. For details see the page
about the Angband OpenSource Initiative on the official Angband home page.

http://thangorodrim.angband.org/development/opensource.html

Translation (partly paraphrased) of that page:

Angband and its predecessor Moria were written before the idea of OpenSource
was widely accepted, but their authors made a license guaranteeing that
Angband and Moria would always be distributed free of charge.
(Translator's note: this is the so-called Moria/Angband license.)

Copyright (c) 1997 Ben Harrison, James E. Wilson, Robert A. Koeneke

This software may be copied and distributed for educational, research,
and not for profit purposes provided that this copyright and statement
are included in all such copies.  Other copyrights may also apply.

That license, however, does not meet the conditions of OpenSource: there is
no obligation to publish the source when distributing a changed version, and
there were several other problems besides. As a result Angband cannot lawfully
be included in a Linux distribution, nor can it use the many services prepared
for OpenSource projects (SourceForge and the like), nor can code distributed
under the GPL be used as part of Angband.

I (Robert Ruehlmann) have begun the Angband OpenSource Initiative to
overcome these problems, and am working to make it possible to distribute
Angband (and any variant that wishes to share in the benefit of the license
change) under the GNU General Public License.

Changing the license requires either the agreement of everyone who wrote any
of the code in Angband, or else a complete rewrite of every part written by an
author who has not agreed to the new license.

Moving Angband to the GPL alone would force the authors of every variant based
on Angband to change their license to the GPL as well. To avoid that, I have
decided to use the "dual license" method, distributing the code under both the
GPL and the Angband license. Variant authors may therefore go on using the
Angband license if they wish; they need not change their variant's license to
the GPL.

Note that at present the dual license applies only to the parts of the source
written by those who agreed to the Angband OpenSource Initiative. Other parts
are still distributed under the Moria/Angband license alone, so the dual
license does not affect the whole of Angband as it is distributed today. Until
every part of Angband can be distributed under both licenses, the only license
valid for the program as a whole is the Moria/Angband license.

About Japanese Zangband
-----------------------
At first I (Itakura) had no thought of translating Zangband into Japanese.
I happened to be reading The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit, and I liked
the way Smeagol talks, so I tried changing Smeagol's lines in the game into
Japanese. But when I rewrote the relevant part in lib/file/monspeak.txt in
Japanese, it was not displayed in the game, so I started tinkering with the
source code to make it display Japanese, and before I knew it I was hooked.
Fortunately, the Angband source, in both the English and Japanese versions,
was very easy to understand and easy to change, so I was able to mechanically
extract the Japanese parts from Japanese Angband and mechanically build them
into the English Zangband, and without much effort a large part of it was
translated into Japanese. Even so, a large amount of Z's own messages
remained that needed to be translated.

So I released the still unfinished source, appealed on the net for Zangband
fans to help with the translation, and also released a Windows binary, in the
hope of drawing in more people. I also e-mailed the creators of the many
wonderful home pages I came across on the net, though I had never met them,
and received a variety of useful information.

In this way I gradually drew in many people, the translation progressed, and
here we are. As of 2000/04/06, the translation of the source is almost
finished, and what remains is the monsters' lines and some of the
descriptions. I am very grateful to everyone who helped. The names of the
main people who helped with the translation are listed below.

    Mr. Wakabayashi (bldg.c, cmd5.c)
    Mr. Yozora no Suisei (quests)
    Mr. Shizuki Kanmaru (monster names, item names, etc.)
    Mr. Tessi (Smeagol's speech)
    Mr. Kuru (Cthulhu and Amber related)
    Mr. Fujioka (Amber related)

I am especially grateful to Mr. Kuru, who searched out the Japanese
translations of the Cthulhu material from a huge body of literature. Thanks
to him, R_INFO_J.TXT has become something of real value as reference
material. I also thank Mr. Shizuki Kanmaru, who devised many of the
translated terms.

For the Cthulhu-type monsters, I received the kind permission of NeSYS, the
administrator of N-SYSTEM (http://www.geocities.com/Tokyo/Palace/8768/ ), a
Cthulhu information site with an overwhelming amount of information, to
quote his descriptions of the monsters, and so I was able to add quite
detailed descriptions. I take this opportunity to express my thanks.

For the help files, I used Mr. Saito's ZKB-Japanese Edition,
http://www.ceres.dti.ne.jp/~asaitoh/roguelike/zkb/contents/main.html .

Of course I also thank the people who made Angband, JAngband and Zangband.
After all, their source code is about ten times easier to translate into
Japanese than NetHack's. And in making the WINDOWS version I was greatly
helped by TeO, who made the JAngband WINDOWS version.

Since then, many people have also given me advice and ideas on the bulletin
board. I cannot list all the names, but I am very grateful.
