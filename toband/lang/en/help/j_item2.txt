=== Item Guide ===

Zangband added a variety of weapons and armor. Here I briefly explain those
items, the armor not covered in Jangband, and the names by which items are
called.

Note for the English edition: most of the weapons and armor described below
come from Zangband and do not appear in TOband. Items that are not in TOband
are marked "(not in TOband)". The AC and to-hit values given for the armor
that does appear in TOband (Soft Leather Armour, Hard Leather Armour, Metal
Scale Mail, Chain Mail, Double Chain Mail, Splint Mail, Mithril Chain Mail,
Full Plate Armour, and Mithril Plate Mail) were checked against the game's
item data.


--- Rods / Wands / Staffs ---

All of these are rod-shaped items with magic stored in them. The definitions
in AD&D, which seems to be where the setting was borrowed from, are quoted
below from Mr. Ferianasu's explanation, with additions. For the differences in
how they behave in the game, see jobjects.txt#ObjectTypes [b].

    A wand is a small staff like a magical girl's baton. It has almost no
    meaning other than "magic stick"; most are decorative or have some single
    ornament at the tip. In the Japanese translation of D&D it is rendered as
    "magic stick".

    A staff is made of wood, about as tall as a person or taller, and is quite
    large. Gandalf's staff... the way it is often drawn may be somewhere
    between a staff and a cane.

    A rod is a metal stick about three feet long. A cane is the same size but
    made of wood, like a gentleman's walking stick, and a sceptre is a heavily
    decorated one like a royal scepter. A rod is "thinner" than a staff; it is
    said to be about as thick as a thumb, so it is quite thin. It is also
    translated as "pole".

I explain these with reference to the "Weapon Dictionary" (Buki Jiten) by
Sadaharu Ichikawa, published by Shinkigensha.

  - Three Piece Rod (not in TOband): You can see it in movies such as "The
    36th Chamber of Shaolin." Three sticks joined like nunchaku.

  - Lajatang (not in TOband): A weapon with a naginata-like blade on both ends
    of a pole.

  - Hatchet (not in TOband): An axe for throwing, but it cannot be used for
    throwing in the game.

--- Japanese Weapons / Armor ---

Zangband contains dubious Japanese weapons and armor, which seem to have been
taken from the old Apple II game "Deathlord",
http://208.167.74.218:2700/tiktok/Docs/DeathlordManual.txt.

  - Bo Staff (not in TOband)

  - Tetsubo (not in TOband)

  - Tanto (not in TOband)

  - Wakizashi (not in TOband)

  - Naginata (not in TOband)

  - Jo Staff (not in TOband): This probably refers to the staff (shakujo)
    carried by yamabushi, the mountain ascetics. In the Japanese version of
    Diablo II it is rendered as "Joe's Staff" ;)

  - Ninjato (not in TOband): The blade is about 50 cm long, and its
    distinguishing feature is a large, square guard (tsuba). This is so that
    it can be stood against a wall and used as a foothold. (From Gakken,
    "Secrets of Ninpo and Ninjutsu")

  - No-dachi (not in TOband): A large Japanese sword.

  - O-yoroi (not in TOband) AC 24, skill modifier -2

  - Do-maru (not in TOband) AC 20, skill modifier -2

  - Haramakido (not in TOband) AC 17, skill modifier -2

  - Kabuto (not in TOband) AC 7

  - Jingasa (not in TOband) AC 4


--- Armor Guide ---

Armor in the game is divided into several categories. Armor that gives high
protection but is hard to move in carries a slight minus modifier to the
to-hit chance of your attacks. From the middle of the game on, however, this
is too small to matter. Much more important is that armor with higher
protection is heavier, so if you lack strength you suffer penalties to speed,
spell success rate, and so on, and this effect is far greater.


1: Types made of joined pieces of metal

Armor that balances mobility and strength. Movement is somewhat restricted,
and in the game it carries a to-hit modifier of 2 to 3.

  - Splint Mail, AC 19, to-hit modifier -2: Made of long, narrow metal plates
    joined together. In Diablo it had the graphics of Japanese armor, but in
    Diablo II it returned to ordinary graphics. In D&D, the names Splint Mail
    and Banded Mail are used to distinguish whether the metal plates run
    vertically or horizontally, but it is doubtful whether the vertical kind
    actually existed.

  - Metal Scale Mail, AC 13, to-hit modifier -2

  - Metal Brigandine Armour (not in TOband), AC 19, to-hit modifier -3

  - Metal Lamellar Armour (not in TOband), AC 23, to-hit modifier -3

    The last three are all made by joining pieces of metal 5 to 15 cm long in
    a regular pattern.


2: Types made of joined metal rings

Mail made of chains or metal rings joined into a surface. Movement is somewhat
restricted, and in the game it carries a to-hit modifier of 1 to 2.

  - Ring Mail (not in TOband), AC 12, to-hit modifier -2: Easy to make if the
    rings are large, but it cannot stop a spear thrust.

  - Double Ring Mail (not in TOband), AC 15, to-hit modifier -2: Ring mail
    made double.

  - Chain Mail, AC 14, to-hit modifier -2: Made with finer rings than ring
    mail. In Japan, too, ninja (for example Kage-Maru in Virtua Fighter 3)
    wore it.

  - Augmented Chain Mail (not in TOband), AC 16, to-hit modifier -2:
    Apparently chain mail with the important parts doubled. An item that has
    existed since Moria, the predecessor of Angband.

  - Double Chain Mail, AC 16, to-hit modifier -2: Apparently chain mail with
    every part doubled. An item that has existed since Moria, the predecessor
    of Angband.

  - Bar Chain Mail (not in TOband), AC 18, to-hit modifier -2: Apparently
    chain mail with the important parts covered by iron plates. An item that
    has existed since Moria, the predecessor of Angband.

  - Mithril Chain Mail, AC 28, to-hit modifier -1: Made of precious mithril.
    Famous as the one that Bilbo gets in the novel "The Hobbit".


3: Types made of metal plates

The kind you often see displayed in Western mansions. The protection is
perfect, but movement is restricted, and in the game it carries a to-hit
modifier of 3 to 4.

  - Partial Plate Armour (not in TOband), AC 22, to-hit modifier -3: Armor
    that covers only part of the body, such as the torso.

  - Full Plate Armour, AC 25, to-hit modifier -3: Armor that covers everything
    from the hands to the feet, leaving no part of the body exposed.

  - Ribbed Plate Armour (not in TOband), AC 28, to-hit modifier -3: Plate mail
    whose iron plates have ridges, like corrugated iron, to increase their
    strength.

  - Mithril Plate Mail, AC 35, to-hit modifier -3: Plate mail made of precious
    mithril.

  - Adamantite Plate Mail (not in TOband), AC 40, to-hit modifier -4: In "The
    Lord of the Rings", adamantite is a sturdy metal also used, for example,
    in the foundation of the Tower of Orthanc. The word adamantite is also
    used as an archaic word for diamond, but in the game it is a special
    metal.


4: Materials other than metal

Movement is hardly restricted at all, but the protection is only moderate.

  - Soft Leather Armour, AC 4

  - Soft Studded Leather (not in TOband), AC 5

  - Hard Leather Armour, AC 6, to-hit modifier -1

  - Hard Studded Leather (not in TOband), AC 7, to-hit modifier -1

    Hard leather gives more protection but makes movement slightly harder. For
    the studded kind, you can imagine the sort of outfit a heavy-metal fan
    often wears.

  - Padded Armour (not in TOband), AC 4: Also called quilted armor. Cloth is
    sewn together with cotton or the like stuffed in between.

  - Leather Jacket (not in TOband), AC 12, to-hit modifier -1: In NetHack it
    is the starting equipment of the Archeologist. The kind that Indiana Jones
    wears.

  - Cord Armour (not in TOband), AC 6: Apparently leather armor reinforced
    with wire.

  - Stone and Hide Armour (not in TOband), AC 15, to-hit modifier -1

  - Rhino Hide Armour (not in TOband), AC 8, to-hit modifier -1

  - Paper Armour (not in TOband), AC 4

    I do not really know about these last three.
