=== TOband Objects ===

The TOband dungeons are full of objects, and many of the monsters you
meet will be carrying one or more items about their person, which you
may take from their corpses should you be skillful enough to defeat
them. By setting an option you may pick up objects simply by walking on
top of them, or be prompted before picking them up (see
joption.txt#always_pickup [b]). You can also set which kinds of objects
are picked up automatically (see autopick.txt).

Many objects found within the dungeon have their own commands for their
use. Wands must be 'a'imed, staffs must be 'u'sed, scrolls must be
'r'ead, potions must be 'q'uaffed, and so on. You may, in general, use
not only the items in your pack but also the items on the floor beneath
you.

***** <EquipInvent>
=== Equipment and Inventory ===

You can carry up to 23 different kinds of objects in your pack, and in
addition you can wear or wield up to 12 others. Note that the powers an
object grants only work while it is equipped. For example, a Shield of
Resist Acid will not protect you from acid attacks while it is merely
in your pack. Items you hold in your hands or wear on your body are
called your 'equipment', while the items in your pack are called your
'inventory'. You can view your equipment with the 'e' command and your
inventory with the 'i' command at any time.

Although you are limited to 23 different kinds of items in your pack,
each kind may actually be a 'stack' of up to 99 similar items. This
allows you to carry multiple spell books, rations of food and other
essential items without using up all of your inventory slots. If you
somehow manage to stuff a 24th kind of item into your pack (for
example, by taking off a piece of equipment while your pack already
holds 23 kinds), your pack will 'overflow' and the item you were trying
to put in it will fall to the floor. You will be warned before you
execute any command that is likely to cause this.

In addition to the number of different kinds of items you may carry,
you are also limited in the total weight you can carry. As you approach
this limit you become slower, which makes it easy for monsters to chase
you. Note that there is no upper bound on how much you can carry if you
do not mind being slow. The amount you may carry before becoming slowed
is determined by your strength. The limit applies to the combined
weight of your equipment and your inventory.

Because of these limits on space and weight, it is important for a
player to learn to manage his inventory efficiently. You must carefully
balance the need to carry essential items such as food, fuel for your
light source, spell books (if you can use them), healing items,
escaping items and everything else that is necessary for your survival,
while at the same time keeping room in your pack to carry back treasure
from the dungeon to sell in the town, and making sure that you do not
exceed your weight limit.


***** <Generation>
=== Object Generation ===

In TOband, objects are generated when you enter a dungeon level, and
they are spread around the dungeon floor. They may also be generated
when a monster is killed (assuming the monster is capable of carrying
objects). Each object has a 'native' depth, a depth at which it begins
to be commonly found in the dungeon. It is unusual, though not
impossible, to find an object on a level significantly shallower than
its native depth. Note that although items generated on the floor may
be in 'piles', they will never be in 'stacks'.

Which items are generated when a level is created, or when a monster
dies, is determined by a number of factors, including the current
dungeon depth, the monster's native level and the native depth of the
object being considered for generation. In the case of monster drops,
the quality of the object is also affected by the guaranteed quality
level (if any) of that monster's drop.

Monster drops are either normal, 'good', 'great' or 'special'. These
terms generally refer to equipment such as weapons and armor (for
example, a 'good' drop means that any equipment generated is guaranteed
to have magical pluses, a 'great' drop means that any equipment
generated is guaranteed to be either an ego item or an artifact, and a
'special' drop means that any equipment generated is guaranteed to be
an artifact), but they also encompass certain other objects. For
example, Amulets of the Magi, the dungeon-only spell books and Rings of
Speed are all considered to be 'great' objects, and may therefore be
dropped by a monster with a 'great' drop. Note that some monsters may
drop treasure rather than objects.


***** <Compaction>
=== Object Compaction ===

TOband can keep track of a large, but finite, number of objects at any
one time. If, as you kill monsters, the number of objects dropped
together with the number of objects already lying on the floor exceeds
this limit, you will be told that the game is "Compacting objects...".

When TOband compacts objects, it first tries to combine similar objects
into piles. If this does not create enough space, it will delete some
already existing items to make room for new ones. As far as possible,
the compaction is performed on objects some distance away from the
player's current position. During compaction, items are removed in
order of increasing value, with the least valuable items removed first.
Ego items are rarely removed during the compaction process, while
artifacts (see below [d]) are never removed.


***** <CursedObjects>
=== Cursed Objects ===

A goodly percentage of the items generated in the dungeon or dropped by
monsters will be cursed. Most cursed items have penalties to various
things such as your skill, your deadliness, or your armor class. They
may also have other undesirable attributes. Shopkeepers will refuse to
buy cursed items.

Should you wield or wear a cursed item, you will be shown the message
"Oops, it feels deathly cold!" and the item will automatically be
inscribed {cursed}. Once equipped, a cursed item cannot be taken off
until the curse has been lifted. Lifting the curse allows the item to
be removed, but it does not remove negative effects such as the
penalties mentioned above.

There are several ways to lift a curse. The Holy realm of magic
contains a spell for this purpose. Scrolls that remove curses can also
be bought in the Inner Temple. In addition, a curse may be lifted as a side
effect of enchanting an item, although this is not guaranteed to
happen.

Note: Items that can be activated (Dragon Scale Mail and artifacts)
become hard to activate when they are cursed.

--- The Curses ---

There are several different types of curse.


Normal Curse
    A normally cursed item will typically have penalties to armor class,
    skill, deadliness and various other things. It can be uncursed by
    reading a Scroll of Remove Curse, or by the weaker version of the
    Holy realm spell.

Heavy Curse
    A heavily cursed item will typically have penalties to armor class,
    skill, deadliness and various other things. It can be uncursed by
    reading a Scroll of *Remove Curse*, or by the stronger version of
    the Holy realm spell.

Permanent Curse
    Items that are permanently cursed cannot be uncursed, and so once
    worn they can never be removed. You may often find permanently
    cursed items that you are tempted to wield. Think carefully before
    you do so, because it will be too late once you change your mind.


***** <AncientCurse>
=== The Ancient and Foul Curse ===

The Ancient and Foul Curse is sometimes incorrectly called 'The Curse
of Topi Ylinen', after the person who first created it. Unlike the
other curses discussed above, an object with the Ancient and Foul Curse
can be taken off at any time, and you will not get the "Oops, it feels
deathly cold!" message when you wield it (unless, of course, it also
carries one of the other curses).

While the item is equipped, the curse will be invoked at random from
time to time. When it is invoked, one or more of several possible
effects will occur at random. The possible effects are: summoning
monsters, summoning Cyberdemons, paralysis (even with Free Action),
teleporting the player, removing the walls around the player, amnesia,
and the temporary or permanent reduction of some of your primary
stats.

Note: Although the Ancient and Foul Curse is mostly found on objects,
there are a few other occasions on which you might fall foul of it,
such as setting off certain traps.


***** <ObjectFlavors>
=== Object Flavors ===

Some objects (scrolls, potions, wands, rods and staffs) are 'flavored
items'. This means that in each game they are given a random flavor.
For example, the first time you find a Potion of Heroism it might be
described as a 'Dark Blue Potion'. Once you have identified it, the
potion will be described as a 'Dark Blue Potion of Heroism'. Flavors
vary from game to game, so your next character might find that the same
Dark Blue Potion is now something entirely different. This means that
each new character must set about learning exactly what each flavored
item is.

One of the TOband options lets you switch to 'plain' object
descriptions, which leave the flavor out of the description of any
flavored item whose effect is already known. With it, the potion above,
once identified, would be described simply as a 'Potion of Heroism'.


***** <PseudoID>
=== Pseudo-ID ===

Occasionally, as you wander around the dungeon, you will sense
something about the quality of the items you are wielding or carrying
in your pack. This also applies to items lying at your feet. This
sensing ability is called 'pseudo-id', and it works only on weapons and
armor. The speed and accuracy of your pseudo-id ability depend on a
number of factors, but the primary consideration is your skill level.
Pseudo-id is either 'strong' or 'weak', and its speed is 'slow',
'medium' or 'fast'.

When you pseudo-id an item, it will be inscribed (see below [e]) with
the quality level you have sensed. This inscription remains until you
identify the item (see below [f]). The various inscriptions and their
meanings are as follows:


Table 1 - Strong Pseudo-Id

    Inscription    Meaning
    -------------------------------------------------
    terrible       a cursed artifact
    worthless      a cursed ego item
    cursed         a cursed item
    average        an average item
    good           an enchanted item
    excellent      an ego item
    special        a non-cursed artifact


Table 2 - Weak Pseudo-Id

    Inscription    Meaning
    ------------------------------------------------
    cursed         a cursed item (includes bad ego
                   items and artifacts)
    uncursed       an enchanted item (includes good
                   ego items and artifacts)

If pseudo-id fails to tell you what an item does, {tried} is inscribed.

As your skill level rises, pseudo-id gives way to ordinary
identification and *identification*.


***** <Identifying>
=== Identifying Objects ===

Each player will rely to a greater or lesser extent on pseudo-id to
judge the quality of items, but in most cases you will need to identify
an item before you can tell how useful it will be to you. There are
several methods of identifying items available to you. These include
Scrolls of Identify and *Identify*, Staffs and Rods of Perception,
spells and prayers, and services offered in the buildings of the town,
among others.

There is an important difference between 'identify'ing an object and
'*identify*'ing an object. 'Identify'ing an object will reveal its
correct name (including the ego type, if applicable), any pluses to
combat or armor class, and any bonuses to stats and skills it may have.
'*Identify*'ing an item will reveal all information about it, including
its fixed abilities and any random abilities it has. Once an item has
been *identified*, you can review its full abilities at any time with
the 'I'nspect command, and you can also use the special inscription '%'
to make all of its abilities visible at a glance (see Object
Inscriptions [e] below).


Another means of identifying flavored items is to sell them to a shop.
After buying the item, the shopkeeper will tell you what it was that
you sold. This can be a useful technique in the early game, but it
exposes you to the risk of selling a valuable item that can only be
found deep in the dungeon and that happened to be generated out of
depth.

Note: You will automatically have full knowledge of any item that you
purchase in the town.


***** <EgoObjects>
=== Ego Objects and Artifacts ===

In addition to the ordinary weapons and armor, you may find in the
dungeon items that are endowed with additional powers. Unlike
artifacts, of which only one exists in each game, it is not unusual to
find several Ego Weapons or pieces of Ego Armor of the same type in the
course of your adventures.

For Ego Weapons, see jattack.txt#EgoArtifact [g]. For Ego Armor, see
jdefend.txt#EgoArtifact [h]. (If you press 'g' or 'h' now, you will
jump there. Press ESC to return when you have finished reading.)

Of all the objects in TOband, by far the most important are the
artifacts. They are unique items that exist only once in the world, and
they increase or sustain several of your stats, and grant resistances
or bonuses to your skills. Often an artifact can be 'A'ctivated for a
magical effect. This is extremely useful, especially for classes that
are weak in magic.

There are two types of artifacts, fixed and random. The fixed artifacts
each have an entry in the lib/edit/a_info.txt file, and consequently
have a chance of appearing in every game you play. Random artifacts, on
the other hand, are created by the game, and have both random names and
random properties. Once created, there is almost no chance that you
will ever see exactly the same one again.


--- (Non-) Preserve Mode ---

One important thing to note when considering artifacts is the
(non-)preserve mode that you choose at the start of the game. In
non-preserve mode, once a fixed artifact has been generated (either on
the dungeon floor or as a monster drop), it will never be generated
again during that game. This means that if you leave the level without
picking up the artifact, it is gone forever. In preserve mode, on the
other hand, this only happens once you have identified the artifact. It
is therefore safer to leave a level before it has been completely
explored, because any fixed artifact you missed may appear again on
another level.

--- Special Feelings ---

If you stay on a dungeon level for a while, a feeling about that level
will be displayed. This is your intuition telling you about the quality
of the items on the level and how dangerous its monsters are. Feelings
are discussed in more detail in the sections on the town and the
dungeon. One of the possible feelings is "You feel there is something
special about this level." This message is only given when playing in
non-preserve mode. A special feeling means one of two things: the level
has either a special room or vault, or an artifact. If there is an
artifact on the level, you will always get this message, but for a
special room or vault the message becomes less and less likely the
deeper you go in the dungeon. At very deep levels, a special feeling is
almost always caused by an artifact.

Note: In non-preserve mode, you may still find an artifact on a level
that did not give you a special feeling. In that case, though, it will
have been dropped by a monster, not lying on the dungeon floor from the
start.

--- The '~' Command ---

By pressing '~' and choosing the option to display known artifacts, you
can see which fixed artifacts you have found so far. In non-preserve
mode, this will also show the fixed artifacts that you have missed.
However, while you are in the dungeon and have not yet picked up the
artifact, nothing about it is shown; the artifacts you have missed are
shown only after you have returned to the town.

--- Random Artifacts ---

In addition to the standard fixed artifacts, which may appear in any
game, TOband also generates random artifacts. Weapons and armor, and
even Amulets of the Magi and Rings of Lordly Protection, can become
artifacts.

Random artifacts can be good or bad, and their quality varies widely.
Unlike fixed artifacts, the details of random artifacts are not
included in the spoiler file the game can output, so *Identify*ing one
is the only way to get detailed information about it.


***** <RandomAbilities>

***** <Inscriptions>
=== Object Inscriptions ===

You may "inscribe" any object with a text inscription of your choice,
using the 'inscribe' command. These inscriptions are not limited in
length, though you may not be able to see the whole inscription on
screen if it is too long. An inscription is enclosed in { } and
appended to the object's name. An inscription is applied to the
specific object (or pile of objects) you chose; it is not automatically
applied to all similar objects (there are two options concerning this,
so please refer to them).

In certain situations, TOband will automatically give an object a
temporary "fake" inscription. The inscription "empty" shows that an
item has no magic left in it. The inscription "tried" is given to an
item that you have used, but whose effect you could not identify.
Items that are known to be cursed are inscribed "cursed", and broken
items are inscribed "broken". Also, any item that was purchased at a
discount (implying that it is slightly "sub-standard") will be
inscribed with the appropriate discount, such as "25% off".

In addition to these, TOband often inscribes an item with an
"impression" of its quality that your character has formed. Every
character forms an impression of an item after carrying it for a while.


--- Making use of Inscriptions ---

Some players use the inscription feature to record where and how they
found an object, or to note the abilities and powers an object has.
TOband has a feature that examines an object's abilities and inscribes
them for you automatically.

Besides this, some inscriptions have a special meaning of their own.
Many commands in TOband require you to specify an object, for example
from your inventory: which potion you wish to 'q'uaff, which staff you
wish to 'u'se, and so on. Inscriptions are a powerful way of saving
effort in such cases.


----------------------------------------------------------------
Auto Inscription of Resistances and Powers '%'
----------------------------------------------------------------

When you inscribe {%} on an *identified* object, using the '{' command
or the auto-pickup/destroy settings, the inscription is automatically
replaced at that moment by a short string of abbreviations describing
the object's abilities. For example, if you inscribe '%' on a random
artifact that has resistance to fire and poison and some bonus to
Strength, it will be written as {St;FiPo}. From then on you can see the
artifact's resistances at a glance, without using the 'I' command.
Naturally, if you do not know the object's abilities (that is, you have
not *identified* it), inscribing '%' does nothing.

For fixed artifacts and ego items, you can tell their resistances from
the name alone (if you remember them!), so the inscription lists only
the extra resistances and abilities that were added at random. For
example, if Boots of Levitation have been given an extra resistance to
nether, they will simply be inscribed {Nt}. The ability of levitation
is not written in the inscription. If you do not like this behavior,
inscribe '%all' instead. Then all resistances and abilities will be
written, with no distinction between the added ones and the ones the
object had from the start. For the boots above, the result would be
{Nt;Lv}.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {@x#} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

The {@x#} inscription (where 'x' is a single letter for the key of a
command, and '#' is a digit from 0 to 9 that identifies the object) is
perhaps the most commonly used inscription. When you select command
'x' and then press the digit '#', the game searches your inventory for
an object with the inscription {@x#} and performs that command on it.
Thus if you inscribe a Potion of Healing with {@q1}, pressing 'q1' will
quaff it. This feature is useful because as items are added to or
removed from your inventory, the label ('a' to 'w') of each item
changes. With this inscription you can select the item regardless of
its label, which makes things much easier.

Note: The letter that specifies the command in an inscription must be
the one from the original keyset. Even if you have chosen the roguelike
keyset, you must use the original keyset's letters in inscriptions. For
example, the command to fire a missile is 't' in the roguelike keyset,
but if you inscribe your arrows with {@t0}, pressing the digit '0'
will not select them. You must inscribe them with {@f0}. This is
inconvenient, but it has the advantage that changing your keyset later
does not affect your item selection or your macros.

As a special case, when the command is 'm' (cast a spell), you may use
any letter instead of a digit, as in {@ma} or {@mb}. Especially with the
roguelike keyset, letters are easier to type over and over than digits.

You can inscribe the same {@x#} inscription on several objects. If you
do so, selecting '#' for that command will act only on the first
matching object found, not on each of the objects bearing the
inscription.

You can also put several such inscriptions on a single object. For
example, a spell book may be inscribed {@m1@b1}, so that it works for
both of the commands that take a spell book as their target: cast a
spell ('m') and browse a book ('b'). You can also give the same command
more than one number; for example, a food item might be inscribed
{@E1@E2}.

Example 1:
    Suppose you have a main weapon and a shovel. You normally keep your
    weapon wielded, but you use the shovel when you dig for treasure.
    Inscribe your main weapon with {@w0} and your shovel with {@w1}.
    Then, when you want to wield the shovel, press 'w1', and to wield
    your main weapon again, press 'w0', wherever they happen to be in
    your inventory.

    Note: You can also inscribe both the main weapon and the shovel
    with {@w0}, and just press 'w0' whenever you want to swap them.

Example 2:

    To cast the first spell from the first spell book in your
    inventory, you would normally type 'maa', and to cast the first
    spell from the second book you would type 'mba'. However, if your
    first book is stolen or destroyed, typing 'mba' will no longer cast
    the spell you wanted. Players often cast the wrong spell in such a
    situation. To prevent this, inscribe your first book with {@ma} and
    your second book with {@mb}, and so on. Then, even if your other
    spell books are lost, you never have to worry about casting the
    wrong spell.

Example 3:
    Suppose you are carrying some Rations of Food and some Waybread. You
    normally want to eat the Rations first, but you also want to be able
    to use the healing properties of the Waybread in an emergency.
    Inscribe the Rations with {@E1} and the Waybread with {@E1@E2}. Since
    the Rations appear above the Waybread in your inventory list, typing
    'E1' will eat the Rations first, and the Waybread only when the
    Rations have run out. When you deliberately want to eat the
    Waybread, simply type 'E2'. You can do something similar with your
    healing potions.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {@#} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

The {@#} inscription (where '#' is a digit from 0 to 9) is similar to
the {@x#} inscription, but it works for any command. For example, if
you inscribe both your main weapon and your digging tool with {@0},
typing 'w0' will swap them, whichever one you are currently wielding.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {!x} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

An object inscribed {!x} (where 'x' is the key of any command) will
always prompt you for "verification" whenever that object is selected
for that command. For example, inscribing an object {!f!k!d} greatly
reduces the odds of losing it by accident, because any attempt to throw
it ('f'), destroy it ('k') or drop it ('d') will prompt you to verify
whether you really wish to take that action.

An object inscribed "!*" prompts for verification in every command, so
you can handle that object with great caution. Also note that "selling"
and "dropping" both use the 'd' command, so inscribing an item with {!d}
will also prevent you from selling it by accident.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {^x} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

If you inscribe {^x} (where 'x' is the key of any command) on an item
you are wearing or wielding, you will be asked to verify before using
that command. A common example is to inscribe your boots with {^<^>},
which makes the game ask for verification before you go up or down a
staircase.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {=g} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

An object inscribed {=g} is picked up automatically when you walk over
it. This overrides any auto-pickup options you have set. The primary
purpose is to make it easy to retrieve missiles such as arrows and
crossbow bolts that are scattered on the floor after being fired.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {#anystring} Inscription (fake artifacts)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Normally an inscription is shown enclosed in { }, so you can tell at a
glance that it was written by the user. Sometimes, however, you may want
to give a favorite item a name of your own, as if it were an artifact.
In that case, put a '#' in the inscription and write the name you like
after it. For example, if you inscribe a Katana of Slaying (4d4) (+9,+9)
with "#'Masamune'", the game will display it as "a Katana of Slaying
'Masamune' (4d4) (+9,+9)". This does not work on real artifacts,
because they already have names.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {!!} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

If you inscribe {!!} on a rod or on certain activatable artifacts, the
game will tell you with a message when the item has finished recharging
and can be used again.

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {.} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

Some items have the ability of random teleportation. In particular, for
items that are not cursed, the game will at random moments ask you for
confirmation whether you want to teleport. If you find this annoying,
inscribe the item with {.}, and the game will no longer ask for
confirmation and the item will not teleport you. The {.} inscription
has no effect on cursed items (they always teleport you).

----------------------------------------------------------------
The {$} Inscription
----------------------------------------------------------------

Some items have the ability of warning. They warn you and ask for
confirmation before you continue when you are about to be caught by a
trap, or when you come into the line of sight of an enemy that makes
strong attacks. If you inscribe such an item with {$}, no warning will
ever be given.



***** <ObjectTypes>
=== The Object Types ===

Ammunition ('{')
   Ammunition of various types and quality is a frequent find in the
   dungeon and is usually found in piles. To get the full benefit from
   ammunition, it must be 'f'ired with the appropriate missile
   launcher. Guns fire bullets, bows fire arrows and crossbows fire
   crossbow bolts.

Missile Launchers ('}')
   There are several types of missile launchers, from firearms to
   crossbows. Each type of launcher uses its own ammunition. Missile
   launchers must be 'w'ielded before they can be used.

Weapons ('|', '/', '\')
   There are numerous types of weapons in the dungeons. Each weapon
   differs in weight and in its damage dice. Weapons must be 'w'ielded
   before they can be used.

Armor ('[', '(', ')', ']')
   Body armor is worn on your body, cloaks about your body, shields on
   your arm, caps and helms on your head, gloves, gauntlets and cesti on
   your hands, and boots on your feet. Armor of any kind must be
   'w'orn with the 'w' command.

Amulets ('"')
   Amulets are flavored items and are worn around your neck, using the
   'w' command.

Rings ('=')
   Rings are flavored items. They are worn on the ring fingers of your
   left and right hands, using the 'w' command.

Scrolls ('?')
    Scrolls are flavored items whose effects take place when they are
    'r'ead. Most scrolls serve practical purposes.

Potions ('!')
    Potions are flavored items whose effects take place when they are
    'q'uaffed. Some potions can be thrown at monsters and will take
    effect on them when the potion shatters. Similarly, a potion you are
    carrying may occasionally shatter during combat, spreading its
    effects on the monsters around you.

Food (',')
    Various food items are scattered throughout the dungeon. They must
    be 'E'aten for you to take in nourishment. If you do not eat
    regularly, you will become hungry and then weak. Eventually you will
    begin to faint from hunger, and finally you will die of starvation.

Note: Undead races and reincarnated classes do not need food. If a food
item has a special effect, however, you can still gain that effect.

Wands ('-')
    Wands are flavored magical items that hold a finite number of
    'charges'. Each charge represents a single use of the wand, and
    using the wand once reduces the number of charges by one. Once all
    the charges are gone, the wand must be 'recharged' before it can be
    used again. In general, wands fire bolt or ball spells and do not
    have area effects. Wands do not normally affect the player directly,
    and are generally used for attacking. Wands are used with the 'a'im
    command, and you need to specify a target.

Staffs ('_')
    Staffs are flavored magical items and are made of various types of
    wood. Like wands, they hold a finite number of charges and must be
    recharged when empty. In general, staffs have an area effect or act
    on the player himself. Unlike wands, few staffs can be used for
    attack. Staffs are used with the 'u'se command and do not require a
    target.

Rods ('-')
    Rods are similar to both wands and staffs, in that many of the
    magical effects available from wands and staffs are also available
    from rods. The main difference is that a rod holds only a single
    charge, and that after it is used it recharges itself naturally over
    time. Typically, the better the effect of a rod, the longer it takes
    to recharge. This means that you have an unlimited supply of the
    magical effect, but that you can only use it infrequently, compared
    with a wand or staff that can be used every turn until it is empty.
    Rods are used with the 'z'ap command, and many of them require a
    target.

Spell Books ('?')
    Each realm of magic has one or more spell books. If you are a
    spellcaster, you can 'b'rowse the books of the realms you can use to
    see what spells they contain. You cannot use books from other
    realms. You cast spells with the 'm' (magic) command.

Chests ('&')
    Chests are complex objects, which may be trapped or locked. If you
    manage to open one, however, you may find treasure or other objects
    inside. The commands that apply to traps and doors also work on
    chests; however, these commands cannot be used while you are
    carrying the chest.

Figurines ('`')
    A figurine is a magical item, a small replica of one of the monsters
    in the dungeon. When a figurine is thrown, it turns into that
    monster and becomes your pet.

Statues (''')
    Statues are made of various materials and can be found throughout
    the dungeon. Unlike figurines, they have no magical powers, but
    depending on their material they may sell for a high price.

Corpses and Bones ('~')
    You may sometimes find the corpses and bones of monsters and of
    other adventurers who died in the dungeon.

Various Junk ('~')
    As in any dungeon, you should expect to find various kinds of junk
    such as broken sticks and empty bottles. Most of it is useless, but
    an Iron Spike can be wedged into a door with the 'j'am command to
    prevent it from being opened.

Light Sources ('~')
    Various light sources and their fuel are also scattered about the
    dungeon. If you are carrying the appropriate items, you can refill
    your light source with the 'F' (fuel) command. Lanterns can be
    refilled with flasks of oil, and torches with other torches.
