=== Creating a Character ===

TOband is a role-playing game, in which you, the player, control a character
you have created and adventure in the world of TOband. The most important
thing to do before you begin your adventure is to create your character. In
character creation you choose and decide various attributes that will affect
the future of the character who is your alter ego.

The order of character creation is this. First you choose the type of
character you want to play, and then the stats are decided by allocating
points. Once the character has been born in this way, you can start the game
with that character.

You may start the entire process over at any time by pressing 'S'.


***** <CharAttributes>
=== Character Attributes ===

Every character has four primary attributes: sex, race, class and element.
They are chosen at the start of character creation and are fixed for the
whole life of the character. However, if you meet conditions such as sex,
stats and alignment, you can change class later at your home.

The sex of the character affects which races and classes you can choose.
More details on the other attributes are found in the section on races,
classes and elements (see jraceclas.hlp [a]) and the section on the magic
system (see jmagic.hlp [b]).

Every character also has a few secondary attributes: height, weight, social
class and background. These are generated randomly, but are somewhat affected
by the sex and race of the character. In general these attributes only add
"flavor" to the character and help you get into the game, but they do have a
few effects on play. For example, weight affects the penalty when riding.

In addition to these, there are several statistics which are used to
determine your character's relative skills and abilities.


--- Primary Statistics ---

Each character has six primary stats: strength, intelligence, wisdom,
dexterity, constitution and charisma, which modify the abilities of the
character in a variety of ways. For example, strength affects your carrying
capacity, the damage you do to an enemy, and the number of attacks you can
make per turn. A more complete discussion is given later (see [c]).

--- Experience ---

Experience affects almost everything about your character. Experience can
be gained when your character kills monsters, casts a spell or prays for the
first time, learns about a kind of item by using it, and disarms traps or
unlocks doors. Certain classes also gain experience by destroying specific
spellbooks, and there are potions in the dungeon that will boost your
experience if you quaff them.

When your character's experience crosses certain fixed boundaries, you will
attain a new level (up to a maximum of 50). When this happens, your hit
points, your mana (if any), and certain skills such as melee fighting, bows
and throws all increase. Some races and classes will also gain new powers
when their experience crosses certain thresholds.

Deep down inside, the real objective of the game is to gain experience and
certain other characteristics and to collect useful items, so that you
become strong enough to defeat the great 'Dorgalua, the King'. Certain
monsters can "drain" your experience, and thus your level, which will cause
you to lose all of the abilities gained with the higher level. Luckily, you
can restore drained experience by magical means, or by simply earning the
experience all over again.

--- Gold (AU) ---

Each character has some gold, which can be used to buy items and services
in town. Gold can be increased by selling items to the shops, by taking it
from defeated enemies, and by mining the veins where it lies buried.

Each character starts the game with some gold, the amount of which is
decided by the points left over from deciding the character's stats. A
character also starts with a few useful items. You may keep them, or sell
them to a shopkeeper for gold.

--- Hit Points ---

Hit points (HP) represent how much damage the character can sustain before
dying. HP are decided by race, class, level, constitution and the luck of
the dice, and can be raised by magical means. HP can be recovered by
resting or by other magical means.

--- Spell Points (Mana) ---

Spell points (or mana, MP) represent how many spells (prayers) the character
can cast. MP are decided by one primary stat determined by class, the race
modifier to that stat, level, and the luck of the dice. MP can be recovered
by resting or by other magical means.

--- Character Skills ---

Each character has several primary skills: disarming, magic devices, saving
throws, stealth, searching, perception, melee, and bows and throws. These
are determined by race, class, level, stats and equipment. The effects of
these skills are fairly obvious from their names, but they are described in
more detail later. The starting abilities depend on race and class. They are
also changed by stats and level (see [d] for details). The amount they grow
varies with the classes you have experienced.


***** <StartupOptions>
=== Birth Options ===

After you choose your element during character creation, the birth options
screen appears; you can also press '=' at any time while choosing your race,
class and so on to change the birth options. For a description of the
various birth options, see the help on options (see [e]). Options with
"(*)" in their name affect the difficulty of the game. If you change them,
your final score is adjusted to reflect the difference in difficulty.


***** <PointConfig>
=== Point Allocation ===

Your character is given a number of points (60). Some of these points have
already been used up by the class you chose, and you decide your character's
abilities by distributing the remaining points. For each stat (see [c]), the
screen shows the base value, the minimum and maximum values, the cost, the
race modifier and the total. Each base stat can be set from 10 to 18.

Use 2/8 (or j/k, or the cursor keys) to move between stats, and 4/6 (or
h/l, or the cursor keys) to decrease or increase the selected stat. When you
are satisfied, press Enter to accept.

If you finish with points left over, you start with more gold (50 gold for
each unused point).

After the stats, you can edit the background (see below), and then you are
asked for the character's name. Finally the character is displayed. If you
accept it (by pressing Enter), you arrive in town. If you do not accept it
(press 'S'), you return to the beginning of character creation; press 'Q' to
quit.


***** <BackgroundEditor>
=== Background Editor ===

A background for the character is generated randomly along with the stats,
but you can change it in the background editor. The editor comes up once you
have finished allocating points, so enter your text there. It cannot be
changed after the game has started.

In the editor, move the cursor with the cursor keys (or Ctrl-P, Ctrl-N,
Ctrl-F and Ctrl-B). Any other printable key, including the number keys,
types that character. Press Enter to finish editing and keep your changes,
or ESC to finish editing and discard them.


***** <QuickStart>
=== Quick Start ===

If you have already created a character, and that character died or you gave
up and chose to create a new character, you will be asked whether to use
quick start before the normal creation. If you press 'y' and choose quick
start, you can start a new game with a character exactly the same as the
last one, including race, class, stats and background.


***** <PrimaryStats>
=== The Six Primary Stats ===

Each character has six primary stats: strength, intelligence, wisdom,
dexterity, constitution and charisma. They modify the abilities of the
character in a variety of ways. Each stat is expressed as a number, with a
minimum value of 3. For traditional reasons, stats above 18 are expressed as
(nearly) percentile numbers such as 18/47. The base stat, before race
bonuses are added, has an upper limit of 18/200. As you gain levels, these
stats rise by amounts set by your class.

Increases from race and the magical effects of equipment can be added to
this base stat, up to a maximum of 18/220. Since it can never go beyond this,
18/220 is shown in the game as 18/*** to show that it is the maximum.

Traditionally, a number such as "18/50" is thought of as a number between 18
and 19. However, probably the best way to understand the bonus after the
"18/" is to think of it as tenths, since it often takes the same magic to
raise a stat from, say, 4 to 5, or 16 to 17, as from 18/40 to 18/50. Another
important thing to note is that most internal calculations ignore the last
digit, so that, for example, "18/40" and "18/49" almost always have the same
effect.

--- The Primary Statistics ---

Strength
     Strength is critical to fighting effectively in melee and with missile
     weapons. A high strength increases your chances of getting multiple
     blows per turn, and dramatically increases the damage done with each
     hit. It also has a slight effect on your chance to hit. Characters with
     low strength may receive penalties. Strength is also useful for
     digging, bashing, and carrying heavy loads without being slowed down.

Intelligence
     Intelligence affects the spellcasting ability of the classes that cast
     by intelligence (Swordmaster, Dragoon, Ninja, Wizard, Warlock, Witch,
     Siren, Lich and Wicce). Intelligence affects the rate at which MP rise,
     and the spell failure rate. Intelligent characters are also better at
     using magic devices, and at picking locks and disarming traps.

Wisdom
     Wisdom affects the spellcasting ability of Beast-Tamers, Exorcists,
     Valkyries, Dragon-Tamers, Clerics, Priests, Divine Knights, Templars and
     White-Knights. In that sense it means exactly the same for them as
     intelligence does for an ordinary mage. A character with high wisdom has
     a greater chance of resisting spells cast on him or her by enemies.

Dexterity
     Dexterity is a combination of agility and quickness. A character with
     high dexterity can get multiple blows with light weapons, increasing
     attack power, and also improves the chance to hit and to avoid being
     hit. A character with high dexterity is good at picking locks and
     disarming traps, and also good at protecting himself or herself from the
     thieves that live in the dungeon.

Constitution
     Constitution is a character's ability to withstand damage and recover
     from damage received. A character with high constitution gets more HP,
     and also recovers faster when resting.

Charisma
     Charisma represents a character's personality and physical appearance. A
     character with high charisma gets better prices in stores, whereas a
     character with very low charisma is cheated blind.


***** <PrimarySkills>
=== Primary Skills ===

A character has several primary skills for survival. Their initial values
depend on the character's race and class, and they change with level, stats
and equipment.

Melee
     Melee is the ability to hit and do damage with weapons or fists.
     Normally a character gets a single blow from any weapon, but if
     dexterity and strength are high enough, he or she may receive two or
     more blows per turn with a light weapon. Both strength and dexterity
     affect the chance to hit. This skill increases with the level of the
     character.

Bows and Throws
     Using ranged missile weapons (and throwing objects) is included in this
     skill. Different stats apply to different weapons, but this ability
     modifies the distance an object is thrown or fired, the amount of damage
     done, and the chance to hit. This skill increases with the level of the
     character.

Saving Throws
     The saving throw is the ability of a character to resist the effects of
     a spell cast on him or her by an enemy; it has no effect on spells you
     cast on yourself. This ability increases with the level of the
     character, but most high-level creatures are good at casting spells, so
     it tends to even out in the end. High wisdom helps increase this
     ability.

Stealth
     The ability to move silently about is very useful. Characters with good
     stealth can usually surprise their enemies and get the first blow. Also,
     monsters may fail to notice a stealthy character at all, letting the
     player avoid some fights. This skill is based on race and class. For
     some classes it improves as the class level rises; otherwise it never
     improves unless magically enhanced.

Disarming
     Disarming is the ability to (safely) remove traps, and it includes
     unlocking doors. When it succeeds, the character gains some experience.
     A trap must be found before it can be disarmed. This ability also
     increases with the character's level.

Perception (Searching Frequency)
     Perception is the ability to notice something without actively seeking
     it out. This skill is based entirely on race and class, and never
     improves unless magically enhanced.

Searching (Searching Ability)
     To search is to actively look for secret doors and traps (on the floor
     or on chests). This skill is based entirely on race and class, and never
     improves unless magically enhanced.

Magic Device Skill
     Using a magical device such as a wand or staff requires experience and
     knowledge. Spellcasters such as the Wizard are therefore much better at
     using these devices than, say, the Warrior. This skill is modified by
     intelligence, and increases with level.

Infravision
     Infravision is the ability to "see" heat sources. Since most of the
     dungeon is cool or cold, infravision will not let you see walls and
     objects. Instead, it lets you see warm-blooded creatures within a
     certain distance. This ability works the same with or without a light
     source. Most of TOband's creatures are cold-blooded and will not be
     detected unless lit by a light source. Many non-human races have this
     ability from birth. Humans can gain it only by magical means.


***** <SkillLevel>
=== Skill Levels ===

In TOband, a character has a skill level for each type of weapon, for each
of several other skills (martial arts, dual wielding, riding, throwing, pet
upkeep, weapon and armor identification, accessory identification, spell
casting), and for each magic realm. You can check them with the knowledge
command ('~'): press Space to go to the second page, and then 'b' (Display
Skills) to open the "Current Skill" screen. At the start of the game, all
weapon and other skills are at [Beginner].

There are no skill points to spend. Skills grow by themselves when your
class reaches a new highest level. How much each skill grows depends on the
class, and a skill that the class cannot learn does not grow at all. A skill
also stops growing when it reaches a limit that depends on the class level,
so the higher your class level, the further it can go.

Weapon skills and the other skills have six levels: [Beginner], [Novice],
[Average], [Skilled], [Expert] and [Master]. The skill of each magic realm
is shown as a number.

The skill level of a weapon affects the chance to hit, the damage and the
number of attacks with that kind of weapon. Martial arts and throwing work
in the same way.

The skill level of dual wielding affects the chance to hit when you hold two
weapons.

A higher riding skill level increases the speed of a mounted character, up
to a maximum equal to the speed of the monster being ridden (see
jmonster.txt#Pets [i]).

The skill level of spell casting lowers the failure rate and MP cost of
spells, and also the energy used to cast them. Because less energy is used,
a spell's effect per turn becomes greater.

As the pet upkeep skill level rises, you can keep higher-level pets, and
more of them.

The skill levels of weapon and armor identification and of accessory
identification affect your ability to identify items on the floor, in your
pack and in your equipment. At a low skill level, you get only a weak, quick
identification of weapons and armor, and you learn nothing about accessories;
as the skill level rises, automatic identification becomes more detailed,
and finally you can automatically *identify* items just by walking around.

The skill of each magic realm limits which spells you can cast: you can use
a spell only if the skill of its realm is at least the spell's level.
