=== TOband Beginner's Guide ===


=== Basic Training ===

Start the game and begin creating your character. Depending on the type of
computer you are using, you may have to choose "New" from the File menu to
begin character creation. Read the on-screen instructions carefully and follow
the steps to choose your sex, race, class, and Element. If you want basic
information about races, classes, and Elements, use the online help command
(the "?" key) and see the "Character Creation" and "Races, Classes, and
Elements" topics. You should also look through the other topics as soon as you
can. For anyone who is new to TOband in particular, the "Command Descriptions"
topic is especially important.

After you name your character, the game begins in a town called Golyat, a port
town. The numbered buildings are shops where you can buy equipment and sell
the things you find in the dungeon. The ">" just a short way to the left is a
staircase, and it marks the entrance to a dungeon (the one here is a dungeon
called "Seaside Cave"). Press the ">" key. You will go down the stairs and
enter the dungeon. The symbol for an up staircase is "<". If you press the "<"
key, you will go back up the stairs to the surface.

Next, try pressing the "<" key on the surface. If there are no monsters
around, you will leave the normal map and enter the wide-area map, where you
can look out over the whole of the vast surface world. Remember this, because
it is essential when you travel to distant towns or other dungeons. Pressing
the ">" key on the wide-area map takes you back to the normal map.

If you want to know what a character shown on the screen stands for, you can
find out with the "look" command (the "l" key or the "x" key). Press the
direction keys to choose what you want to look at. Note, however, that at
first the cursor does not move freely when you press the direction keys; it
can only select interesting things such as items, monsters, and shop
entrances. If you want a description of an ordinary "." (the floor symbol) or
"#" (the wall symbol), press "o" after pressing the "l" key or the "x" key.
Then you can move the cursor freely. Also, instead of moving the cursor, you
can press the space bar or the enter key to go one after another through the
interesting things in view.

Now let's look around the town. But stay away from the killer whales. They can
easily send you to your grave. They can be useful, though, for raising your
level by throwing things at them. Also, be careful about shop number 7. It is
the Black Market, where extremely powerful items are sold at ridiculously high
prices.

Don't forget to stop in at shops 1 and 2, the General Store and the Armory. At
the General Store, buy a Cloak, a Brass Lantern, and one or two Flasks of oil.
Then sell the Wooden Torch you started with. At the Armory, buy some light
armor. How about Hard Leather Armour, a Leather Hat, and Leather Gloves? If a
Small Leather Shield is for sale and you can afford it, buy that too.

By now you have probably spent all your money. But if you still have some
left, we strongly recommend visiting the Alchemy shop (shop 5) and buying a
Scroll of Phase Door. When you are surrounded by monsters, read it. It
teleports you, though only within a short range.

Of course, these items are sometimes not for sale. Each shop replaces its
stock from time to time. If what you need is not on sale at the moment, you
can wait for the shop's goods to change.

As a matter of fact, TOband is not very hard to learn to play. You will pick
up how to handle it quite quickly.

From here on, I would like to give you detailed information on how to survive
in the dungeon. Some of it you could work out by intuition, while some of it
you would probably not notice until after dying many times. If you want to
learn by getting hurt and earn your success that way, do not read any further.
But if you are tired of worrying about dying, read on.

=== Weapons and Multiple Attacks ===

Most warrior-type characters start out able to attack with a weapon two or
more times per turn. Whether you can attack more often than that later in the
game depends on your class, the weight of your weapon, and your Strength and
Dexterity scores. The number of blows is shown on the character sheet screen
(press "C").

Multiple attacks become very important in the later part of the dungeon. The
more blows you get, the more damage your weapon does (obviously).


=== The Importance of Magical Items ===

You may think you can get by without magical items. If so, your stay in the
dungeon may be interesting, but it will be short. It may even turn out to be a
one-way ticket.

--- Teleportation Items ---

The most important are the items that let you teleport. There are many kinds
of this type of item:

Phase Door
     A short-range teleport, a good way to escape when you want to stay near
     the enemy (it is also the basis of the "Shoot'n Scoot" tactic; details
     later).

Teleportation
     Teleports you quite far away within the floor. This is a handy way to
     escape instantly when you are surrounded by dangerous enemies or by a
     horde of them. It has the unfortunate effect of sometimes putting you
     next to an enraged monster, but that is still better than simply waiting
     to die. Also, some enemies have the ability to follow you when you
     teleport.

Teleport Other
     Teleports one or several monsters far away. It is safer than
     Teleportation, but a single use may not be able to send away every enemy.
     Also, there are enemies that resist being teleported.

Teleport Level
     Moves you one level up or down. When the current level is full of
     dangerous monsters and you want to teleport, this is a ticket to safety.
     Also, as a very important point, right after a Teleport Level your next
     turn comes immediately. If you are near death and use ordinary
     Teleportation, you risk being killed in an instant if an enemy happens to
     be at the place you land. With Teleport Level, that one instant is always
     safe.

*Destruction*
     This powerful spell blows away everything around you (artifacts
     included!) along with the dungeon itself, and makes rocks rain down from
     the ceiling. If you fall into serious danger, it is the safest way to
     escape. Unique monsters are not killed by *Destruction*; they are simply
     removed from the level and will appear before you again later.


--- Healing Items ---

Second in importance are the healing items. Here "healing" includes Cure Light
Wounds, Cure Serious Wounds, Cure Critical Wounds, Curing, Healing, *Curing*,
*Healing*, and the Potion of Life, which are listed in order of increasing
power.

Cure Light Wounds, Cure Serious Wounds, Cure Critical Wounds
     Cure Light Wounds restores 2d8 HP and cures cuts, blindness, and berserk
     rage. Cure Serious Wounds restores 4d8 HP and cures cuts, blindness,
     confusion, and berserk rage. Cure Critical Wounds restores 6d8 HP and
     cures cuts, blindness, confusion, stun, poison, and berserk rage.

Curing
     This potion focuses more on curing a variety of status ailments than on
     healing wounds. It cures cuts, blindness, confusion, stun, poison, and
     hallucination, and also restores 50 HP.

Healing
     Restores 300 HP and cures cuts, blindness, confusion, stun, poison, and
     berserk rage.

*Curing*
     Restores 700 HP and cures cuts, blindness, confusion, stun, poison,
     hallucination, and slowly progressing petrification.

*Healing*
     Restores 1200 HP and cures cuts, blindness, confusion, stun, poison, and
     berserk rage.

Life
     Basically cures everything bad: it restores 5000 HP, restores stats and
     experience drained by enemies, and cures all temporary bad conditions.
     This item is extremely rare, so save it for dangerous battles to come.

--- Identification Items ---

There are various ways to identify things: Scrolls of Identify, Staffs of
Perception, Rods of Perception, and the Identify spell and prayer. These let
you learn every magical property an object has, for example a weapon's to-hit
and to-damage bonuses and the modifiers it gives to your stats. They also tell
you whether an item is cursed. And you also learn what that wand, rod, staff,
potion, scroll, and so on actually is, which is very important. You would not
want to drink some troublesome potion found deep in the dungeon without
knowing what it is. Sometimes the description of an item is vague. Examples
are the Staff of Holiness and the Staff of Power. To find out what these do,
you may have to try them out a little.

There is a more advanced kind of identification, called *Identify*. It reveals
all the attributes of a single item, for example that it lets you see
invisible things, protects your stats from being drained, gives you rapid
regeneration, and so on. You should use it on artifacts and on high-level ego
items (weapons of Lodis, (Holy Avenger) and (Defender) weapons, armor of
Xenobia, boots of Levitation, and so on).


--- Light Sources ---

A note about light sources: without light you can hardly do anything. You
cannot see most monsters, you cannot cast spells or read scrolls, and, to take
the most obvious example, you cannot see the layout of the dungeon. There are
items that light up the inside of the dungeon, but only in part. Any item or
spell that serves as a light source lights up the room you are in (or a part
of a corridor). A Potion of Enlightenment or the Enlightenment spell lights up
the entire level.

--- Word of Recall ---

Finally, the most useful magical item may be the Scroll of Word of Recall. If
you read it when you are in town, you choose a dungeon and are then teleported
to the deepest level you have reached in that dungeon so far. If you read it
in a dungeon, you are teleported to town. (If you read it on a level shallower
than your deepest one, the game asks whether to reset the recall depth to the
current level.) Because it takes 15 to 35 turns after you read the scroll for
the effect to take hold, it is not a perfect means of escape.


=== About Resistances ===

TOband has many kinds of attacks, divided into physical attacks, magic
attacks, and breath attacks. Unless a character has the matching resistance,
these cause special damage that can easily kill the character. Each kind of
attack has a resistance that corresponds to it. Broadly, the resistances fall
into three basic groups: the "lower" resistances, the "higher" resistances,
and the magical resistances. The lower resistances are acid, lightning, fire,
and cold. The higher resistances are poison, light, darkness, shards,
confusion, sound, nether, stone, chaos, and disenchantment. The magical
resistances are resistance to blindness, resistance to fear, Free Action
(immunity to paralysis), and Hold Life (resistance to life draining).
Resistances come from items and from racial traits. For example, Fairies
resist light (you can see these in the character creation section of the
online help). Some types of items give all four of the basic (lower)
resistances. Such items include weapons with the (Defender) mark, the Robe of
Permanence, armor of Resistance and of Xenobia, and Shields of Resistance.
Many artifacts also have some or all of the basic resistances, plus one or two
of the "higher" resistances.

Resistance generally reduces the total damage to between 1/5 and 2/3. Also,
only the four basic resistances and poison can have a double resistance
effect, by combining resistance from equipment with temporary resistance. In
that case the damage becomes 1/9.

Resistances are your lifeline for surviving in the dungeon. For example, the
Great Hell Wyrm, a quite powerful fire dragon, breathes fire, and without
resistance the damage can be as much as 1600 HP. If you have resistance to
fire, however, the damage becomes 1/3, which is 533 HP. With double
resistance, the damage is reduced to a mere 178 HP.


=== Important Dungeon Levels ===

The dungeon has certain levels that serve as guidelines: beyond them you will
need particular resistances or abilities. The levels shown here are only
guidelines and should not be taken literally. But if you decide to go past one
of those levels without the listed properties, you should be very careful, and
you should not complain when you see the dreadful message "It breathes -more-
You die."

Dungeon level 15: Resist Blindness, or if you have none, light and darkness
Dungeon level 20: See Invisible, Resist Fire, Free Action (paralysis)
Dungeon level 25: Resist Acid, Lightning, and Cold
Dungeon level 30: Confusion, blindness (mage-type classes), (stone)
Dungeon level 35: Poison, blindness (warrior-type classes)
Dungeon level 40: Nether, Chaos, (darkness)
Dungeon level 50: Hold Life (resistance to life draining), Telepathy
Dungeon level 60: Permanent speed +10 or more
Dungeon level 70: Permanent speed +15 or more, shards, darkness
Dungeon level 90: Permanent speed +25 or more
Dungeon level 99: Every resistance and ability you can get

Note: if you have good infravision, you can use it for a while as a substitute
for See Invisible. But ghosts will soon begin to appear, and you cannot see
them with infravision.

Going without resistance to paralysis is like walking to your own death.
Carrion crawlers (and monsters of that kind) have a paralyzing attack that
leaves you unable to move until you die. Also, from around this point monsters
that cast paralysis spells start to appear. There is nothing worse than
helplessly watching an insignificant monster kill your character right before
your eyes.

Resistance to confusion and blindness is important. If you have them, you can
rely on Scrolls of Teleportation in any situation. If you do not have the
resistances, you will need a Staff of Teleportation when you are confused or
blind, but it has some chance of failure. Scrolls are safe.

Resistance to poison becomes essential because of two kinds of monsters that
begin to appear from about the 40th level down, the Ancient Multi-hued Dragon
and the Drolem. Both breathe poison and deal heavy damage (700 and 800
respectively). It is also a resistance you want to have on at all times, as
early as possible from level 24 on, to guard against the deadly side effects
of radioactive waste.

You also need resistance to stone to prevent its side effects. Without it, you
are turned to stone on the spot and your adventure is over. If you have the
"earth" resistances, acid and shards, you escape instant death (and a
successful saving throw can also turn the attack aside), but petrification
then proceeds gradually, and if you have no means of curing it and cannot
reach the healer in town, you become a complete statue.

Resistance to chaos is a *lifeline*. Chaos not only deals large damage but
also brings nasty side effects such as hallucination and mutation.

Resistance to nether is also very important. A great many enemies make this
type of attack, and it does heavy damage.

Resistance to life draining (Hold Life) is a very good resistance, but it is
not essential. If you cannot get it, carry plenty of Potions of Restore Life
Levels.


=== About Secret Doors ===

In the dungeon, many doors are hidden so that they cannot be found. To find
them, you must use the Search (S) command to switch into search mode. (Each
press of S cycles through search mode, element scope mode, and normal mode.
The "s" key searches once.) But sometimes you do not even know where to look.
Usually, in cases like the following, there is often a hidden door at the
places marked with "*".

#*###  ###'###  ####*#    ###*###
*@...  ..'@'..  ..@'.*    ..'@...
#*###  ###*###  ####'#    #######

In general, if a corridor has a door, there is another door nearby. If you
find a corner with two doors, try looking for a hidden door. Crossroads often
have hidden doors. Finally, hidden doors are never in places where you would
have to move diagonally to open them. Dead ends (the first example) usually
have a door, but not always. If you search for a while and find nothing, give
up and look elsewhere. Dead ends often occur at the edge of the map, because
the corridor cannot be extended any further.


=== About Preserve Mode ===

You choose ON or OFF for this when you create the character, with the
"Preserve artifacts" item (preserve_mode) in the birth option menu. It cannot
be changed afterwards.

With Preserve Mode OFF, a level containing an artifact is generated, and if
you leave for another level without picking it up, that item is lost forever.
With Preserve Mode ON, you can find that artifact again, but in exchange you
can no longer sense the "special" feeling of a level (see the next section).
The same goes for an artifact you unknowingly blow away with the *Destruction*
described above.


=== Level Feelings ===

After you have spent a little time on a level, you sense a feeling that
vaguely tells you how good that level is. How long this takes depends on the
depth of the level and on your Searching skill. The more "dangerous" the
feeling you get, the more powerful the items and the higher-level the enemies
on that level. The feeling is worked out from the monsters on the level
(monsters whose level is deeper than the current level, unique monsters that
are nearly out of depth, and unusually crowded groups) and from the items
lying on the floor that you have not identified (ego items, artifacts, some
special items such as dragon armor and Rings of Speed, and items whose base
level is deeper than the current level). The feeling is checked again as time
passes, and the game announces it whenever it changes.

For example, the base level of a Long Sword is 10. If one is seen on a level
shallower than that, it is regarded as "unusual". An item is regarded as
"unusual" by the condition above alone; it does not need to have any special
power. If an ordinary, non-magical sword lies on a shallow level, it gives
rise to a dangerous feeling, because it is "unusual". A pit (a large room
filled with one kind of monster) or a vault (a dense, dangerous room with many
monsters and items not normally seen on that level) usually raises the feeling
too, because of the monsters and items in it. There is also a "special
feeling". It occurs only when Preserve Mode is off, and it tells the player
that there is an artifact on the level.

Pressing "^F" (the Control key + "F") redisplays the feeling you already know.
If not enough time has passed since you entered the current level, the message
"Looks like any other level." is displayed, and you get no information at all
about the level. In town, "^F" instead tells you the attitude of the
townspeople toward you (welcoming, indifferent, or hostile), which depends on
the Chaos Frame of that town's ethnic group.


=== The Meaning of the Numbers on Equipment ===

Your equipment always has numbers displayed on it that show how effective it
is.

For armor:               Armor of Something (+x,+y) [a,+b] (+c)
For weapons:             Sword of Something (XdY) (+x,+y) [+b] (+c)
Unidentified armor:      Armor of Something [a]
Unidentified weapons:    Sword of Something (XdY)

Meaning of (+x,+y):

 These are the magical bonuses added to the to-hit modifier and the to-damage
 modifier, respectively. They are added together with your other bonuses when
 you attack. Some armor also has bonuses to the to-hit and to-damage
 modifiers. Most of these are artifacts, but exceptions include things like
 Gloves of Slaying. Also, heavy armor has a small minus value in front of the
 AC display. This is a minus modifier to your to-hit, caused by the armor
 being heavy and restricting your movement. The modifier is small, so you can
 ignore it unless the character is of very low level.

Rings of Damage and Rings of Accuracy have a (+y) or a (+x) modifier, one or
the other. When worn, they add to damage and to the chance to hit,
respectively. But be careful! The effect of a ring applies only to the weapon
held in the arm on the same side as the ring. Normally you hold your weapon in
your right hand, so wearing a ring on a finger of your left hand has no effect
at all.

[a,+b]. This shows the item's base AC and the bonus to it. The larger the
number, the better. (Note: some armor shows a small negative value in "()" in
front of these numbers. This means the armor is so heavy that you have trouble
moving, giving you a negative modifier to your to-hit. Normally, though, it is
small enough to ignore.) Some weapons also have a bonus to AC.

A Ring of Protection has a [+b] modifier and raises your AC. In practice,
however, this ring is not useful. It is far better to use your precious ring
slots for something else.

(+c). This is the bonus the item gives to your other stats: Strength,
Intelligence, Wisdom, Constitution, Dexterity, Charisma, speed, stealth,
searching ability, infravision, number of blows, digging ability, and so on.
Not every item has it, and it gives you a reason to choose one item over
another. (This value does not affect every stat in the list above. Most items
give this bonus to only one or two stats. Some give it to more (weapons of
Lodis give bonuses to Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution).)

For example, a Holy Avenger looks like this:

Mace (Holy Avenger) (2d4) (+7,+5) [+4] (+1)

This means that when you attack you roll two 4-sided dice, that you get a +7
modifier to your chance to hit and a +5 modifier to damage, that your AC goes
up by 4, and that one stat (Wisdom, in this case) goes up by 1.

XdY. This is the weapon's base damage. If an attack hits, you roll a Y-sided
die X times, and the total of the rolls is the damage before modification by
brands, critical hits, and other bonuses. For example, a Long Sword rolls a
5-sided die twice (2d5) when it hits.

For arrows, bolts, and bullets, in addition to the usual weapon information,
the average damage the item can deal per shot and per turn is displayed. These
numbers are displayed only when you are wielding a suitable launcher. (The
average damage of melee attacks is displayed by the "C" command.) The display
format is as follows:

Arrow (1d4) (+0,+0) (16/32)

The first number is the average damage per shot, and the next number is the
average damage per turn. The numbers here are only an example; they are for
the case where you fire 2 shots per turn.


=== About Slays and Brands ===

Many weapons in the dungeon are terribly powerful against particular kinds of
enemies. These properties are collectively called slays. Such weapons multiply
the damage dice you roll. They do not affect the damage modifier, however. The
slay properties are listed below:

     Slay Evil: damage dice x2 against evil monsters
     *Slay* Evil: damage dice x3.5 against evil monsters
     Slay Good: damage dice x2 against good monsters
     *Slay* Good: damage dice x3.5 against good monsters
     Slay Animal: damage dice x2.5 against animals
     *Slay* Animal: damage dice x4 against animals
     Slay Human: damage dice x2.5; *Slay* Human: x4
     Slay Living: damage dice x2; *Slay* Living: x3.5
     Slay Undead, Demon, Orc, Troll, Giant, or Dragon: damage dice x3
     *Slay* Undead, Demon, Orc, Troll, Giant, or Dragon: damage dice x5

Weapons with an elemental brand (Melting, Burning, Freezing, Shocking, Venom)
attack with damage dice multiplied by 2.5 against monsters that do not resist
that element (5 against monsters that are especially vulnerable to it). In
addition, (Vampiric) weapons drain HP from living enemies and heal the player
by the same amount, and (Chaotic) weapons sometimes cause random effects
(draining life, confusion, an earthquake, teleporting the enemy away, or
polymorphing it).


=== Monster Memory ===

In many other games, you have to take notes on monsters' weaknesses and
strengths, the spells they use, and so on. TOband takes care of this by
recording a brief memory of each monster for you. When your character finds
out something new about a monster, for example that it uses a breath attack,
it is added to your information about that monster. You can look at this
information by typing "/" and then the character that represents the monster.
Press the space bar to skip past the records of other monsters until you reach
the one you want to see. By setting window flags, you can have this memory
always displayed in a subwindow. In that case, the memory window is updated
automatically as you fight monsters.

Unfortunately, when your character dies, you can use that character's savefile
to create the next character. Instead of "New", choose the dead character's
file with "Open", and you will go to the character creation screen. A
character created this way inherits the knowledge about monsters of all its
ancestors, and for the monster that killed an ancestor, a special sentence is
displayed in the memory.


=== Monster Colors ===

You can often read some information from a monster's color. This means the
color that is part of its name, as in "White Dragon", not the color displayed
on the screen (you should not look at a white "p" and assume it is a
cold-based monster). In general, white monsters are cold-based, red ones are
fire-based, blue ones are lightning-based, black ones are acid-based, and
yellow or green ones are poison-based. Multi-hued monsters combine all of
these.


=== The Options Screen ===

The options screen is displayed by typing "=". It contains various settings
that make TOband more comfortable to play. Most of them can stay at their
defaults, but a few are worth setting.

After typing "=", press "H" to enter the hitpoint warning setting, and change
it to about 50%. You will see the effect as soon as you are about to die.

After typing "=", press "D" to enter the base delay factor screen. This
determines how long the animation takes when special effects actually play
out, that is, the explosion of a ball spell, or a bolt or missile in flight.
Normally it is a good idea to set this to 2 or 3.

After typing "=", press "W" to enter the window flags screen. This sets what
each subwindow displays. With it you can keep the message history, the monster
memory, your inventory and equipment, and other things always displayed in
several windows.


=== Death ===

In TOband, death is permanent. In this game, saving is merely a means of
suspending the game. When you die, the savefile is marked, and the next time
you try to start the game with that savefile, you begin by creating a new
character. The dead character's descendant inherits the monster memory and the
options you have set, passing them on to the next generation.

Some people make backups of their savefiles. If their character dies, they
simply load the old character and resume from where they stopped before. They
are set back to the point of their last backup, but alive. This, however, is
considered "cheating". If you do this only for your own private enjoyment,
there is no reason to stop you. The only exception in which you can use a
backed-up savefile is when your computer crashes, or the character data is
corrupted by a serious bug or the like.

The Cheating menu of the options screen has an option for cheating death. But
like the other cheating options, it invalidates your high score, and it
becomes impossible to be a true winner of the game.


=== About Speed in TOband ===

TOband has a system that confuses players (especially veterans of
Angband-family games). It concerns speed. In most other Angband-family games,
the speed at which you and your enemies act is fixed by the type and condition
of the character. A character with +10 speed (or one who is "Fast") moves at
twice the speed of a normal monster, and can always act twice in the time it
takes a +0 player (or a "normal speed" monster) to act once. In TOband,
however, speed is not constant. The speed number simply indicates a
statistical average. Therefore a monster moving at normal speed may suddenly
act two or three times in a row, or may stand still for one or two turns.

Also, the speed number has the complexity common to Angband-family games. When
it is displayed as speed +nn, the actual increase in speed is not proportional
to +nn; beyond +27, the actual gain in speed per point of the displayed number
gradually becomes smaller.

You can understand this system more deeply by learning the details of TOband's
time system. In TOband, players and monsters gain some points of "energy" as
time passes, according to their speed. As mentioned above, unlike other
Angband-family games, the amount of energy needed to act in TOband is random.
Only the average is equal: on average, you can act once you have gained 100
energy points. Most actions cost 100 energy. There are a few exceptions; for
example, shooting with a bow of Extra Shots costs only half, 50 energy, so
your turn comes around twice on average before a monster of the same speed
moves.

And the speed bonus is added to the energy a player gains each turn. With
speed +10 you gain twice the normal energy each turn, so you move twice as
fast. With +20 it is three times. With +30, however, it is only 3.8 times, and
no matter how high you raise your speed, the maximum is 4.9 times.


=== Techniques for Killing Monsters ===

Shoot'n Scoot.
     You need a large room, Phase Door, and some means of ranged attack (a
     bow, a crossbow, or a gun). Stand in one corner of the room, with the
     enemy in another corner. Keep making ranged attacks until the enemy comes
     next to you, then jump to a distant place with Phase Door. Repeat this.
     By the time you run out of ammunition, the enemy will be dead or nearly
     dead.

Wail'n Bail.
     You need a teleportation item. Fight a monster until you are about to
     die, then teleport. After that, find it and resume the fight. This is a
     dangerous method, because teleporting can drop you next to other
     monsters. It is also not very useful for killing unique monsters. They
     recover damage very quickly, so by the time you find the enemy again, it
     will have recovered the damage you inflicted.

The Anti-Summoning Corridor.
     This takes a little time to prepare. It is a technique you can use
     anywhere. First, dig a winding corridor into the rock, and take your
     position at one end of it. Even when the enemy comes there, it cannot
     summon monsters next to you. This is a very important technique when you
     fight powerful monsters that can summon hordes of monsters.
