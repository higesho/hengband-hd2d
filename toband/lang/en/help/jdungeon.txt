=== The Dungeon ===


Although the vast overworld of TOband contains several towns and a large
wilderness, the bulk of your adventuring will take place in the dungeon.
Symbols appearing on your screen represent the dungeon's walls, floor,
objects, features, and the creatures lurking about. In order to direct your
character, you enter single-character commands (see jcommand.txt [b]).


***** <MapSymbols>
=== Symbols On Your Map ===

Symbols on your map can be broken down into three categories: features of
the dungeon such as walls, floor, doors and traps; objects which can be
picked up such as treasure, weapons and magical devices; and creatures which
may or may not move about the dungeon, but are mostly harmful to your
character's well-being.

Some symbols are used to represent more than one type of entity, and some
symbols are used to represent entities in more than one category. The '@'
symbol (by default) is used to represent the character.

It will not be necessary to remember all of the symbols and their meanings.
The '/' command will identify any symbol on your map at any time.

Note that you can use a "user pref file" to change any of these symbols to
something you are more comfortable with.

***** <WithinDungeon>
=== Within The Dungeon ===

Once your character is adequately supplied with food, light, armor and
weapons, he or she is ready to enter the dungeon. Move on top of the '>'
symbol and use the "Down" command ('>').

Your character will enter a maze of interconnecting staircases and finally
arrive somewhere on the first level of the dungeon. Each level of the
dungeon is fifty feet high (thus dungeon level "1" is sometimes called "50
feet"), and is divided into large rectangular regions, each larger than the
screen.

In the dungeon, there are many items to find, but your character must
survive many horrible monsters that are after you in order to find the items
lying about and take them safely back to town to sell.

There are two sources of light inside the dungeon. One is the permanent
light that has been magically placed within rooms. The other is light
sources carried by the player or monsters. If neither is present, the
character will be unable to see. This will affect searching, picking locks,
disarming traps, reading scrolls, casting spells and prayers, browsing books
and more. So be very careful not to run out of light!

A character must wield a torch or lamp in order to supply his or her own
light. A torch or lamp burns fuel as it is used, and once it is out of fuel
it stops supplying light. You will be warned as the light approaches this
point. You may use the 'F' command to refuel your lantern (with flasks of
oil) or your torch (with other torches), so it is a good idea to carry a
reasonable number of extra torches or flasks of oil. There are rumors of
items of exceptional power which glow with their own never-ending light.


***** <Objects>
=== Objects In The Dungeon ===

The dungeon is full of items just waiting to be picked up and used. How did
they get there? Well, most of the useful items belonged to the adventurers
who entered the dungeon before you. After they were killed, the monsters
busily scattered the items they left behind throughout the dungeon. Most
cursed items were placed there by spiteful evil sorcerers, who delight in
watching the adventurer who picks one up come to a bad end.

One item in particular is worth mentioning here. The scroll of "Word of
Recall" can be found within the dungeon, or bought at the temple in town. It
works in two ways, depending on your current location. If read within the
dungeon, it will teleport you back to town. If read in town, it will
(usually) teleport you down to the deepest level of the dungeon that your
character has previously been on. This makes the scroll very useful for
getting back to the deeper levels of the dungeon. The level you return to is
usually the deepest one you have been to, but you can change it by going
back to a shallower level and reading a scroll of "Word of Recall".

A scroll of "Word of Recall" takes some time to take effect after you read
it, so don't expect to use it as an instant escape from a crisis. If you
read another such scroll before the first has taken effect, the two cancel
each other out and you will not be teleported.

A more complete description of TOband items is found elsewhere in the
documentation (see jobjects.txt [c]).


***** <Mining>
=== Mining ===

Much of the treasure within the dungeon can be had only by digging it out of
the walls. Rich veins exist on every level, but of course they must be
found and mined. Quartz veins are the richest, holding most of the metals
and gems, but magma veins also hold some.

Mining is rather difficult without a pick or shovel. Some picks and shovels
have an additional magical ability, shown in the form "(+#)". The higher the
number, the better the magical digging ability of the tool. A pick or shovel
may also have plusses to hit and to damage, which means it can be used as a
weapon too.

When a vein of quartz or magma is located, the character may wield a pick or
shovel and begin digging out a section. When that section is removed, he or
she can go on and keep digging further. Since granite rock is very hard, the
efficient way to mine is to follow the vein and dig around the granite. At a
certain point in the game, mining for treasure becomes more trouble than
simply killing monsters and finding items to sell. Early on, however,
mineral veins are a storehouse of easy treasure.

If the character has a scroll, staff or spell of treasure detection, all the
treasure in the veins on the screen is shown at once. This makes mining much
easier and more fruitful. Note, however, that some treasure is not revealed
even by detection.

A character with high strength, or one wielding a heavy weapon, can dig
without a pick or shovel. But even the strongest character will benefit from
a pick or shovel when digging through granite.

Various magical spells and items can sometimes leave a character trapped. So
it is a good idea to always carry some kind of digging tool to escape with,
even if you are not planning to dig for treasure.

There are rumors of rooms deep in the dungeon holding incredible treasure,
completely surrounded by permanent rock and granite walls, which can be
entered only with a digging tool or magic. The same rumors say that these
rooms are guarded by incredibly powerful monsters, so beware!


***** <StairsDoorsEtc>
=== Staircases, Secret Doors, Passages, and Rooms ===

Staircases are how you go deeper into the dungeon or climb out of it. The
symbols for the up and down staircases and shafts are the same as the
commands to use them. A "<" represents an up staircase and a ">" represents
a down staircase. You must move your character onto the staircase before you
can use it.

In TOband, a staircase takes you up or down one level, and a shaft takes you
up or down two levels.

Each level has 3 or 4 staircases leading onward (down in a normal dungeon,
up in a dungeon that is climbed upward) and 1 or 2 leading back. You may have
trouble finding a well-hidden secret door, or you may have to dig through
obstructions to reach the stairs, but if you look hard enough you can find
them in almost every case. Like permanent rock, the stairs and the entrances
to shops cannot be destroyed by any means.

Many secret doors are used within the dungeon to confuse and demoralize
adventurers foolish enough to enter. But with some luck, or careful
searching, you can find these secret doors. Behind a secret door there may be
a room or corridor, or sometimes a whole main section of the level is hidden.
Sometimes, though, there is only an empty room or a dead-end corridor.
Secret doors always look like granite walls, just as traps always look like
normal floor.

Monsters in the dungeon will generally know about and use these secret
doors, and you may see them left open behind a monster that has passed
through.

Doors can be broken down by bashing them. Once a door is bashed open, it is
forever useless and cannot be closed.


***** <Feelings>
=== Level Feelings ===

Once you have been on a particular dungeon level for a while, you will
receive a "feeling" prompt representing what your intuition tells you about
the quality of the items and the difficulty of the monsters found on that
level. You may check this prompt again at any time after receiving it by
pressing 'CTRL-F'.

The actual feeling is determined by a number of factors. Things which raise
the feeling level include the presence of vaults and special rooms, items
and monsters that are out of depth for the level, and items of a certain
quality (named items, artifacts, and "great" items (see jobjects.txt)). The
feeling is the one you get when you enter the level, and it does not change
because of monsters or items generated afterwards.

In TOband, the nastier the feeling prompt, the better the level. From
worst to best, the prompts are as follows:

     "What a boring place..."
     "This level looks reasonably safe."
     "You don't like the look of this place."
     "You feel your luck is turning..."
     "You feel nervous."
     "You have a bad feeling..."
     "You have a very bad feeling..."
     "This level looks very dangerous."
     "You nearly faint as horrible visions of death fill your mind!"

If for some reason you have a "white aura", you are very optimistic, and the
prompts are quite different. In that case, the more "good" the feeling, the
better the level:

     "What a boring place..."
     "This level can't be all bad..."
     "You like the look of this place..."
     "You feel your luck is turning..."
     "You feel strangely lucky..."
     "You have a good feeling..."
     "You have a very good feeling..."
     "You have an excellent feeling..."
     "You have a superb feeling about this level."

TOband also has two other special feelings. If you have not yet been on a
level long enough to get a feeling and press 'CTRL-F', you will be given the
prompt:

     "Looks like any other level."

If you are playing with artifact preservation turned off, you may also
occasionally receive the following prompt:

     "You feel there is something special about this level."

A special feeling means one of two things: either there is a special room or
vault on the level, or there is an artifact on the level. As you go deeper
into the dungeon, special feelings caused by special rooms and vaults become
increasingly rare, and are more commonly given when an artifact has been
generated. Note that a level can have both a vault and an artifact, or more
than one artifact, so you should never leave a special level without fully
exploring it unless your character's survival is in question.


***** <RandomQuests>
=== Random Quests ===

When your character is created, 10 random quests are set up automatically
(you are not asked how many you want). Random quests are always of the type
"Kill a particular powerful unique monster". There is a limit to how powerful
the monster can be, but these quests are still very dangerous.

Random quests appear in the Palace of the Dead, on levels 6, 12, 24, 38, 44,
50, 56, 62, 76 and 88. On arriving at a random quest level, you will be told
which monster is the quest monster.

On random quest levels, no down staircase appears until the quest monster
is killed. When you kill the quest monster, the down staircase will appear
and the monster will drop an item of 'excellent' quality or above. This is
true even of monsters that do not normally drop items.

If the quest monster is too strong for you, simply run away by taking the
up staircase or by using Word of Recall. In that case, the random quest is
merely treated as failed, and you can carry on with your adventure.
